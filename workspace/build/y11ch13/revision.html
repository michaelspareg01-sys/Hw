Probability (Chapter 12)
  • If \(E\) is a subset of a uniform sample space \(S\) (all outcomes equally likely), then \(P(E)=\dfrac{|E|}{|S|}\), where \(|E|\) is the number of members of \(E\). An impossible event has probability \(0\), a certain event has probability \(1\), and the probabilities of all the outcomes of an experiment add to \(1\).
  • The complement \(\overline{E}\) is the event ‘\(E\) does not occur’, and \(P(\overline{E})=1-P(E)\). Always consider using complementary events, particularly when the word ‘not’ occurs.
  • Addition rule: \(P(A\text{ or }B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\text{ and }B)\). If \(A\) and \(B\) are mutually exclusive (they cannot both occur), then \(P(A\text{ or }B)=P(A)+P(B)\).
  • Product rule: if \(A_1,A_2,\dots,A_n\) are events in successive independent stages, then \(P(A_1A_2\cdots A_n)=P(A_1)\times P(A_2)\times\cdots\times P(A_n)\). Sampling with replacement gives independent stages. Without replacement the second probability changes, because it is found from the objects that are left. On a tree diagram, multiply along a path and add the paths you want.
  • Relative frequency: if a result occurs \(f\) times in \(n\) runs of an experiment, its relative frequency is \(f/n\). It estimates the probability.
Mean, standard deviation and sigma notation (Advanced statistics)
  • The mean of \(n\) numbers is found by adding them up and dividing by \(n\): the mean of \(0,1,2,3,4\) is \((0+1+2+3+4)/5=2\). In a frequency table the value \(x\) occurs \(f\) times and \(n=\sum f\). The relative frequency is \(f_r=f/n\), and the \(f_r\) add to \(1\). The mean is the mean of the values weighted by their relative frequencies: \(\bar x=\sum xf\big/\sum f=\sum x\,f_r\).
  • The standard deviation \(\sigma\) measures spread. Each deviation \(x-\bar x\) is squared, and the mean of these is the variance. It is easier to use the mean of the squares minus the square of the mean: \[\sigma^2=\sum(x-\bar x)^2f_r=\sum x^2f_r-\bar x^{\,2}\qquad\sigma=\sqrt{\sigma^2}\] On a calculator use the key \(\sigma_n\).
  • Sigma notation: \(\sum\) is the Greek capital sigma, standing for ‘take the sum’, so \(\sum_{k=1}^{4}k^2=1+4+9+16=30\). Sums split and constants come out: \(\sum(a_k+b_k)=\sum a_k+\sum b_k\) and \(\sum c\,a_k=c\sum a_k\).
