a The mean of \(Z\) is \(\mu+a\), by the previous question. Hence \[\begin{aligned}\text{Var}(Z)&=\text{E}\big((Z-(\mu+a))^2\big)\\ &=\text{E}\big((Z-a-\mu)^2\big)\\ &=\text{E}\big((X-\mu)^2\big)\\ &=\text{Var}(X)\end{aligned}\] Hence the standard deviation of the new distribution remains \(\sigma\). This is to be expected, because the distribution is no more spread out than previously.
b The mean of \(Z\) is \(k\mu\), by the previous question. Hence \[\begin{aligned}\text{Var}(Z)&=\text{E}\big((Z-k\mu)^2\big)\\ &=\text{E}\big((kX-k\mu)^2\big)\\ &=k^2\times\text{E}\big((X-\mu)^2\big)\\ &=k^2\,\text{Var}(X)\end{aligned}\] Hence the standard deviation of the new distribution is \(\sqrt{k^2\sigma^2}=k\sigma\).

PP 3Hurlstone Agricultural High School, 2023, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q8

The Bernoulli variable takes the value 1 with probability \(p\) and the value 0 with probability \(1-p\). So

\[\text{E}(X)=0\times(1-p)+1\times p=p,\qquad\text{E}(X^2)=0^2\times(1-p)+1^2\times p=p,\] \[\text{Var}(X)=\text{E}(X^2)-\mu^2=p-p^2=p(1-p).\]

The condition \(\text{E}(X)=5\,\text{Var}(X)\) becomes \(p=5p(1-p)\). Since \(p\ne0\), divide both sides by \(p\):

\[1=5(1-p),\qquad1-p=\tfrac15,\qquad p=\tfrac45.\]

(Check: \(\text{E}(X)=\tfrac45\) and \(\text{Var}(X)=\tfrac45\times\tfrac15=\tfrac{4}{25}\), and \(5\times\tfrac{4}{25}=\tfrac45\).)

Answer (B). Option (C), \(\tfrac15\), is the value of \(1-p\), not \(p\). For options (A) and (D), \(\tfrac12\) gives \(\text{E}(X)=\tfrac12\) but \(5\,\text{Var}(X)=\tfrac54\), and \(\tfrac35\) gives \(\text{E}(X)=\tfrac35\) but \(5\,\text{Var}(X)=\tfrac65\).
