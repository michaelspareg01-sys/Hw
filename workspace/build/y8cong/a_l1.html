C2
Tutor demo. Turn \(WXYZ\) a quarter turn anticlockwise and it sits on \(PQRS\), with \(Y\) on \(P\), \(Z\) on \(Q\), \(W\) on \(R\) and \(X\) on \(S\).
a vertex \(X\): \(S\) has the largest angle of \(PQRS\) and is at one end of the shortest side \(SP\); \(X\) has the largest angle of \(WXYZ\) and is at one end of the shortest side \(XY\).
b side \(YZ\): \(PQ\) and \(YZ\) are the longest sides. \(P\) corresponds to \(Y\) and \(Q\) corresponds to \(Z\).
c \(\angle Z\): \(Q\) is the end of the longest side that is not on the shortest side, and so is \(Z\).
d The right angles are at \(L\) and \(T\), the dots at \(M\) and \(R\), the open circles at \(N\) and \(S\). So \(\triangle LMN\equiv\triangle TRS\).
Your turn. Reflect \(EFGH\) in a vertical line and it sits on \(ABCD\), with \(G\) on \(A\), \(F\) on \(B\), \(E\) on \(C\) and \(H\) on \(D\).
a vertex \(F\): \(\angle B\) and \(\angle F\) are the largest angles.
b side \(EH\): \(CD\) and \(EH\) are the longest sides.
c \(\angle G\): \(A\) is at one end of the shortest side \(AB\), next to the largest angle \(\angle B\); \(G\) is at one end of the shortest side \(GF\), next to the largest angle \(\angle F\).
d The right angles are at \(P\) and \(Y\), the dots at \(Q\) and \(Z\), the open circles at \(R\) and \(X\). So \(\triangle PQR\equiv\triangle YZX\).

PP 1Sydney Grammar School, 2023, Annual Examination, Form III Mathematics, Q1(e)

In the statement \(\triangle ABC\equiv\triangle XYZ\) the vertices are named in matching order: \(A\) matches \(X\), \(B\) matches \(Y\) and \(C\) matches \(Z\).

So side \(YZ\) matches side \(BC\), and \(YZ=BC=7\) (matching sides of congruent triangles).

PP 2Sydney Grammar School, 2020, Annual Examination, Form III Mathematics, Q2(h)(ii)

In \(\triangle OAB\equiv\triangle OCD\), \(O\) matches \(O\), \(A\) matches \(C\) and \(B\) matches \(D\). So \(\angle OCD\) matches \(\angle OAB\), which is \(\angle BAO\).

\(\angle OCD=\angle BAO=65^\circ\) (matching angles of congruent triangles)
