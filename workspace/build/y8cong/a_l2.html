Tutor demo.
a \(PQ=MN\) (one mark), \(PR=ML\) (two marks), and the angles at \(P\) and \(M\) are equal (arcs); each is the angle between the two marked sides. So \(P\) matches \(M\), \(Q\) matches \(N\) and \(R\) matches \(L\): \(\triangle PQR\equiv\triangle MNL\) (SAS).
b The 8 cm side lies between the \(35^\circ\) and \(105^\circ\) angles, and \(x\) cm lies between the \(35^\circ\) angle and \(a^\circ\). So the triangles match with \(a^\circ\) matching \(105^\circ\) and \(x\) matching 8:
\(x=8\), \(a=105\) (matching parts of congruent triangles), and \(b=180-35-105=40\) (angle sum of a triangle).

b \(x=9\), \(a=102\) (matching parts of congruent triangles), and \(b=180-38-102=40\) (angle sum of a triangle).

Year 8 Exercise 8E Q11
a \(AB=AC\) (given equal sides)
\(BM=CM\) (given equal sides)
\(AM=AM\) (given and common equal side)
\(\therefore\triangle ABM\equiv\triangle ACM\) (SSS)
\(\therefore\angle AMB=\angle AMC\) (matching angles of congruent triangles)
As \(\angle AMB+\angle AMC=180^\circ\) then \(\angle AMB=\angle AMC=90^\circ\).
b \(\angle AEB=\angle CDB\) (alternate angles)
\(\angle EAB=\angle DCB\) (alternate angles)
\(EB=BD\) (given equal sides)
\(\therefore\triangle AEB\equiv\triangle CDB\) (AAS)
\(\therefore AB=BC\) (matching sides of congruent triangles) and \(AC=AB+BC=2AB\)
c \(AD=DC\) (given equal sides)
\(AB=CB\) (given equal sides)
\(BD\) is a common side
\(\therefore\triangle ABD\equiv\triangle CBD\) (SSS)
\(\therefore\angle DAB=\angle DCB\) (matching angles of congruent triangles)
d \(AD=AB\) (sides of a rhombus)
\(CD=CB\) (sides of a rhombus)
\(AC\) is a common side
\(\therefore\triangle ACD\equiv\triangle ACB\) (SSS)
so \(\angle DCA=\angle BCA\) (matching angles of congruent triangles), that is, \(\angle DCE=\angle BCE\).
\(\angle DCE=\angle BCE\) (shown above)
\(\angle CDE=\angle CBE\) (isosceles triangle, \(CD=CB\))
\(CE\) is a common side
\(\therefore\triangle DCE\equiv\triangle BCE\) (AAS)
So \(\angle DEC=\angle BEC\) (matching angles of congruent triangles).
Since \(\angle DEC\) and \(\angle BEC\) are supplementary (sum to \(180^\circ\)), \(\angle DEC=\angle BEC=90^\circ\).
So the diagonals intersect at right angles.

PP 1Sydney Grammar School, 2020, Annual Examination, Form III Mathematics, Q2(h)(i)

In \(\triangle OAB\) and \(\triangle OCD\): \(OA=OC\) (given), \(OB=OD\) (given), and \(\angle AOB=\angle COD\) (vertically opposite angles). The angle at \(O\) is between the two pairs of equal sides, so the test is SAS.

PP 2Merewether High School, 2023, Year 10 Assessment Task 4, Mathematics, Section III Part A Q2

B (AAS). Both triangles have angles \(38^\circ\) and \(74^\circ\), and the side of length 18 is next to the \(74^\circ\) angle and opposite the \(38^\circ\) angle in both, so it is the matching side.

PP 3Girraween High School, 2020, Year 10 Term 4 (Yearly Examination), Q14

B. Triangles \(II\) and \(III\) are right-angled, share their hypotenuse, and have equal marked sides, so they are congruent (RHS). Their other sides, which are the hypotenuses of \(I\) and \(IV\), are then equal (matching sides of congruent triangles). \(I\) and \(IV\) are right-angled with equal marked sides, so they are congruent too (RHS). \(I\) is not congruent to \(II\) or \(III\): its hypotenuse is a shorter side of \(II\).

PP 4Sydney Technical High School, 2025, Year 9 Mathematics Assessment Task 4, Q12(e)

i. RHS needs the hypotenuse and a second side. Only the hypotenuse (8) is given, along with two angles (the right angle and \(53^\circ\)), not another side.

ii. AAS: \(\angle R=\angle U=90^\circ\), \(\angle S=\angle V=53^\circ\), and \(ST=VW=8\), which is opposite the right angle in both.
