Lesson 3

Proving properties with congruent triangles

The tests for congruent triangles can be used to prove facts about shapes. This lesson teaches how to set out a congruence proof, line by line, with a reason for every statement, and then uses such proofs to establish the properties of the special quadrilaterals: the parallelogram, rhombus, rectangle, square, trapezium and kite. The teaching follows Year 8 section 8H, Using congruent triangles to establish properties of quadrilaterals. The lesson ends with a short Chapter 8 review.
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:setting out a congruence proof}}
Using congruent triangles to establish properties of quadrilaterals (Year 8, 8H)

The properties of special quadrilaterals, including the parallelogram, rhombus, rectangle, square, trapezium and kite, can be examined more closely using congruence. By drawing the diagonals and using the tests for the congruence of triangles, we can prove many of the properties of these special quadrilaterals.

Let’s start: Do the diagonals bisect each other? (8H)

Here is a parallelogram with two pairs of parallel sides. Let’s assume also that opposite sides are equal (a basic property of a parallelogram, which we prove later in this exercise).

  • First locate \(\triangle ABE\) and \(\triangle CDE\).
  • What can be said about the angles \(\angle BAE\) and \(\angle DCE\)?
  • What can be said about the angles \(\angle ABE\) and \(\angle CDE\)?
  • What can be said about the sides \(AB\) and \(DC\)?
  • Now what can be said about \(\angle ABE\) and \(\angle CDE\)? Discuss the reasons.
  • What does this tell us about where the diagonals intersect? Do they bisect each other and why? (To bisect means to cut in half.)
{{svg:l3_start}}
The shape of a congruence proof

A proof that two triangles are congruent is set out in four steps, one statement per line.

  1. Name the two triangles: “In \(\triangle\ldots\) and \(\triangle\ldots\)”.
  2. List three pairs of equal parts, one per line, each with its reason in brackets.
  3. Write the congruence statement with the vertices in matching order, and the test in brackets: \(\therefore\triangle\ldots\equiv\triangle\ldots\) (SSS, SAS, AAS or RHS).
  4. Write what follows from the congruence.

The three pairs of parts must be the ones the test needs. For SAS, the angle must be the included angle, between the two pairs of sides.

Worked exampleYear 8 Example 13 (8H) Proving that diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other: keep for homework

Prove that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other and give reasons.

{{svg:l3_ex13}}
Solution
Explanation
\(\angle BAE=\angle DCE\) (alternate angles in parallel lines)
\(\angle ABE=\angle CDE\) (alternate angles in parallel lines)
\(AB=CD\) (given equal side lengths)
\(\therefore\triangle ABE\equiv\triangle CDE\) (AAS)
{{svg:l3_ex13a}}{{svg:l3_ex13b}}
\(\therefore BE=DE\) and \(AE=CE\)
\(\therefore\) Diagonals bisect each other.
The two triangles are congruent, using the AAS test.
Since \(\triangle ABE\) and \(\triangle CDE\) are congruent the corresponding sides are equal.
Steps the book skips: Example 13

In the left-hand Z shape the transversal is the diagonal \(AC\) and the parallel lines are \(AB\) and \(DC\). In the right-hand Z shape the transversal is the diagonal \(BD\). The equal sides \(AB\) and \(CD\) are opposite the vertically opposite angles at \(E\) in both triangles, so they are matching sides, as AAS needs.

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 2:properties of the special quadrilaterals}}
Key ideas (8H)

This is a summary of the properties of the special quadrilaterals.

■ Kite: A quadrilateral with two pairs of adjacent sides equal.

  • Two pairs of adjacent sides of a kite are equal.
  • One diagonal of a kite bisects the other diagonal.
  • One diagonal of a kite bisects the opposite angles.
  • The diagonals of a kite are perpendicular.
{{svg:l3_k_kite}}

■ Trapezium: A quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides

  • At least one pair of sides of a trapezium are parallel.
{{svg:l3_k_trap}}

■ Parallelogram: A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel

  • The opposite sides of a parallelogram are parallel.
  • The opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal.
  • The opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal.
  • The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
{{svg:l3_k_para}}

■ Rhombus: A parallelogram with two adjacent sides equal in length

  • The opposite sides of a rhombus are parallel.
  • All sides of a rhombus are equal.
  • The opposite angles of a rhombus are equal.
  • The diagonals of a rhombus bisect the vertex angles.
  • The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other.
  • The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular.
{{svg:l3_k_rhom}}

■ Rectangle: A parallelogram with a right angle

  • The opposite sides of a rectangle are parallel.
  • The opposite sides of a rectangle are equal.
  • All angles at the vertices of a rectangle are \(90^\circ\).
  • The diagonals of a rectangle are equal.
  • The diagonals of a rectangle bisect each other.
{{svg:l3_k_rect}}

■ Square: A rectangle with two adjacent sides equal

  • Opposite sides of a square are parallel.
  • All sides of a square are equal.
  • All angles at the vertices of a square are \(90^\circ\).
  • The diagonals of a square bisect the vertex angles.
  • The diagonals of a square bisect each other.
  • The diagonals of a square are perpendicular.
{{svg:l3_k_sq}}
Proving a property

You may use the definitions above, and any property that has already been proved.
