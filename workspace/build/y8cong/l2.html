Lesson 2

Congruent triangles

Two triangles are congruent when their three sides and three angles match, but we do not have to check all six. This lesson teaches the four tests (SSS, SAS, AAS and RHS) that decide congruence from just three matching parts, how to write a congruence statement with the test as its reason, and why two sides with a non-included angle, or three angles, are not enough. The teaching follows Year 8 section 8E, Congruent triangles.
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:the four tests for congruent triangles}}
Congruent triangles (Year 8, 8E)

Imagine the sorts of design and engineering problems we would face if we could not guarantee that two objects, such as window panes or roof truss frames, were not the same size or shape. Furthermore, it might not be possible or practical to measure every length and angle to test for congruence. In the case of triangles, it is possible to consider only a number of pairs of sides or angles to test whether or not they are congruent.

Key ideas (8E)
  • There are four ‘tests’ that can be used to decide if two triangles are congruent.
  • Two triangles are congruent when:
  • • the three sides of a triangle are respectively equal to the three sides of another triangle (SSS test)
{{svg:l2_k_sss}}
  • • two sides and the included angle of a triangle are respectively equal to two sides and the included angle of another triangle (SAS test)
{{svg:l2_k_sas}}
  • • two angles and one side of a triangle are respectively equal to two angles and the matching side of another triangle (AAS test)
{{svg:l2_k_aas}}
  • • the hypotenuse and a second side of a right-angled triangle are respectively equal to the hypotenuse and a second side of another right-angled triangle (RHS test)
{{svg:l2_k_rhs}}
Reading the four tests

S stands for a pair of equal sides and A for a pair of equal angles. In SAS the angle must be the included angle: the angle where the two marked sides meet. In AAS the side must be the matching side. RHS is only for right-angled triangles: R is the right angle and H is the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle.

There are two equal angles and one pair of equal corresponding sides. The side that is 5 cm is adjacent to the \(60^\circ\) angle on both triangles.
b RHS
There is a pair of right angles with equal hypotenuse lengths. A second pair of corresponding sides are also of equal length.

In a the 5 cm side is also opposite the \(70^\circ\) angle in both triangles, so it really is the matching side. In b the 15 m sides are opposite the right angles, so they are the hypotenuses, and the 12 m sides are the second pair of sides.

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 2:matching parts and congruence statements}}
Key ideas (8E), continued
  • Corresponding (matching) parts of two figures have the same geometric properties.
    • Vertex \(C\) corresponds to vertex \(E\).
    • Side \(AB\) corresponds to side \(FD\).
    • \(\angle B\) corresponds to \(\angle D\).
{{svg:l1_keytri}}
  • A congruence statement can be written using the symbol \(\equiv\). For example, \(\triangle ABC\equiv\triangle FDE\).
    • This is read as ‘\(\triangle ABC\) is congruent to \(\triangle FDE\)’.
    • In a congruence statement, vertices are named in matching order. For example, \(\triangle ABC\equiv\triangle FDE\) not \(\triangle ABC\equiv\triangle DEF\) because \(B\) matches \(D\).
Statement, then reason

Once a test shows that two triangles are congruent, write the congruence statement with the test after it in brackets, as in \(\triangle PQR\equiv\triangle XYZ\) (SSS).

Let’s start: How much information is enough? (8E)

When given one corresponding angle (say \(30^\circ\)) and one corresponding equal side length (say 3 cm), it is clearly not enough information to say two triangles are congruent. This is because more than one triangle can be drawn with the given information.

{{svg:l2_start}}

Decide if the following information is enough to determine if two triangles are congruent. If you can draw two non-identical triangles, then there is not enough information. If you can draw only one unique triangle, then you have the conditions for congruence.

  • \(\triangle ABC\) with \(AC=4\) cm and \(\angle C=40^\circ\)
  • \(\triangle ABC\) with \(AB=5\) cm and \(AC=4\) cm
  • \(\triangle ABC\) with \(AB=5\) cm, \(AC=4\) cm and \(\angle A=45^\circ\)
  • \(\triangle ABC\) with \(AB=5\) cm, \(AC=4\) cm and \(BC=3\) cm
  • \(\triangle ABC\) with \(AB=4\) cm, \(\angle A=40^\circ\) and \(\angle B=60^\circ\)
