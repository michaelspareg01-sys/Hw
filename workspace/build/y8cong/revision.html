Angle facts and transformations

Congruence uses two earlier pieces of Year 8 work. The angle facts of chapter 6 give the reasons in every congruence argument: angles at a point, vertically opposite angles, angles on parallel lines, and the angle sums of triangles and quadrilaterals. The transformations of sections 8A to 8C (reflection, translation and rotation) move a figure without changing its size or shape, and that is where congruence begins. These pages review both from the Year 8 textbook (Cambridge Maths NSW Stage 4 Year 8, chapters 6 and 8).

Transversal lines and parallel lines (Year 8, 6B)
  • A transversal is a line cutting two or more other lines.
  • Lines are parallel if they do not intersect. Parallel lines are marked with the same number of arrows.
  • When two parallel lines are cut by a transversal:
    • the corresponding angles are equal
    • the alternate angles are equal
    • the cointerior angles are supplementary (sum to \(180^\circ\)).
{{svg:rev_parallel}}
Triangles (Year 8, 6C)
  • Triangles classified by side lengths
    • Sides with the same number of dashes are of equal length.
  • The angle sum of a triangle is \(180^\circ\).
  • The exterior angle theorem: The exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of the two opposite interior angles.
{{svg:rev_tri}}
Isosceles triangles

In an isosceles triangle the angles opposite the equal sides are equal. These are the two base angles. Many congruence questions in this pack use this fact.

Quadrilaterals (Year 8, 6D)

All quadrilaterals can be drawn as two triangles and, since the six angles inside the two triangles make up the four angles of the quadrilateral, the angle sum is \(2\times180^\circ=360^\circ\).

  • The angle sum of any quadrilateral is \(360^\circ\).
  • Quadrilaterals with parallel sides contain two pairs of cointerior angles.
{{svg:rev_quad}}
Reflection (Year 8, 8A)

When an object is shifted from one position to another, rotated about a point, reflected over a line or enlarged by a scale factor, we say the object has been transformed. The names of these types of transformations are reflection, translation, rotation and dilation.

The first three of these transformations are called isometric transformations because the object’s geometric properties are unchanged and the transformed object will be congruent to the original object.

  • Reflection is an isometric transformation in which the size of the object is unchanged.
  • The image of a point \(A\) is denoted \(A'\).
  • Each point is reflected at right angles to the mirror line.
  • The distance from a point \(A\) to the mirror line is equal to the distance from the image point \(A'\) to the mirror line.
{{svg:rev_refl}}
  • When a point is reflected across the \(x\)-axis:
    • the \(x\)-coordinate remains unchanged
    • the \(y\)-coordinate undergoes a sign change. For example, \((5,3)\) becomes \((5,-3)\); that is, the \(y\) values are equal in magnitude but opposite in sign.
  • When the point is reflected across the \(y\)-axis:
    • the \(x\)-coordinate undergoes a sign change
    • the \(y\)-coordinate remains unchanged. For example, \((5,3)\) becomes \((-5,3)\).
Translation (Year 8, 8B)
  • Translation is an isometric transformation that involves a shift by a given distance in a given direction.
  • A vector \(\begin{bmatrix}a\\b\end{bmatrix}\) can be used to describe the distance and direction of a translation.
    vector \(\begin{bmatrix}2\\-3\end{bmatrix}\) (right 2, down 3)  vector \(\begin{bmatrix}-1\\4\end{bmatrix}\) (left 1, up 4)
    • If \(a\) is positive, you shift to the right.
    • If \(a\) is negative, you shift to the left.
    • If \(b\) is positive, you shift up.
    • If \(b\) is negative, you shift down.
  • The image of a point \(A\) is denoted \(A'\).
Rotation (Year 8, 8C)
  • Rotation is an isometric transformation about a centre point and by a given angle.
  • An object can be rotated clockwise or anticlockwise.
  • Each point is rotated on a circular arc about the centre of rotation, \(C\).
    • For example, this diagram shows a \(90^\circ\) anticlockwise rotation about the point \(C\).
