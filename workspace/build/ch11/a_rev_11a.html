Past-Paper Homework - Worked Solutions
PP 1Blacktown Boys' High School, 2022, HSC Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(d)
\[\begin{aligned} 6\sin x\cos x-\cos x&=0\\ \cos x\,(6\sin x-1)&=0 \end{aligned}\]

\(\cos x=0\): \(x=\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{3\pi}{2}\).   \(\sin x=\tfrac16\): \(x=\sin^{-1}\tfrac16\approx0.17\) or \(x=\pi-\sin^{-1}\tfrac16\approx2.97\).

So \(x\approx0.17,\ 1.57,\ 2.97,\ 4.71\). (Dividing by \(\cos x\) would lose \(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\) and \(\tfrac{3\pi}{2}\).)

PP 2Sydney Grammar School, 2020, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(a)
\[\begin{aligned} 2\sin x\cos x-\sqrt2\sin x&=0\\ \sin x\,(2\cos x-\sqrt2)&=0 \end{aligned}\]

\(\sin x=0\): \(x=0,\pi,2\pi\).   \(\cos x=\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\): \(x=\tfrac{\pi}{4},\tfrac{7\pi}{4}\).

So \(x=0,\ \tfrac{\pi}{4},\ \pi,\ \tfrac{7\pi}{4},\ 2\pi\).

PP 3Ascham School, 2023, Year 12 Mathematics Extension 1 Trial Examination, Q13(a)

(i)

\[\tan 2x+\tan x=\dfrac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x}+\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}=\dfrac{\sin 2x\cos x+\cos 2x\sin x}{\cos 2x\cos x}=\dfrac{\sin(2x+x)}{\cos 2x\cos x}=\dfrac{\sin 3x}{\cos 2x\cos x}\]

(ii) \(\tan 2x+\tan x=0\) requires \(\sin 3x=0\) (with \(\cos 2x\cos x\ne0\)). For \(0\le x\le\tfrac{\pi}{2}\), \(0\le 3x\le\tfrac{3\pi}{2}\), so \(3x=0\) or \(\pi\).

So \(x=0\) or \(x=\tfrac{\pi}{3}\). (At \(x=\tfrac{\pi}{3}\), \(\cos\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\cos\tfrac{\pi}{3}\ne0\), so it is valid. \(x=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\) is excluded because \(\tan x\) is undefined there.)

PP 4James Ruse Agricultural High School, 2020, Year 12 Trial HSC Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(c)

(i) Write \(\sin4\theta=\sin(3\theta+\theta)\) and \(\sin2\theta=\sin(3\theta-\theta)\). Adding, the \(\cos3\theta\sin\theta\) terms cancel:

\[\sin 2\theta+\sin 4\theta=2\sin3\theta\cos\theta.\]

Also \(\sin6\theta=2\sin3\theta\cos3\theta\). So

\[\begin{aligned} \text{LHS}&=2\sin3\theta\,(\cos\theta-\cos3\theta)\\ &=2\sin3\theta\,\big(\cos(2\theta-\theta)-\cos(2\theta+\theta)\big)\\ &=2\sin3\theta\,(2\sin2\theta\sin\theta)=4\sin3\theta\sin2\theta\sin\theta. \end{aligned}\]

(ii) The equation is equivalent to \(4\sin3\theta\sin2\theta\sin\theta=0\), for \(0\le\theta\le\pi\):

\(\sin3\theta=0\): \(\theta=0,\tfrac{\pi}{3},\tfrac{2\pi}{3},\pi\);   \(\sin2\theta=0\): \(\theta=0,\tfrac{\pi}{2},\pi\);   \(\sin\theta=0\): \(\theta=0,\pi\).

So \(\theta=0,\ \tfrac{\pi}{3},\ \tfrac{\pi}{2},\ \tfrac{2\pi}{3},\ \pi\).

PP 5The King's School, 2021, Year 12 Alternative Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(c)

(i)

\[\begin{aligned} \sin(x-y)\sin(x+y)&=(\sin x\cos y-\cos x\sin y)(\sin x\cos y+\cos x\sin y)\\ &=\sin^2x\cos^2y-\cos^2x\sin^2y\\ &=\sin^2x(1-\sin^2y)-(1-\sin^2x)\sin^2y\\ &=\sin^2x-\sin^2y \end{aligned}\]

(ii) By part (i) with \(3x\) and \(x\): \(\sin^23x-\sin^2x=\sin2x\sin4x\). The equation becomes

\[\sin2x\sin4x-\sin4x=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sin4x\,(\sin2x-1)=0.\]

For \(0\le x\le\pi\): \(\sin4x=0\) with \(0\le4x\le4\pi\) gives \(x=0,\tfrac{\pi}{4},\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{3\pi}{4},\pi\); \(\sin2x=1\) gives \(x=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\) (already found).

So \(x=0,\ \tfrac{\pi}{4},\ \tfrac{\pi}{2},\ \tfrac{3\pi}{4},\ \pi\).
