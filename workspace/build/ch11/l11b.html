Lesson 11B

The sum of sine and cosine functions

Write \(a\sin x+b\cos x\) as a single wave \(R\sin(x\pm\alpha)\) or \(R\cos(x\pm\alpha)\), then use it to sketch, find maximum and minimum values, and solve equations and inequations.
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:adding a sine wave and a cosine wave}}
Sketching y = sin x + cos x by graphical methods

Add the heights of \(y=\sin x\) and \(y=\cos x\) at convenient points.

  • The new graph has the same period \(2\pi\). It looks like a wave, and within \([0,2\pi]\) its zeroes are at \(x=\tfrac{3\pi}{4}\) and \(x=\tfrac{7\pi}{4}\), where \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\) take opposite values.
  • The amplitude is bigger than 1. At \(x=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\) the value is \(\tfrac12\sqrt2+\tfrac12\sqrt2=\sqrt2\), so if the maximum occurs there, the amplitude is \(\sqrt2\).
{{svg:sumwave}}

This suggests \(y=\sqrt2\sin\left(x+\tfrac{\pi}{4}\right)\): the sine curve stretched by \(\sqrt2\) and shifted left \(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\). Check by expanding:

\[\sqrt2\sin\left(x+\tfrac{\pi}{4}\right)=\sqrt2\left(\sin x\cos\tfrac{\pi}{4}+\cos x\sin\tfrac{\pi}{4}\right)=\sin x+\cos x,\quad\text{because }\cos\tfrac{\pi}{4}=\sin\tfrac{\pi}{4}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}.\]
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 2:the auxiliary-angle method}}
Auxiliary-angle method
  • Any function \(f(x)=a\sin x+b\cos x\) (\(a, b\) constants, not both zero) can be written in any one of the four forms \[y=R\sin(x-\alpha)\qquad y=R\sin(x+\alpha)\qquad y=R\cos(x-\alpha)\qquad y=R\cos(x+\alpha)\] where \(R>0\) and \(0^\circ\le\alpha<360^\circ\). The constant \(R=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\) is the same for all forms, but the auxiliary angle \(\alpha\) depends on which form is chosen.
  • To begin, expand the standard form and equate coefficients of \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\). Be careful to identify the quadrant in which \(\alpha\) lies.
The algorithm
  1. Expand the required form with the compound-angle formula.
  2. Equate coefficients of \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\): this gives \(R\cos\alpha=\dots\;(1)\) and \(R\sin\alpha=\dots\;(2)\).
  3. Square and add: \(R^2=a^2+b^2\), and \(R>0\).
  4. Use the signs of \(\cos\alpha\) and \(\sin\alpha\) to find the quadrant, then the related angle.

Maximum value \(=R\), minimum value \(=-R\). The graph is a sine or cosine wave with amplitude \(R\), shifted sideways by \(\alpha\).

Worked example 1Keep for homework
Express \(y=\sin x+\cos x\) in the two forms a \(R\sin(x+\alpha)\), b \(R\cos(x+\alpha)\), where \(R>0\) and \(0\le\alpha<2\pi\). Then sketch the curve, showing all intercepts and turning points in \(0\le x\le 2\pi\).
Solution

a Expanding, \(R\sin(x+\alpha)=R\sin x\cos\alpha+R\cos x\sin\alpha\), so for all \(x\),

\[\sin x+\cos x=R\sin x\cos\alpha+R\cos x\sin\alpha.\]

Equating coefficients:

\[\begin{aligned} \sin x:\quad R\cos\alpha&=1 \quad(1)\\ \cos x:\quad R\sin\alpha&=1 \quad(2) \end{aligned}\]

Squaring and adding, \(R^2=2\), and because \(R>0\), \(R=\sqrt2\).

\[\cos\alpha=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}>0,\qquad \sin\alpha=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}>0\]

so \(\alpha\) is in the 1st quadrant, with related angle \(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\):

\[\sin x+\cos x=\sqrt2\sin\left(x+\tfrac{\pi}{4}\right)\]

b Expanding, \(R\cos(x+\alpha)=R\cos x\cos\alpha-R\sin x\sin\alpha\).

\[\begin{aligned} \cos x:\quad R\cos\alpha&=1 \quad(1)\\ \sin x:\quad R\sin\alpha&=-1 \quad(2) \end{aligned}\]

Squaring and adding, \(R=\sqrt2\). Now \(\cos\alpha>0\) and \(\sin\alpha<0\), so \(\alpha\) is in the 4th quadrant, with related angle \(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\):

\[\sin x+\cos x=\sqrt2\cos\left(x+\tfrac{7\pi}{4}\right)\]
{{svg:q45}}{{svg:q315}}

The graph is \(y=\sin x\) shifted left \(\tfrac{\pi}{4}\) and stretched vertically by \(\sqrt2\). The \(x\)-intercepts are \(x=\tfrac{3\pi}{4}\) and \(x=\tfrac{7\pi}{4}\); there is a maximum of \(\sqrt2\) when \(x=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\) and a minimum of \(-\sqrt2\) when \(x=\tfrac{5\pi}{4}\).

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 3:approximating the auxiliary angle}}
When α is not a special angle

Unless special angles are involved, approximate the auxiliary angle on the calculator. Degrees or radians may be used.

Worked example 2Keep for homework
a Express \(y=3\sin x-4\cos x\) in the form \(y=R\cos(x-\alpha)\), where \(R>0\) and \(0^\circ\le\alpha<360^\circ\), giving \(\alpha\) correct to the nearest degree.
b Sketch the curve, showing all intercepts and turning points in \(-180^\circ\le x\le 180^\circ\), correct to the nearest degree.
Solution

a Expanding, \(R\cos(x-\alpha)=R\cos x\cos\alpha+R\sin x\sin\alpha\), so for all \(x\),

\[3\sin x-4\cos x=R\cos x\cos\alpha+R\sin x\sin\alpha.\] \[\begin{aligned} \cos x:\quad R\cos\alpha&=-4 \quad(1)\\ \sin x:\quad R\sin\alpha&=3 \quad(2) \end{aligned}\]

Squaring and adding, \(R^2=25\), so \(R=5\).

\[\cos\alpha=-\tfrac45,\qquad\sin\alpha=\tfrac35\]

so \(\alpha\) is in the 2nd quadrant, with related angle about \(37^\circ\). Hence

\[3\sin x-4\cos x=5\cos(x-\alpha),\ \text{where}\ \alpha\approx 143^\circ.\]
{{svg:q143}}

b The graph is \(y=\cos x\) shifted right by \(\alpha\approx143^\circ\) and stretched vertically by 5. The \(x\)-intercepts are \(x\approx53^\circ\) and \(x\approx-127^\circ\); there is a maximum of 5 when \(x\approx143^\circ\) and a minimum of \(-5\) when \(x\approx-37^\circ\). (The \(y\)-intercept is \(-4\).)

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 4:solving a sin x + b cos x = c, and inequations}}
Auxiliary-angle method for a sin x + b cos x = c
  • Get the LHS into one of the four forms \(R\sin(x+\alpha)\), \(R\sin(x-\alpha)\), \(R\cos(x+\alpha)\) or \(R\cos(x-\alpha)\).
  • Then solve the resulting equation. Always keep track of the restriction on the compound angle: if \(0^\circ\le x\le 360^\circ\), then \(-\alpha\le x-\alpha\le 360^\circ-\alpha\).
  • For an inequation, solve the equation first, then use the graph of the wave and the horizontal line to read off where the curve is above or below the line.

a Using \(3\sin x-4\cos x=5\cos(x-\alpha)\), where \(\alpha\approx143^\circ\):

\[\begin{aligned} x-\alpha&\approx-114^\circ\ \text{or}\ -246^\circ\\ x&\approx 30^\circ\ \text{or}\ -103^\circ \end{aligned}\]

Be careful to use the calculator's memory here. Never re-enter approximations of the angles.

b The graph of \(y=3\sin x-4\cos x\) with the line \(y=-2\) added verifies the two answers in part a, and shows that the solution of the inequation is

\[-103^\circ\le x\le 30^\circ.\] {{svg:fivecos}}
