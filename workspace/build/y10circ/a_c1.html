a Every translation of any distance along \(\ell\) ‘maps’ \(\ell\) onto itself. Let \(O\) be any point on \(\ell\). Then reflection in the line \(m\) through \(O\) perpendicular to \(\ell\), and rotation of \(180^\circ\) about \(O\), both map \(\ell\) onto itself (and are identical in their action on the points of \(\ell\)). Reflection in the perpendicular bisector of the interval \(PQ\) maps \(\ell\) onto itself, and exchanges the points \(P\) and \(Q\). (Rotation of \(180^\circ\) about the midpoint of \(PQ\) does the same.)

PP 1Sydney Boys High School, 2020, Year 10 Stage 5.3 Term 4 Yearly Assessment, Mathematics Stage 5.3, Q40(b) I

In the cross section, \(X\) and \(Y\) lie on the circle with centre \(A\), so \(XY\) is a chord of that circle. \(AB\) bisects \(XY\), so the line \(AB\) passes through the centre \(A\) and the midpoint of the chord \(XY\).

The line from the centre of a circle to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord. \(\therefore AB\perp XY\).

The marking scheme gives the mark for using this circle theorem. A congruent-triangle proof (\(AX=AY\), \(MX=MY\), \(AM\) common, SSS) is also correct but long for 1 mark.

PP 2Sydney Boys High School, 2022, Year 10 Stage 5.3 Term 4 Yearly Assessment, Mathematics, Part A Q9

The pentagon is regular, so its five sides are equal chords of the circle. Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre, and these five angles make a revolution, so \(\angle AOB=\dfrac{360^\circ}{5}=72^\circ\).

\(AOF\) is a diameter, a straight line, so \(\angle BOF=180^\circ-72^\circ=108^\circ\).

\(OB=OF\) (radii), so \(\triangle OBF\) is isosceles and \(\angle OBF=\dfrac{180^\circ-108^\circ}{2}=36^\circ\).

The school’s solution reaches the same answer through the interior angle of the pentagon: \(\angle EAB=108^\circ\), reflex \(\angle EOB=216^\circ\), so \(\angle FOB=108^\circ\) and \(\angle OBF=36^\circ\).

In \(\triangle OJI\) and \(\triangle OJK\):

\(OJ\) is common
