Angles, triangles, congruence and proof

Circle geometry is built on the geometry of Years 7 to 9: angle facts with parallel lines, the angle sum and exterior angle of a triangle, isosceles triangles, the congruence and similarity tests, and proofs written as a chain of steps, each with a reason. These pages review that work from the Year 8 textbook (Cambridge Maths NSW Stage 4 Year 8, chapters 3, 6 and 8). Every circle theorem in this pack is proved with these tools.

Transversals and parallel lines (Year 8, 6B)

A transversal is a line cutting two or more other lines. When a transversal crosses two or more lines, pairs of angles can be corresponding (in matching positions), alternate (on opposite sides of the transversal and inside the other two lines), co-interior (on the same side of the transversal and inside the other two lines), vertically opposite, or angles on a straight line. Lines are parallel if they do not intersect. Parallel lines are marked with the same number of arrows.

{{svg:rev_parallel}}

When two parallel lines are cut by a transversal:

  • the corresponding angles are equal (\(a=b\));
  • the alternate angles are equal (\(a=b\));
  • the co-interior angles are supplementary, that is, they sum to \(180^\circ\) (\(x+y=180\)).
Triangles (Year 8, 6C)
  • A triangle has 3 sides, 3 vertices and 3 interior angles. Sides with the same number of dashes are of equal length.
  • Triangles are classified by side lengths (scalene, isosceles, equilateral) and by interior angles (acute-angled, right-angled, obtuse-angled).
  • The angle sum of a triangle is \(180^\circ\).
  • The exterior angle theorem: the exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of the two opposite interior angles (\(z=x+y\)).
{{svg:rev_isos_ext}}
Isosceles triangles

In an isosceles triangle the two equal sides meet at the apex. The angles opposite the equal sides, the base angles, are equal. In circle geometry, two radii of the same circle are always equal, so a triangle with two radii as sides is isosceles. This is used in almost every proof in this pack.

Worked exampleYear 8 Example 3 (6C)
Find the value of \(a\) in these triangles. {{svg:rev_ex3}}
Solution
a \(\begin{aligned}[t]a+38+92&=180\quad\text{(angle sum of triangle)}\\a+130&=180\\\therefore a&=50\end{aligned}\) b \(\begin{aligned}[t]a+a+26&=180\\2a+26&=180\quad\text{(angle sum of a triangle)}\\2a&=154\\\therefore a&=77\end{aligned}\)

Steps the book skips: Year 8 Example 3In a, \(38+92=130\) and \(180-130=50\). In b, the angles opposite the equal sides are equal, so both base angles are \(a^\circ\).

Congruent triangles (Year 8, 8E)

There are four ‘tests’ that can be used to decide if two triangles are congruent. Two triangles are congruent when:

  • the three sides of a triangle are respectively equal to the three sides of another triangle (SSS test);
  • two sides and the included angle of a triangle are respectively equal to two sides and the included angle of another triangle (SAS test);
  • two angles and one side of a triangle are respectively equal to two angles and the matching side of another triangle (AAS test);
  • the hypotenuse and a second side of a right-angled triangle are respectively equal to the hypotenuse and a second side of another right-angled triangle (RHS test).

Corresponding (matching) parts of two figures have the same geometric properties. A congruence statement can be written using the symbol \(\equiv\). For example, \(\triangle ABC\equiv\triangle FDE\) is read as ‘\(\triangle ABC\) is congruent to \(\triangle FDE\)’. In a congruence statement, vertices are named in matching order.

Similar triangles (Year 8, 8G)

Two triangles are similar when:

  • two angles of a triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle;
  • three sides of a triangle are proportional to three sides of another triangle;
  • two sides of a triangle are proportional to two sides of another triangle, and the included angles are equal;
  • the hypotenuse and a second side of a right-angled triangle are proportional to the hypotenuse and second side of another right-angled triangle.

If two triangles \(\triangle ABC\) and \(\triangle DEF\) are similar, we write \(\triangle ABC\,|||\,\triangle DEF\). The common ratio of matching sides is the scale factor.

Properties of the special quadrilaterals (Year 8, 8H)
  • Parallelogram: a quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel. The opposite sides are equal, the opposite angles are equal, and the diagonals bisect each other.
  • Rhombus: a parallelogram with two adjacent sides equal in length. All sides are equal, and the diagonals bisect each other at right angles and bisect the vertex angles.
  • Rectangle: a parallelogram with a right angle. All angles at the vertices are \(90^\circ\), and the diagonals are equal and bisect each other.
  • Square: a rectangle with two adjacent sides equal. The diagonals are equal, bisect each other at right angles and bisect the vertex angles.
  • Kite: two pairs of adjacent sides equal. One diagonal bisects the other, and the diagonals are perpendicular.
Worked exampleYear 8 Example 13 (8H)
Prove that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other and give reasons.
Solution

\(\angle BAE=\angle DCE\) (alternate angles in parallel lines)
\(\angle ABE=\angle CDE\) (alternate angles in parallel lines)
\(AB=CD\) (given equal side lengths)
\(\therefore\triangle ABE\equiv\triangle CDE\) (AAS)

\(\therefore BE=DE\) and \(AE=CE\)
\(\therefore\) Diagonals bisect each other.

{{svg:rev_ex13}}
The shape of a geometric proof

Every line of a proof is a statement followed by its reason in brackets. Name the two triangles first, list three matching facts, state the test, and then use ‘matching sides (or angles) of congruent triangles’ to reach the result. The two triangles above are congruent by AAS, and since \(\triangle ABE\) and \(\triangle CDE\) are congruent, the corresponding sides are equal. The same pattern proves the chord theorems of Lesson C1.

Pythagoras’ theorem and sectors (Year 8, 3K and 3F)

Pythagoras’ theorem. The hypotenuse is the longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle. The square of the hypotenuse is the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two shorter sides: \(a^2+b^2=c^2\).

Sectors. A sector is formed by dividing a circle with two radii. A sector’s area is determined by calculating a fraction of the area of a circle with the same radius. The fraction is \(\dfrac{\theta}{360}\), so

\[\text{Sector area}=\frac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2.\]
From Topic 11: the area of a triangle

The area of a triangle with two sides \(a\) and \(b\) and included angle \(C\) is \(\text{Area}=\tfrac12ab\sin C\). One past-paper question in Lesson C1 uses it.
