Lesson 13D

y′ = g(y) and the logistic equation

Solve autonomous DEs \(y'=g(y)\) by separating or by taking reciprocals, including exponential growth \(y'=ky\) and the logistic equation \(y'=ky(P-y)\), and use the DE itself to analyse gradient and concavity.
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:solving y′ = g(y)}}
Two equivalent approaches

Solve \(y'=g(y)\) by integration using either of the forms

\[\dfrac{1}{g(y)}\,dy=dx\qquad\text{OR}\qquad\dfrac{dx}{dy}=\dfrac{1}{g(y)}.\]

A DE that does not involve the independent variable \(x\) is called autonomous. Laws of physics are usually independent of time, so autonomous DEs model populations, radioactive decay and cooling.

Worked example 1Keep for homework
Solve \(y'=e^{y}\) using both approaches.
Solution

EITHER regard it as a separable DE, \(e^{-y}\,dy=dx\), and integrate:

\[\begin{aligned}-\int-e^{-y}\,dy&=-\int dx\\e^{-y}&=-x+C\\x&=-e^{-y}+C\end{aligned}\]

OR take reciprocals, \(\dfrac{dx}{dy}=e^{-y}\), and integrate:

\[x=-e^{-y}+C,\quad\text{for some constant }C.\]

After either approach, solving for \(y\) gives \(y=-\log(-x+C)\).

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 2:the exponential growth DE}}
y′ = ky

Exponential growth has the DE \(y'=ky\) (\(k\ne0\)): the rate of change of the quantity is proportional to the quantity. Growth occurs when \(k\) is positive, and decay when \(k\) is negative. Its general solution is \(y=Ae^{kx}\), where \(A\) can be any real number. (The constant function \(y=0\) is the case \(A=0\).)

Worked example 2Keep for homework
Solve the differential equation \(y'=-3y\) given the initial value \(y(2)=50\).
Solution

First, the constant function \(y=0\) is trivially a solution. Otherwise, rearranging, \(\dfrac1y\,dy=-3\,dx\), and integrating,

\[\begin{aligned}\log|y|&=-3x+C,\quad\text{for some constant }C\\|y|&=e^{-3x+C}=Ae^{-3x},\quad\text{where }A=e^C\\y&=Ae^{-3x},\quad\text{where }A\text{ can be positive or negative.}\end{aligned}\]

Because the constant function \(y=0\) is a solution, the general solution is \(y=Ae^{-3x}\), where \(A\) can be any real number. Substituting \(y(2)=50\) gives \(50=Ae^{-6}\), so \(A=50e^6\), and \(y=50e^{-3(x-2)}\).

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 3:the logistic equation}}
The logistic differential equation
  • In this course a logistic DE has the form \(y'=ky(P-y)\), where \(k\) and \(P\) are constants (assumed positive below).
  • The constant functions \(y=0\) and \(y=P\) are solutions.
  • Otherwise there are three groups of solution curves: between \(y=0\) and \(y=P\), with domain all real \(x\); above \(y=P\); and below \(y=0\).
  • Solving needs a partial fractions decomposition, which will always be given in a question.
  • In applications, one group, or even two groups, may have no significance.
Worked example 3Keep for homework
a Show that \(\dfrac{1}{y(1-y)}=\dfrac1y+\dfrac{1}{1-y}\).   b Hence solve \(y'=y(1-y)\), writing \(y\) as the subject.
Solution

a RHS \(=\dfrac{(1-y)+y}{y(1-y)}=\) LHS.

b First, \(y=0\) and \(y=1\) are trivially solutions. Otherwise, using part a,

\[\begin{aligned}\left(\dfrac1y+\dfrac{1}{1-y}\right)dy&=dx\\\log|y|-\log|1-y|&=x+C\\\left|\dfrac{y}{1-y}\right|&=Ae^x,\quad A=e^C>0\\\dfrac{y}{1-y}&=Ae^x,\quad A\ne0\\y&=\dfrac{Ae^x}{Ae^x+1}=\dfrac{1}{1+Be^{-x}}\end{aligned}\]

where \(B=A^{-1}\). Hence \(y=0\), or \(y=1\), or \(y=\dfrac{1}{1+Be^{-x}}\) for some non-zero constant \(B\).

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 4:using the DE to find the second derivative}}
Concavity straight from the DE

Rather than differentiating the solution twice, differentiate the DE itself with the chain rule: \(y''=\dfrac{d}{dy}\big(g(y)\big)\times\dfrac{dy}{dx}=g'(y)\,y'\).
