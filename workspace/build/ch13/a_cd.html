PP 1Sydney Grammar School, 2020, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(b)
\[e^y\,dy=2x\,dx\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e^y=x^2+C\]

\(y(0)=\ln4\): \(4=0+C\), so \(C=4\) and \(y=\ln(x^2+4)\).

PP 2Abbotsleigh, 2021, Higher School Certificate Assessment Task 4, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(f)

\(y=0\) is a solution, but it fails \(y(0)=2\). Otherwise

\[\int\dfrac{dy}{y}=\int\dfrac{dx}{1+x^2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ln|y|=\tan^{-1}x+C\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=Ae^{\tan^{-1}x}.\]

\(y(0)=2\): \(A=2\), so \(y=2e^{\tan^{-1}x}\).

PP 3Hornsby Girls High School, 2023, Year 12 Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(b)
\[y\,dy=(1-x)\,dx\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\tfrac12y^2=x-\tfrac12x^2+C\]

At \((2,0)\): \(0=2-2+C\), so \(C=0\). Hence \(y^2=2x-x^2\), that is, \((x-1)^2+y^2=1\): the circle with centre \((1,0)\) and radius 1.

PP 4Sydney Grammar School, 2022, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(e)
\[\int\dfrac{dy}{y^2+4}=\int x\,dx\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\tfrac12\tan^{-1}\dfrac y2=\tfrac12x^2+C\]

At \((0,2)\): \(\tfrac12\times\tfrac{\pi}{4}=C\), so \(\tan^{-1}\dfrac y2=x^2+\dfrac{\pi}{4}\) and

\[y=2\tan\left(x^2+\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right).\]

(The solution is valid while \(x^2+\tfrac{\pi}{4}<\tfrac{\pi}{2}\), that is, \(|x|<\tfrac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\).)

PP 5Sydney Technical High School, 2023, Trial HSC, Extension 1 Mathematics, Q13(a)

\(y=-2\) is a constant solution, but it fails \(y(0)=7\). Otherwise

\[\int\dfrac{dy}{y+2}=\int3x^{\frac12}\,dx\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ln|y+2|=2x^{\frac32}+C.\]

\(y(0)=7\): \(\ln9=C\). Since \(y+2=9>0\) initially, \(y+2=9e^{2x^{\frac32}}\), so \(y=9e^{2x\sqrt x}-2\).

PP 6Blacktown Boys' High School, 2020, HSC Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(d)
\[\int(2-y)\,dy=\int\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{2-x^2}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad2y-\tfrac12y^2=\sin^{-1}\dfrac{x}{\sqrt2}+C\]

When \(x=1\), \(y=0\): \(0=\tfrac{\pi}{4}+C\), so \(C=-\tfrac{\pi}{4}\). Multiplying by \(-2\):

\[y^2-4y+2\sin^{-1}\dfrac{x}{\sqrt2}-\dfrac{\pi}{2}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=2\pm\sqrt{4-2\sin^{-1}\dfrac{x}{\sqrt2}+\dfrac{\pi}{2}}.\]

At \(x=1\) the square root is \(\sqrt{4}=2\), and \(y=0\) needs the negative sign:

\[y=2-\sqrt{4+\dfrac{\pi}{2}-2\sin^{-1}\dfrac{x}{\sqrt2}}.\]
