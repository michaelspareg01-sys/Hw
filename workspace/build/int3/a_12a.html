PP 2Baulkham Hills High School, 2023, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q9

For \(x\cos^{-1}x\) use the product rule with \(u=x\), \(v=\cos^{-1}x\), \(u'=1\), \(v'=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\). For \(\sqrt{1-x^2}=(1-x^2)^{\frac12}\) use the chain rule: its derivative is \(\tfrac12(1-x^2)^{-\frac12}\times(-2x)=\dfrac{-x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\). So

\[\begin{aligned}\frac{d}{dx}\Big(x\cos^{-1}x-\sqrt{1-x^2}\Big)&=\cos^{-1}x\times1+x\times\Big(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\Big)-\frac{-x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\\&=\cos^{-1}x-\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\\&=\cos^{-1}x.\end{aligned}\]

Answer C.

When \(x=-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\), \(y=\sin^{-1}\big(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big)+\tfrac{\pi}{2}=\sin^{-1}0+\tfrac{\pi}{2}=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\). So the point is \(\big(-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big)\).

By Box 2 with \(a=1\) and \(b=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\big(x+\frac{\pi}{2}\big)^2}}\). At \(x=-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-0^2}}=1\).

PP 6Hurlstone Agricultural High School, 2023, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q14(a)(i)(ii)

(i) Let \(u=x\) and \(v=\tan^{-1}x\). Then \(u'=1\) and \(v'=\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}\). By the product rule,

\[\frac{d}{dx}\big(x\tan^{-1}x\big)=\tan^{-1}x\times1+x\times\frac{1}{1+x^2}=\tan^{-1}x+\frac{x}{1+x^2}.\]

(ii) From (i), \(\tan^{-1}x=\dfrac{d}{dx}\big(x\tan^{-1}x\big)-\dfrac{x}{1+x^2}\). Integrate both sides from 0 to \(\sqrt3\). For the last term, \(\displaystyle\int\frac{x}{1+x^2}\,dx=\tfrac12\int\frac{2x}{1+x^2}\,dx=\tfrac12\ln(1+x^2)+C\), since \(2x\) is the derivative of \(1+x^2\).

\[\begin{aligned}\int_0^{\sqrt3}\tan^{-1}x\,dx&=\Big[x\tan^{-1}x\Big]_0^{\sqrt3}-\int_0^{\sqrt3}\frac{x}{1+x^2}\,dx\\&=\Big(\sqrt3\tan^{-1}\sqrt3-0\Big)-\Big[\tfrac12\ln(1+x^2)\Big]_0^{\sqrt3}\\&=\sqrt3\times\frac{\pi}{3}-\big(\tfrac12\ln4-\tfrac12\ln1\big)\\&=\frac{\pi\sqrt3}{3}-\tfrac12\ln4\\&=\frac{\pi\sqrt3}{3}-\ln2.\end{aligned}\]
PP 7Blacktown Boys' High School, 2024, HSC Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(d)

Use the quotient rule with \(u=\sin^{-1}(3x)\) and \(v=3x\). Then \(u'=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{1-9x^2}}\) (Box 2 with \(a=3\)) and \(v'=3\). In the third line, the top and bottom are multiplied by \(\sqrt{1-9x^2}\).

PP 8Knox Grammar School, 2023, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(c)

By the chain rule, with Box 2 for \(\sin^{-1}\dfrac{x}{b}\) (here \(a=\tfrac1b\)),

\[g'(x)=2\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{b}\times\frac{d}{dx}\Big(\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{b}\Big)=2\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{b}\times\frac{\frac1b}{\sqrt{1-\frac{x^2}{b^2}}}.\]

At \(x=\tfrac{b}{2}\), \(\dfrac{x}{b}=\tfrac12\), so \(\sin^{-1}\dfrac{x}{b}=\sin^{-1}\tfrac12=\tfrac{\pi}{6}\) and \(\sqrt{1-\dfrac{x^2}{b^2}}=\sqrt{1-\tfrac14}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\). Hence

\[g'\Big(\frac{b}{2}\Big)=2\times\frac{\pi}{6}\times\frac1b\times\frac{2}{\sqrt3}=\frac{2\pi}{3\sqrt3\,b}.\]

This gradient is \(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\), so

\[\frac{2\pi}{3\sqrt3\,b}=\frac{2\pi}{3},\quad\text{so}\quad\sqrt3\,b=1,\quad\text{and}\quad b=\frac{1}{\sqrt3}=\frac{\sqrt3}{3}.\]
PP 9Ascham School, 2020, Mathematics Extension 1 Trial HSC Examination, Q14(c)

Work with \(0<x<1\), where both derivatives exist. By Box 2 with \(a=2\), \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\cos^{-1}(2x-1)=-\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{1-(2x-1)^2}}\). By Box 3 with \(u=\sqrt x\) and \(u'=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt x}\), \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\cos^{-1}\sqrt x=-\dfrac{u'}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}=-\dfrac{\frac{1}{2\sqrt x}}{\sqrt{1-x}}=-\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt x\,\sqrt{1-x}}\).

Also \(1-(2x-1)^2=1-(4x^2-4x+1)=4x-4x^2=4x(1-x)\), so \(\sqrt{1-(2x-1)^2}=2\sqrt x\,\sqrt{1-x}\). Hence

\[\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{1-(2x-1)^2}}-2\times\Big(-\frac{1}{2\sqrt x\,\sqrt{1-x}}\Big)\\&=-\frac{2}{2\sqrt x\,\sqrt{1-x}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt x\,\sqrt{1-x}}\\&=-\frac{1}{\sqrt x\,\sqrt{1-x}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt x\,\sqrt{1-x}}\\&=0.\end{aligned}\]
