Tutor demo. Squaring, \(y^2=25-x^2\). The limits are \(x=3\) (the line) and \(x=5\) (where the semicircle meets the \(x\)-axis).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{volume}&=\int_3^5\pi y^2\,dx\\&=\pi\int_3^5\big(25-x^2\big)\,dx\\&=\pi\Big[25x-\tfrac13x^3\Big]_3^5\\&=\pi\Big(\big(25\times5-\tfrac13\times5^3\big)-\big(25\times3-\tfrac13\times3^3\big)\Big)\\&=\pi\big(125-\tfrac{125}{3}-75+9\big)\\&=\pi\big(59-\tfrac{125}{3}\big)\\&=\tfrac{52\pi}{3}\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]
Your turn. Squaring, \(y^2=9-x^2\). The limits are \(x=1\) and \(x=3\).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{volume}&=\int_1^3\pi y^2\,dx\\&=\pi\int_1^3\big(9-x^2\big)\,dx\\&=\pi\Big[9x-\tfrac13x^3\Big]_1^3\\&=\pi\Big(\big(9\times3-\tfrac13\times3^3\big)-\big(9\times1-\tfrac13\times1^3\big)\Big)\\&=\pi\big(27-9-9+\tfrac13\big)\\&=\pi\big(9+\tfrac13\big)\\&=\tfrac{28\pi}{3}\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]
C2
Tutor demo. Because \(y=2x^2\), \(x^2=\tfrac12y\). The limits are \(y=0\) (the vertex) and \(y=8\) (the line).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{volume}&=\int_0^8\pi x^2\,dy\\&=\pi\int_0^8\tfrac12y\,dy\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac14y^2\Big]_0^8\\&=\pi\big(\tfrac14\times8^2-\tfrac14\times0^2\big)\\&=16\pi\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]
Your turn. Because \(y=x^2\), \(x^2=y\). The limits are \(y=0\) and \(y=9\).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{volume}&=\int_0^9\pi x^2\,dy\\&=\pi\int_0^9y\,dy\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac12y^2\Big]_0^9\\&=\pi\big(\tfrac12\times9^2-\tfrac12\times0^2\big)\\&=\tfrac{81\pi}{2}\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]
C3
Tutor demo. a The parabola is \(y_2=9-x^2\). The line \(AB\) has gradient \(\dfrac{0-9}{3-0}=-3\), so it is \(y_1=9-3x\). For \(0\le x\le3\), \(y_2-y_1=3x-x^2=x(3-x)\ge0\).
Squaring, \(y_2^{\,2}=\big(9-x^2\big)^2=81-18x^2+x^4\) and \(y_1^{\,2}=(9-3x)^2=81-54x+9x^2\).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{volume}&=\int_0^3\pi\big(y_2^{\,2}-y_1^{\,2}\big)\,dx\\&=\pi\int_0^3\Big(\big(81-18x^2+x^4\big)-\big(81-54x+9x^2\big)\Big)\,dx\\&=\pi\int_0^3\big(x^4-27x^2+54x\big)\,dx\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac15x^5-9x^3+27x^2\Big]_0^3\\&=\pi\Big(\big(\tfrac15\times3^5-9\times3^3+27\times3^2\big)-0\Big)\\&=\pi\big(\tfrac{243}{5}-243+243\big)\\&=\tfrac{243\pi}{5}\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]
b From \(y=9-x^2\), \(x_2^{\,2}=9-y\). From \(y=9-3x\), \(3x=9-y\), so \(x_1=3-\tfrac13y\) and \(x_1^{\,2}=9-2y+\tfrac19y^2\). The limits are \(y=0\) (at \(B\)) and \(y=9\) (at \(A\)).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{volume}&=\int_0^9\pi\big(x_2^{\,2}-x_1^{\,2}\big)\,dy\\&=\pi\int_0^9\Big((9-y)-\big(9-2y+\tfrac19y^2\big)\Big)\,dy\\&=\pi\int_0^9\big(y-\tfrac19y^2\big)\,dy\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac12y^2-\tfrac{1}{27}y^3\Big]_0^9\\&=\pi\Big(\big(\tfrac12\times9^2-\tfrac{1}{27}\times9^3\big)-0\Big)\\&=\pi\big(\tfrac{81}{2}-27\big)\\&=\tfrac{27\pi}{2}\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]
Your turn. a The parabola is \(y_2=1-x^2\). The line \(AB\) has gradient \(\dfrac{0-1}{1-0}=-1\), so it is \(y_1=1-x\). For \(0\le x\le1\), \(y_2-y_1=x-x^2=x(1-x)\ge0\).
Squaring, \(y_2^{\,2}=\big(1-x^2\big)^2=1-2x^2+x^4\) and \(y_1^{\,2}=(1-x)^2=1-2x+x^2\).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{volume}&=\int_0^1\pi\big(y_2^{\,2}-y_1^{\,2}\big)\,dx\\&=\pi\int_0^1\Big(\big(1-2x^2+x^4\big)-\big(1-2x+x^2\big)\Big)\,dx\\&=\pi\int_0^1\big(x^4-3x^2+2x\big)\,dx\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac15x^5-x^3+x^2\Big]_0^1\\&=\pi\Big(\big(\tfrac15-1+1\big)-0\Big)\\&=\tfrac{\pi}{5}\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]
b From \(y=1-x^2\), \(x_2^{\,2}=1-y\). From \(y=1-x\), \(x_1=1-y\) and \(x_1^{\,2}=1-2y+y^2\). The limits are \(y=0\) and \(y=1\).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{volume}&=\int_0^1\pi\big(x_2^{\,2}-x_1^{\,2}\big)\,dy\\&=\pi\int_0^1\Big((1-y)-\big(1-2y+y^2\big)\Big)\,dy\\&=\pi\int_0^1\big(y-y^2\big)\,dy\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac12y^2-\tfrac13y^3\Big]_0^1\\&=\pi\Big(\big(\tfrac12-\tfrac13\big)-0\Big)\\&=\tfrac{\pi}{6}\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]

Past-Paper Homework - Worked Solutions
PP 1Sydney Boys High School, 2020, Year 12 Term 3 Trial HSC, Mathematics Extension 1, Q8

The rotation is about the \(y\)-axis, so the volume is \(\pi\displaystyle\int_1^2x^2\,dy\), and \(x^2\) must be written in terms of \(y\):

\[y=e^{\frac x2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac x2=\ln y\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=2\ln y\quad\Rightarrow\quad x^2=4(\ln y)^2.\] \[\text{So}\quad V=\pi\int_1^24(\ln y)^2\,dy=4\pi\int_1^2(\ln y)^2\,dy.\]

Options A and B have \(4y\ln y\) and \(4\ln y^2=8\ln y\) in place of \(4(\ln y)^2\). Option C integrates in \(x\) with limits that are values of \(y\). Answer D.

PP 2Caringbah High School, 2021, Year 12 Mathematics Extension 1 HSC Course Assessment Task 4, Q12(a)

Squaring, \(y^2=\dfrac{4}{x-7}\). So

\[\begin{aligned}V&=\pi\int_8^{10}y^2\,dx\\&=\pi\int_8^{10}\frac{4}{x-7}\,dx\\&=4\pi\Big[\ln(x-7)\Big]_8^{10}\\&=4\pi\big(\ln(10-7)-\ln(8-7)\big)\\&=4\pi(\ln3-\ln1)\\&=4\pi\ln3\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]

On \(8\le x\le10\), \(x-7>0\), so no absolute value is needed, and \(\ln1=0\).

PP 3North Sydney Boys High School, 2020, Year 12 Assessment Task 3, Mathematics Extension 1, Q8

Rotation about the \(y\)-axis: write each curve with \(x^2\) as the subject. On the shaded region \(x\ge1\), so for the parabola take the positive square root:

\[\begin{aligned}y=(x-1)^2\ &\Rightarrow\ x-1=\sqrt y\ \Rightarrow\ x=\sqrt y+1\\&\Rightarrow\ x^2=\big(\sqrt y+1\big)^2=y+2\sqrt y+1,\\y=\log_ex\ &\Rightarrow\ x=e^y\ \Rightarrow\ x^2=e^{2y}.\end{aligned}\]

The region runs from \(y=0\) (at \(B\)) to \(y=\ln a\) (at \(A\)). Across it the parabola is further from the \(y\)-axis (for example at \(y=0.25\): \(\sqrt{0.25}+1=1.5\) and \(e^{0.25}\doteq1.28\)), so \(x_2=\sqrt y+1\) and \(x_1=e^y\) in Box 13:

\[V=\pi\int_0^{\ln a}\big(x_2^{\,2}-x_1^{\,2}\big)\,dy=\pi\int_0^{\ln a}\big(y+2\sqrt y+1-e^{2y}\big)\,dy.\]

Option A squares the difference of the radii, which is not the difference of the squares. Option C is a formula for rotation about the \(x\)-axis. Option D does not square the radii. Answer B.

PP 4Killara High School, 2024, Year 12 Examination, Mathematics Extension 1 HSC Trial Examination, Q13(a)

The curve is above the \(x\)-axis on \(\big[0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big]\) and meets it at both ends. Square \(f(x)\), using \(\sin2x=2\sin x\cos x\) and then \(\sin^2\theta=\tfrac12-\tfrac12\cos2\theta\) with \(\theta=2x\):

\[\begin{aligned}\big(f(x)\big)^2&=\sin^2x\cos^2x\\&=\big(\tfrac12\sin2x\big)^2\\&=\tfrac14\sin^22x\\&=\tfrac14\big(\tfrac12-\tfrac12\cos4x\big)\\&=\tfrac18(1-\cos4x).\end{aligned}\] \[\begin{aligned}V&=\pi\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\big(f(x)\big)^2\,dx\\&=\frac{\pi}{8}\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(1-\cos4x)\,dx\\&=\frac{\pi}{8}\Big[x-\tfrac14\sin4x\Big]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\\&=\frac{\pi}{8}\Big(\big(\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\tfrac14\sin2\pi\big)-\big(0-\tfrac14\sin0\big)\Big)\\&=\frac{\pi}{8}\times\frac{\pi}{2}\\&=\frac{\pi^2}{16}\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]
PP 5Blacktown Boys' High School, 2020, HSC Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q14(b)

(i) By the chain rule, with \(u=2\sin x\) and \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=2\cos x\),

\[\frac{d}{dx}\big(e^{2\sin x}\big)=e^{2\sin x}\times2\cos x=2\cos x\,e^{2\sin x}.\]

(ii) Squaring, \(y^2=\big(\sqrt{\cos x}\big)^2\big(e^{\sin x}\big)^2=\cos x\,e^{2\sin x}\). Using part (i), a primitive of \(2\cos x\,e^{2\sin x}\) is \(e^{2\sin x}\):

\[\begin{aligned}V&=\pi\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}y^2\,dx\\&=\pi\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cos x\,e^{2\sin x}\,dx\\&=\frac{\pi}{2}\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}2\cos x\,e^{2\sin x}\,dx\\&=\frac{\pi}{2}\Big[e^{2\sin x}\Big]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\\&=\frac{\pi}{2}\Big(e^{2\sin\frac{\pi}{2}}-e^{2\sin0}\Big)\\&=\frac{\pi}{2}\big(e^2-e^0\big)\\&=\frac{\pi}{2}\big(e^2-1\big)\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]
PP 6Sydney Technical High School, 2021, Extension 1 Mathematics, Trial HSC, Q13(c)

The region is bounded on the right by \(y=x^2\) for \(0\le y\le1\), and by \(y=5-4x^2\) for \(1\le y\le5\) (they meet at \((1,1)\), and \(y=5-4x^2\) meets the \(y\)-axis at 5). Write \(x^2\) as the subject:

\[y=x^2\ \Rightarrow\ x^2=y,\qquad y=5-4x^2\ \Rightarrow\ 4x^2=5-y\ \Rightarrow\ x^2=\frac{5-y}{4}.\] \[\begin{aligned}V&=\pi\int_0^1y\,dy+\pi\int_1^5\frac{5-y}{4}\,dy\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac12y^2\Big]_0^1+\frac{\pi}{4}\Big[5y-\tfrac12y^2\Big]_1^5\\&=\pi\big(\tfrac12-0\big)+\frac{\pi}{4}\Big(\big(25-\tfrac{25}{2}\big)-\big(5-\tfrac12\big)\Big)\\&=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4}\big(\tfrac{25}{2}-\tfrac92\big)\\&=\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{\pi}{4}\times8\\&=\frac{\pi}{2}+2\pi\\&=\frac{5\pi}{2}\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]
PP 7Sydney Girls High School, 2023, Trial Higher School Certificate Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(e)

The line and the parabola meet where \(x^2=\tfrac12x+3\), that is \(2x^2-x-6=0\), \((2x+3)(x-2)=0\). In the first quadrant \(x=2\), so they meet at \((2,4)\). The line meets the \(y\)-axis at \((0,3)\). The region lies between the parabola (below) and the line (above), for \(0\le x\le2\).

As functions of \(y\): the parabola is \(x=\sqrt y\), so \(x^2=y\); the line is \(x=2y-6\). For \(0\le y\le3\), the region runs from the \(y\)-axis to the parabola. For \(3\le y\le4\), it runs from the line \(x_1=2y-6\) to the parabola \(x_2=\sqrt y\). Also \((2y-6)^2=4y^2-24y+36\).

\[\begin{aligned}V&=\pi\int_0^3y\,dy+\pi\int_3^4\Big(y-(2y-6)^2\Big)\,dy\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac12y^2\Big]_0^3+\pi\int_3^4\big(-4y^2+25y-36\big)\,dy\\&=\frac{9\pi}{2}+\pi\Big[-\tfrac43y^3+\tfrac{25}{2}y^2-36y\Big]_3^4\\&=\frac{9\pi}{2}+\pi\Big(\big(-\tfrac{256}{3}+200-144\big)-\big(-36+\tfrac{225}{2}-108\big)\Big)\\&=\frac{9\pi}{2}+\pi\big(-\tfrac{88}{3}+\tfrac{63}{2}\big)\\&=\frac{9\pi}{2}+\frac{13\pi}{6}\\&=\frac{20\pi}{3}\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]

\(\pi\big[\tfrac12y^2\big]_0^3=\pi\big(\tfrac92-0\big)\). At \(y=4\): \(-\tfrac43\times64+\tfrac{25}{2}\times16-36\times4\). At \(y=3\): \(-\tfrac43\times27+\tfrac{25}{2}\times9-36\times3\).

PP 8James Ruse Agricultural High School, 2020, Year 12 Trial HSC Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(a)

(i) \(x^2=8(y-3)\) gives \(y=3+\tfrac18x^2\): a concave-up parabola with vertex \((0,3)\) and axis the \(y\)-axis.

(ii) The line \(y=5\) meets the parabola where \(x^2=8(5-3)=16\), so at \((-4,5)\) and \((4,5)\). The region lies between \(y_2=5\) (above) and \(y_1=3+\tfrac18x^2\) (below) for \(-4\le x\le4\), and both are positive, so Box 13 applies.

{{svg:a12f_jr20}}

Squaring, \(y_1^{\,2}=\big(3+\tfrac18x^2\big)^2=9+\tfrac34x^2+\tfrac{1}{64}x^4\), and \(y_2^{\,2}=25\). The integrand is even, so

\[\begin{aligned}V&=\pi\int_{-4}^4\big(y_2^{\,2}-y_1^{\,2}\big)\,dx\\&=\pi\int_{-4}^4\big(16-\tfrac34x^2-\tfrac{1}{64}x^4\big)\,dx\\&=2\pi\int_0^4\big(16-\tfrac34x^2-\tfrac{1}{64}x^4\big)\,dx\\&=2\pi\Big[16x-\tfrac14x^3-\tfrac{1}{320}x^5\Big]_0^4\\&=2\pi\Big(\big(64-16-\tfrac{1024}{320}\big)-0\Big)\\&=2\pi\big(48-\tfrac{16}{5}\big)\\&=\frac{448\pi}{5}\text{ cubic units}\end{aligned}\]

The same answer comes from the cylinder of radius 5 and length 8 (\(200\pi\)) minus the solid under the parabola: \(200\pi-2\pi\big(\tfrac{16}{5}+16+36\big)=\tfrac{448\pi}{5}\).
