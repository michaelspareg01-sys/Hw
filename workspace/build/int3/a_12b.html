a Let \(y=x\cos^{-1}x\). By the product rule with \(u=x\) and \(v=\cos^{-1}x\), where \(u'=1\) and \(v'=\dfrac{-1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\), \[\frac{dy}{dx}=1\times\cos^{-1}x+x\times\frac{-1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}=\cos^{-1}x-\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}.\] Integrating both sides and rearranging, \[\begin{aligned}\int\cos^{-1}x\,dx-\int\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx&=x\cos^{-1}x\\\int\cos^{-1}x\,dx&=x\cos^{-1}x+\int\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx.\end{aligned}\] By the reverse chain rule with \(u=1-x^2\) and \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=-2x\), \[\begin{aligned}\int\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx&=-\tfrac12\int\big(1-x^2\big)^{-\frac12}(-2x)\,dx\\&=-\tfrac12\times\big(1-x^2\big)^{\frac12}\times2\\&=-\sqrt{1-x^2},\end{aligned}\] so \(\displaystyle\int\cos^{-1}x\,dx=x\cos^{-1}x-\sqrt{1-x^2}+C\).
b The curve is above the \(x\)-axis for \(0\le x\lt1\), so the area is \[\begin{aligned}\int_0^1\cos^{-1}x\,dx&=\Big[x\cos^{-1}x-\sqrt{1-x^2}\,\Big]_0^1\\&=(1\times0-0)-(0-1)\\&=1\text{ square unit.}\end{aligned}\] (At \(x=1\): \(\cos^{-1}1=0\) and \(\sqrt{1-1}=0\). At \(x=0\): \(0\times\cos^{-1}0=0\) and \(\sqrt{1-0}=1\).)

PP 11Sydney Technical High School, 2023, Extension 1 Mathematics, Trial HSC, Q14(b)

The curves meet at \(x=\pm2\): there \(y=4-\dfrac{8}{\sqrt{8-4}}=4-4=0\) and \(y=-\sqrt{4-4}=0\). Split the region by the \(x\)-axis into \(A_1\) (above) and \(A_2\) (below).

\(A_2\) is the lower half of the circle \(x^2+y^2=4\) of radius 2, so \(A_2=\tfrac12\times\pi\times2^2=2\pi\).

For \(A_1\), use symmetry (the integrand is even) and Box 5 with \(a=\sqrt8=2\sqrt2\):

\[\begin{aligned}A_1&=\int_{-2}^{2}\Big(4-\frac{8}{\sqrt{8-x^2}}\Big)dx\\&=2\int_0^2\Big(4-\frac{8}{\sqrt{8-x^2}}\Big)dx\\&=2\Big[4x-8\sin^{-1}\frac{x}{2\sqrt2}\Big]_0^2\\&=2\Big(\big(8-8\sin^{-1}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big)-(0-0)\Big)\\&=2\Big(8-8\times\frac{\pi}{4}\Big)\\&=16-4\pi\end{aligned}\]

So the shaded area is \(A_1+A_2=16-4\pi+2\pi=16-2\pi\) square units.
