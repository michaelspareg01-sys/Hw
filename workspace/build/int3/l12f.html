Lesson 12F

Volumes of rotation

When a region in the coordinate plane is rotated about the \(x\)-axis or the \(y\)-axis, it generates a solid. Slicing the solid into thin circular slices gives a simple integral for its volume. Rotating the region between two curves needs a subtraction of volumes, and the same method finally proves the formulae for the volumes of cones and spheres.
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:rotating a region about the x-axis}}
Solids of revolution

When a region in the coordinate plane is rotated about either the \(x\)-axis or the \(y\)-axis, a solid region in three dimensions is generated, called a solid of revolution. The process is similar to shaping wood on a lathe, or making pottery on a wheel, because such shapes have rotational symmetry and circular cross-sections.

The volumes of such solids can be found using a simple integration formula. The well-known formulae for the volumes of cones and spheres can finally be proven by this method.

Slicing the solid

The first diagram below shows the region under the curve \(y=f(x)\) in the interval \(a\le x\le b\), and the second shows the solid generated when this region is rotated about the \(x\)-axis.

{{svg:c12f_slice}}

Imagine the solid sliced like salami perpendicular to the \(x\)-axis into infinitely many circular slices, each of width \(dx\). One of the slices is shown in the right-hand diagram. The vertical strip in the left-hand diagram is what generates this slice when it is rotated about the \(x\)-axis.

Nowradius of circular slice \(=y\),the height of the strip, soarea of circular slice \(=\pi y^2\),because it is a circle. The slice is a thin cylinder of infinitesimal thickness \(dx\), sovolume of circular slice \(=\pi y^2\,dx\)(area \(\times\) thickness).

To get the total volume, we add all the slices from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\), so   \(\displaystyle\text{volume of solid}=\int_a^b\pi y^2\,dx.\)

11 Volumes of revolution about the \(x\)-axis

The volume of the solid generated when the region between a curve and the \(x\)-axis from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis is

\[\text{volume}=\int_a^b\pi y^2\,dx\ \text{cubic units.}\]
Two remarks
  • If the curve is below the \(x\)-axis, so that \(y\) is negative, then the volume calculated is still positive, because \(y^2\) rather than \(y\) occurs in the formula.
  • Unless other units are specified, ‘cubic units’ (\(u^3\)) should be used, by analogy with the areas of regions discussed in Section 3F.
The steps

(1) Square the equation of the curve, so that \(y^2\) is written in terms of \(x\). (2) Write \(V=\pi\displaystyle\int_a^b y^2\,dx\), with the limits on the \(x\)-axis. (3) Find the primitive. (4) Substitute the upper and lower limits and subtract. (5) Write the units.

Worked example 22Keep for homework
The shaded region cut off the semicircle \(y=\sqrt{16-x^2}\) by the line \(x=2\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. Find the volume generated.
Solution

Squaring, \(\ y^2=16-x^2\).

{{svg:c12f_ex22}}
\[\begin{aligned}\text{so}\quad\text{volume}&=\int_2^4\pi y^2\,dx\\&=\pi\int_2^4\big(16-x^2\big)\,dx\\&=\pi\Big[16x-\tfrac13x^3\Big]_2^4\\&=\pi\Big(\big(16\times4-\tfrac13\times4^3\big)-\big(16\times2-\tfrac13\times2^3\big)\Big)\\&=\pi\big(64-\tfrac{64}{3}-32+\tfrac83\big)\\&=\pi\big(32-\tfrac{56}{3}\big)\\&=\tfrac{40\pi}{3}\text{ cubic units.}\end{aligned}\]
Limits and arithmetic

The region runs from the line \(x=2\) to \(x=4\), where the semicircle meets the \(x\)-axis, so these are the limits. In the last step, \(32-\tfrac{56}{3}=\tfrac{96}{3}-\tfrac{56}{3}=\tfrac{40}{3}\).

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 2:volumes of revolution about the y-axis}}
Exchange \(x\) and \(y\)

To calculate the volume when a region is rotated about the \(y\)-axis, we exchange \(x\) and \(y\).

12 Volumes of revolution about the \(y\)-axis

The volume of the solid generated when the region between a curve and the \(y\)-axis from \(y=a\) to \(y=b\) is rotated about the \(y\)-axis is

\[\text{volume}=\int_a^b\pi x^2\,dy\ \text{cubic units.}\]
Make \(x^2\) the subject

When \(y\) is given as a function of \(x\), the equation will need to be written with \(x^2\) as the subject.

Horizontal slices

The slices are now horizontal circular slices of radius \(x\) and thickness \(dy\), so the limits are values of \(y\). For example, \(y=3x^2\) gives \(x^2=\tfrac13y\).

Find the volume of the solid formed by rotating the region between \(y=x^2\) and the line \(y=4\) about the \(y\)-axis.
Solution

Because \(x^2=y\),

\[\begin{aligned}\text{volume}&=\int_0^4\pi x^2\,dy\\&=\pi\int_0^4y\,dy\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac12y^2\Big]_0^4\\&=\pi\big(\tfrac12\times4^2-\tfrac12\times0^2\big)\\&=8\pi\text{ cubic units.}\end{aligned}\]
{{svg:c12f_ex23}}
Limits

The limits are \(y=0\) (the vertex of the parabola) and \(y=4\) (the line).

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 3:volume by subtraction}}
The region between two curves

When rotating the region between two curves lying above the \(x\)-axis, the two integrals need to be subtracted, as if the outer volume has been formed first, and the inner volume then cut away from it. The two volumes can always be calculated separately and subtracted, but if the two integrals have the same limits of integration, it may be more convenient to combine them.

13 Rotating the region between curves
  • The volume of the solid generated when the region between two curves from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis is \[\text{volume}=\int_a^b\pi\big(y_2^{\,2}-y_1^{\,2}\big)\,dx\qquad(\text{where }y_2>y_1>0).\]
  • Similarly, the volume of the solid generated when the region between two curves from \(y=a\) to \(y=b\) is rotated about the \(y\)-axis is \[\text{volume}=\int_a^b\pi\big(x_2^{\,2}-x_1^{\,2}\big)\,dy\qquad(\text{where }x_2>x_1>0).\]
Square each curve first, then subtract

Each slice is now a washer: a disc of radius \(y_2\) with a hole of radius \(y_1\). Its area is \(\pi y_2^{\,2}-\pi y_1^{\,2}\), which is not \(\pi\big(y_2-y_1\big)^2\).

Note

One would not normally expect the volumes of revolution about the two different axes to be equal. This is because an element of area will generate a larger element of volume if it is moved further away from the axis of rotation.

Worked example 24Keep for homework
The curve \(y=4-x^2\) meets the \(y\)-axis at \(A(0,4)\) and the \(x\)-axis at \(B(2,0)\) and \(C(-2,0)\). Find the volume generated when the region between the curve and the line \(AB\) is rotated:
aabout the \(x\)-axis,babout the \(y\)-axis.
Solution

a The parabola is \(y_2=4-x^2\), and the line \(AB\) is \(y_1=4-2x\),

The line and the outer curve

The line \(AB\) has gradient \(\dfrac{0-4}{2-0}=-2\) and \(y\)-intercept 4. For \(0\le x\le2\), \(y_2-y_1=2x-x^2=x(2-x)\ge0\), so the parabola is the outer curve.

{{svg:c12f_ex24}}
\[\begin{aligned}\text{so}\quad\text{volume}&=\int_0^2\pi\big(y_2^{\,2}-y_1^{\,2}\big)\,dx\\&=\pi\int_0^2\Big(\big(16-8x^2+x^4\big)-\big(16-16x+4x^2\big)\Big)\,dx\\&=\pi\int_0^2\big(x^4-12x^2+16x\big)\,dx\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac15x^5-4x^3+8x^2\Big]_0^2\\&=\pi\Big(\big(\tfrac15\times2^5-4\times2^3+8\times2^2\big)-0\Big)\\&=\pi\big(\tfrac{32}{5}-32+32\big)\\&=\tfrac{32\pi}{5}\text{ cubic units.}\end{aligned}\]
Squaring both

The second line uses

\[\begin{aligned}y_2^{\,2}&=\big(4-x^2\big)^2=16-8x^2+x^4,\\y_1^{\,2}&=(4-2x)^2=16-16x+4x^2.\end{aligned}\]

b The parabola is \(x_2^{\,2}=4-y\), and the line is \(x_1=2-\tfrac12y\),

\[\begin{aligned}\text{so}\quad\text{volume}&=\int_0^4\pi\big(x_2^{\,2}-x_1^{\,2}\big)\,dy\\&=\pi\int_0^4\Big((4-y)-\big(4-2y+\tfrac14y^2\big)\Big)\,dy\\&=\pi\int_0^4\big(-\tfrac14y^2+y\big)\,dy\\&=\pi\Big[-\tfrac{1}{12}y^3+\tfrac12y^2\Big]_0^4\\&=\pi\Big(\big(-\tfrac{1}{12}\times4^3+\tfrac12\times4^2\big)-0\Big)\\&=\pi\big(-\tfrac{16}{3}+8\big)\\&=\tfrac{8\pi}{3}\text{ cubic units.}\end{aligned}\]
Making \(x\) the subject

From \(y=4-x^2\), \(x^2=4-y\). From \(y=4-2x\), \(2x=4-y\), so \(x=2-\tfrac12y\), and \(x_1^{\,2}=\big(2-\tfrac12y\big)^2=4-2y+\tfrac14y^2\). The limits are \(y=0\) (at \(B\)) and \(y=4\) (at \(A\)).

Compare

The two volumes are different, as the note above says they usually will be.

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 4:cones and spheres}}
Proving the formulae

The formulae for the volumes of cones and spheres may have been learnt earlier, but they cannot be proven without arguments involving integration. The proofs of both results are developed in Question 22 of the exercise, and these questions should be carefully worked through.

How the solids are generated
  • A right circular cone of height \(h\) and radius \(r\) is generated by rotating the region under the line \(y=\dfrac{rx}{h}\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=h\) about the \(x\)-axis.
  • A sphere of radius \(r\) is generated by rotating the region under the semicircle \(y=\sqrt{r^2-x^2}\) from \(x=-r\) to \(x=r\) about the \(x\)-axis.
14 Volume of cones and spheres
  • For a cone, \(V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h\).
  • For a sphere, \(V=\tfrac43\pi r^3\).
