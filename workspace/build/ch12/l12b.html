Lesson 12B

Inverse trigonometric functions: integrating

Reverse the standard derivatives to integrate \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}\) and \(\dfrac{1}{a^2+x^2}\), handle the constants, use the reverse chain rule, and integrate the inverse trigonometric functions themselves.
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:the basic standard forms}}
Reversing the derivatives

Differentiation of the inverse trigonometric functions gave purely algebraic functions:

\[\dfrac{d}{dx}\sin^{-1}x=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\dfrac{d}{dx}\tan^{-1}x=\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}.\]

So some purely algebraic functions need the inverse trigonometric functions for their integration, just as \(\int\frac1x\,dx=\log_e|x|\) needed the logarithm.

Standard forms for integration
\[\int\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx=\sin^{-1}x+C\quad\text{OR}\quad\int\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx=-\cos^{-1}x+C\qquad\qquad\int\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx=\tan^{-1}x+C\]

Both forms of the first integral are correct: they differ by a constant, because \(\sin^{-1}x+\cos^{-1}x=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\).

Worked example 1Keep for homework
a Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac12}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx\) using both standard forms.   b Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^{4}\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx\) correct to four significant figures.
Solution
\[\begin{aligned}\int_0^{\frac12}\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}&=\Big[\sin^{-1}x\Big]_0^{\frac12}\\&=\sin^{-1}\tfrac12-\sin^{-1}0=\tfrac{\pi}{6}\end{aligned}\]

OR

\[\begin{aligned}\int_0^{\frac12}\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}&=\Big[-\cos^{-1}x\Big]_0^{\frac12}\\&=-\tfrac{\pi}{3}+\tfrac{\pi}{2}=\tfrac{\pi}{6}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\int_0^{4}\dfrac{dx}{1+x^2}&=\Big[\tan^{-1}x\Big]_0^{4}\\&=\tan^{-1}4-\tan^{-1}0\\&\approx1.326\end{aligned}\]
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 2:standard forms with constants}}
Standard forms with one constant
\[\int\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}\,dx=\sin^{-1}\dfrac{x}{a}+C\quad\text{OR}\quad-\cos^{-1}\dfrac{x}{a}+C\qquad\qquad \int\dfrac{1}{a^2+x^2}\,dx=\dfrac1a\tan^{-1}\dfrac{x}{a}+C\]
Proof (for a > 0)
\[\begin{aligned}\int\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}&=\int\dfrac{dx}{a\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)^2}}\\&=\int\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)^2}}\times\dfrac1a\,dx\\&=\sin^{-1}\dfrac{x}{a}+C\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\int\dfrac{dx}{a^2+x^2}&=\int\dfrac{dx}{a^2\left(1+\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)^2\right)}\\&=\dfrac1a\int\dfrac{1}{1+\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)^2}\times\dfrac1a\,dx\\&=\dfrac1a\tan^{-1}\dfrac{x}{a}+C\end{aligned}\]

In each case let \(u=\tfrac{x}{a}\), so \(\tfrac{du}{dx}=\tfrac1a\), and use the basic standard form in \(u\).

Standard forms with two constants
\[\int\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2x^2}}\,dx=\dfrac1b\sin^{-1}\dfrac{bx}{a}+C\quad\text{OR}\quad-\dfrac1b\cos^{-1}\dfrac{bx}{a}+C\qquad\qquad\int\dfrac{1}{a^2+b^2x^2}\,dx=\dfrac{1}{ab}\tan^{-1}\dfrac{bx}{a}+C\]

If you prefer the one-constant forms, take the coefficient of \(x^2\) out first, as in parts c and d of Worked Example 2.

Reverse chain rule forms
\[\int\dfrac{f'(x)}{\sqrt{1-\big(f(x)\big)^2}}\,dx=\sin^{-1}f(x)+C\quad\text{OR}\quad-\cos^{-1}f(x)+C\qquad\qquad\int\dfrac{f'(x)}{1+\big(f(x)\big)^2}\,dx=\tan^{-1}f(x)+C\]

These are the reversals of the chain-rule forms at the end of Lesson 12A. Look for a function whose derivative also appears in the numerator; adjust constants as needed.

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 4:given a derivative, find an integral}}
Using a product-rule derivative

As always, the result of a differentiation can be used to obtain an integral. In particular, differentiating \(x\sin^{-1}x\) (or \(x\tan^{-1}x\)) with the product rule allows the primitives of the inverse trigonometric functions themselves to be found.

Worked example 4Keep for homework
a Differentiate \(x\sin^{-1}x\), and hence find a primitive of \(\sin^{-1}x\).   b Find the shaded area under the curve \(y=\sin^{-1}x\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=1\).
Solution

a Using the product rule with \(u=x\) and \(v=\sin^{-1}x\):

\[\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(x\sin^{-1}x\right)=\sin^{-1}x+\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}.\]

Hence \(\displaystyle\int\sin^{-1}x\,dx=x\sin^{-1}x-\int\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx\).

By the reverse chain rule (\(u=1-x^2\), \(\tfrac{du}{dx}=-2x\)):

\[-\int\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx=\tfrac12\int(1-x^2)^{-\frac12}(-2x)\,dx=\sqrt{1-x^2},\] \[\text{so}\quad\int\sin^{-1}x\,dx=x\sin^{-1}x+\sqrt{1-x^2}+C.\]
{{svg:asin_area}}
\[\textbf{b}\quad\int_0^1\sin^{-1}x\,dx=\Big[x\sin^{-1}x+\sqrt{1-x^2}\Big]_0^1=\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}+0\right)-(0+1)=\tfrac{\pi}{2}-1\ \text{square units.}\]

Check: the area between \(y=\sin^{-1}x\) and the \(y\)-axis is the same as the area under \(y=\sin x\) from 0 to \(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\), which is 1. Subtracting from the rectangle of area \(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\) gives \(\tfrac{\pi}{2}-1\) again.
