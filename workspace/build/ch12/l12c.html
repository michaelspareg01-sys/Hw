Lesson 12C

Further trigonometric integrals

Integrate \(\sin^2x\) and \(\cos^2x\) using the \(\cos 2\theta\) formulae, integrate the squares of the other trigonometric functions, and use the reverse chain rule and products-to-sums with trigonometric integrands.
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:six standard integrals}}
Six standard integrals
\[\int\cos x\,dx=\sin x+C\qquad\int\sin x\,dx=-\cos x+C\qquad\int\sec^2x\,dx=\tan x+C\] \[{}^*\!\int\mathrm{cosec}^2x\,dx=-\cot x+C\qquad{}^*\!\int\sec x\tan x\,dx=\sec x+C\qquad{}^*\!\int\mathrm{cosec}\,x\cot x\,dx=-\mathrm{cosec}\,x+C\]

The three marked * need not be memorised: they are reversals of derivatives you can check by differentiating.

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 2:the primitives of sin²x and cos²x}}
Use the cos 2θ formulae from Lesson 11A
\[\sin^2\theta=\tfrac12-\tfrac12\cos 2\theta\qquad\qquad\cos^2\theta=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\cos 2\theta\]

It is better to remember these identities than the actual integrals. Replace the square, then integrate term by term.

Integrating the squares of the trigonometric functions
\[\int\sin^2x\,dx=\int\left(\tfrac12-\tfrac12\cos 2x\right)dx\qquad\int\cos^2x\,dx=\int\left(\tfrac12+\tfrac12\cos 2x\right)dx\] \[\int\sec^2x\,dx=\tan x+C\qquad\int\tan^2x\,dx=\int\left(\sec^2x-1\right)dx\] \[{}^*\!\int\mathrm{cosec}^2x\,dx=-\cot x+C\qquad{}^*\!\int\cot^2x\,dx=\int\left(\mathrm{cosec}^2x-1\right)dx\]

Worked example 1Keep for homework
Evaluate: a \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^2x\,dx\)    b \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\frac{2\pi}{3}}\cos^2\tfrac12x\,dx\)
Solution
\[\begin{aligned}\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^2x\,dx&=\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\tfrac12-\tfrac12\cos 2x\right)dx\\&=\Big[\tfrac12x-\tfrac14\sin 2x\Big]_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\\&=\left(\tfrac{\pi}{4}-0\right)-(0-0)\\&=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\int_0^{\frac{2\pi}{3}}\cos^2\tfrac12x\,dx&=\int_0^{\frac{2\pi}{3}}\left(\tfrac12+\tfrac12\cos x\right)dx\\&=\Big[\tfrac12x+\tfrac12\sin x\Big]_0^{\frac{2\pi}{3}}\\&=\left(\tfrac{\pi}{3}+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\right)-(0+0)\\&=\tfrac{\pi}{3}+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}\end{aligned}\]

In part b the angle is \(\tfrac12x\), so \(\cos 2\theta\) becomes \(\cos x\).

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 3:trigonometric integrals using the reverse chain rule}}
Reverse chain rule with trigonometric functions
\[\int\dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\,dx=\log_e|f(x)|+C\qquad\int f'(x)\big(f(x)\big)^n\,dx=\dfrac{\big(f(x)\big)^{n+1}}{n+1}+C\]

Look for \(\sin x\) with \(\cos x\) (one is the derivative of the other, up to sign), or \(\tan x\) with \(\sec^2x\). This prepares for substitution in Lessons 12D and 12E.

Worked example 2Keep for homework
a Integrate \(\cot x\) using the reverse chain rule.   b Find primitives of \(\sin x\cos^4x\) and \(\cos x\sin^nx\).
Solution
\[\textbf{a}\quad\int\cot x\,dx=\int\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}\,dx=\log_e|\sin x|+C\qquad(u=\sin x,\ u'=\cos x)\] \[\textbf{b}\quad\int\sin x\cos^4x\,dx=-\int(-\sin x)\cos^4x\,dx=-\tfrac15\cos^5x+C\qquad(u=\cos x,\ u'=-\sin x)\] \[\int\cos x\sin^nx\,dx=\dfrac{\sin^{n+1}x}{n+1}+C\qquad(u=\sin x,\ u'=\cos x,\ n\ne-1)\]
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 4:products to sums}}
Integrating a product of two trig functions

Use the products-to-sums formulae from the Chapter 11 formula sheet to turn a product such as \(\sin 5x\cos 3x\) into a sum, then integrate each term:

\[\begin{aligned}2\sin A\cos B&=\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)\\ 2\cos A\cos B&=\cos(A+B)+\cos(A-B)\\ -2\sin A\sin B&=\cos(A+B)-\cos(A-B)\end{aligned}\]
