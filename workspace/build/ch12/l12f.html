Lesson 12F

Volumes of rotation

Find the volume of a solid of revolution formed by rotating a region about the \(x\)-axis or the \(y\)-axis, including regions between two curves.
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:rotating a region about the x-axis}}
Where the formula comes from

When a region in the plane is rotated about the \(x\)-axis or the \(y\)-axis, a solid of revolution is formed, like shaping wood on a lathe. Take the region under \(y=f(x)\) for \(a\le x\le b\) and rotate it about the \(x\)-axis.

Imagine the solid sliced perpendicular to the \(x\)-axis into infinitely many circular slices, each of width \(dx\). The vertical strip of height \(y\) generates one slice:

  • radius of circular slice \(=y\), the height of the strip,
  • area of circular slice \(=\pi y^2\), because it is a circle,
  • the slice is a thin cylinder of thickness \(dx\), so its volume is \(\pi y^2\,dx\) (area \(\times\) thickness).

Adding all the slices from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\) gives the total volume.

Volumes of revolution about the x-axis

The volume of the solid generated when the region between a curve and the \(x\)-axis from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis is

\[V=\int_a^b\pi y^2\,dx\ \text{ cubic units.}\]

If the curve is below the \(x\)-axis, the volume is still positive, because \(y^2\) rather than \(y\) occurs in the formula. Unless units are given, use cubic units (u³).

Worked example 1Keep for homework
The shaded region cut off the semicircle \(y=\sqrt{16-x^2}\) by the line \(x=2\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. Find the volume generated.
Solution

Squaring, \(y^2=16-x^2\), so

\[\begin{aligned}V&=\int_2^4\pi y^2\,dx\\&=\pi\int_2^4\left(16-x^2\right)dx\\&=\pi\Big[16x-\tfrac13x^3\Big]_2^4\\&=\pi\left(64-\tfrac{64}{3}-32+\tfrac83\right)\\&=\tfrac{40\pi}{3}\ \text{cubic units.}\end{aligned}\]

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 2:volumes of revolution about the y-axis}}
Volumes of revolution about the y-axis

To rotate about the \(y\)-axis, exchange \(x\) and \(y\). The volume of the solid generated when the region between a curve and the \(y\)-axis from \(y=a\) to \(y=b\) is rotated about the \(y\)-axis is

\[V=\int_a^b\pi x^2\,dy\ \text{ cubic units.}\]

When \(y\) is given as a function of \(x\), rewrite the equation with \(x^2\) as the subject, and use \(y\)-values for the limits.

Worked example 2Keep for homework
Find the volume of the solid formed by rotating the region between \(y=x^2\) and the line \(y=4\) about the \(y\)-axis.
Solution

Because \(x^2=y\),

\[\begin{aligned}V&=\int_0^4\pi x^2\,dy\\&=\pi\int_0^4y\,dy\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac12y^2\Big]_0^4\\&=8\pi\ \text{cubic units.}\end{aligned}\]
{{svg:v_para}}
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 3:volume by subtraction}}
Rotating the region between two curves

When rotating the region between two curves lying above the \(x\)-axis, the two integrals are subtracted, as if the outer volume had been formed first and then the inner volume cut away from it. If the two integrals have the same limits, combine them:

\[V=\int_a^b\pi\left(y_2^2-y_1^2\right)dx\quad(y_2>y_1>0)\qquad\qquad V=\int_a^b\pi\left(x_2^2-x_1^2\right)dy\quad(x_2>x_1>0)\]

Note: \(\pi(y_2^2-y_1^2)\) is not \(\pi(y_2-y_1)^2\). Also, the volumes about the two axes are not normally equal: an element of area generates a larger element of volume when it is further from the axis of rotation.

Worked example 3Keep for homework
The curve \(y=4-x^2\) meets the \(y\)-axis at \(A(0,4)\) and the \(x\)-axis at \(B(2,0)\) and \(C(-2,0)\). Find the volume generated when the region between the curve and the line \(AB\) is rotated a about the \(x\)-axis, b about the \(y\)-axis.
Solution

a The parabola is \(y_2=4-x^2\), and the line \(AB\) is \(y_1=4-2x\).

\[\begin{aligned}V&=\int_0^2\pi\left(y_2^2-y_1^2\right)dx\\&=\pi\int_0^2\Big((16-8x^2+x^4)-(16-16x+4x^2)\Big)dx\\&=\pi\int_0^2\left(x^4-12x^2+16x\right)dx\\&=\pi\Big[\tfrac15x^5-4x^3+8x^2\Big]_0^2\\&=\pi\left(\tfrac{32}{5}-32+32\right)=\tfrac{32\pi}{5}\ \text{u}^3\end{aligned}\]
{{svg:v_between}}

b The parabola is \(x_2^2=4-y\), and the line is \(x_1=2-\tfrac12y\).

\[\begin{aligned}V&=\int_0^4\pi\left(x_2^2-x_1^2\right)dy\\&=\pi\int_0^4\Big((4-y)-\left(4-2y+\tfrac14y^2\right)\Big)dy\\&=\pi\int_0^4\left(-\tfrac14y^2+y\right)dy\\&=\pi\Big[-\tfrac1{12}y^3+\tfrac12y^2\Big]_0^4\\&=\pi\left(-\tfrac{16}{3}+8\right)=\tfrac{8\pi}{3}\ \text{u}^3\end{aligned}\]
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 4:cones and spheres}}
Volume of cones and spheres

For a cone, \(V=\tfrac13\pi r^2h\). For a sphere, \(V=\tfrac43\pi r^3\).

These formulae can only be proven using integration: rotate the line \(y=\tfrac{r}{h}x\) (cone) or the semicircle \(y=\sqrt{r^2-x^2}\) (sphere) about the \(x\)-axis. Homework question 13 works through the proofs.
