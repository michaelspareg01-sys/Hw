Past-Paper Homework - Worked Solutions
PP 1Sydney Technical High School, 2021, Extension 1 Mathematics, Trial HSC, Q2

\(\cos^{-1}\) is defined only for inputs from \(-1\) to \(1\) inclusive, so \(-1\le\dfrac{3x}{2}\le1\), that is, \(-\dfrac23\le x\le\dfrac23\). The end points are included. Answer (A).

PP 2Hurlstone Agricultural High School, 2021, Higher School Certificate Assessment Task 4, Mathematics Extension 1, Q1

The input \(4x\) must satisfy \(-1\le4x\le1\), so \(-\dfrac14\le x\le\dfrac14\). The factor 5 multiplies the output only, so it changes the range, not the domain. Answer C.

PP 3Abbotsleigh, 2023, Higher School Certificate Assessment 4 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q3

Domain: \(-1\le3x\le1\), so \(-\dfrac13\le x\le\dfrac13\). Range: \(-\dfrac\pi2\le\sin^{-1}(3x)\le\dfrac\pi2\), so \(-3\pi\le y\le3\pi\) after multiplying by 6. Answer (B). Options (C) and (D) use the range of \(\cos^{-1}\), and (A) doubles the range once too often.

PP 4Killara High School, 2024, Year 12 Examination, Mathematics Extension 1 HSC Trial Examination, Q1

The value of \(\sin^{-1}\) must lie in \(-\dfrac\pi2\le y\le\dfrac\pi2\), so it is not \(\dfrac{11\pi}{12}\). Since \(\sin(\pi-\theta)=\sin\theta\), \(\sin\dfrac{\pi}{12}=\sin\Big(\pi-\dfrac{11\pi}{12}\Big)=\sin\dfrac{11\pi}{12}=\dfrac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4}\), and \(\dfrac\pi{12}\) is in the range. So \(\sin^{-1}\Big(\dfrac{\sqrt6-\sqrt2}{4}\Big)=\dfrac{\pi}{12}\). Answer A. (Options C and D are negative, but the sine is positive.)

PP 5Ascham School, 2023, Year 12 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q6

B and D are not even defined for \(1<x\le\pi\), because \(\sin^{-1}x\) and \(\cos^{-1}x\) need \(-1\le x\le1\). A fails in the second quadrant: \(\sin^{-1}\Big(\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\Big)=\sin^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=\dfrac\pi3\ne\dfrac{2\pi}3\). C is true: \(\cos^{-1}x\) is the inverse of \(\cos x\) restricted to \(0\le x\le\pi\), which is exactly the given domain. Answer C.

PP 6Sydney Grammar School, 2020, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q9

\(\cos\Big(\alpha-\dfrac\pi2\Big)=\cos\Big(\dfrac\pi2-\alpha\Big)=\sin\alpha\), so \(\sin\alpha=\cos\Big(\alpha-\dfrac\pi2\Big)\). Since \(\dfrac\pi2<\alpha<\pi\), the angle \(\alpha-\dfrac\pi2\) lies between \(0\) and \(\dfrac\pi2\), inside the range \(0\le y\le\pi\) of \(\cos^{-1}\). Hence \(\cos^{-1}(\sin\alpha)=\alpha-\dfrac\pi2\). Answer (C). (A) and (D) lie outside \([0,\pi]\) or give the wrong cosine, and (B) is negative.

PP 7Killara High School, 2024, Year 12 Examination, Mathematics Extension 1 HSC Trial Examination, Q8

For \(\dfrac{3\pi}{2}<\theta<2\pi\), the angle \(2\pi-\theta\) is acute and \(\cos\theta=\cos(2\pi-\theta)\). By the complementary angle identity \(\cos A=\sin\Big(\dfrac\pi2-A\Big)\), \(\cos(2\pi-\theta)=\sin\Big(\dfrac\pi2-2\pi+\theta\Big)=\sin\Big(\theta-\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\Big)\). The angle \(\theta-\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\) lies between \(0\) and \(\dfrac\pi2\), inside the range of \(\sin^{-1}\). Hence \(\sin^{-1}(\cos\theta)=-\dfrac{3\pi}{2}+\theta\). Answer C. Options A and B are negative and below \(-\dfrac\pi2\), and D gives \(\sin(2\pi-\theta)=-\sin\theta\), not \(\cos\theta\).

PP 8Kinross Wolaroi School, 2023, Year 12 Mathematics Extension 1 Trial HSC Examination, Q11(f)

\(\sin^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=\dfrac\pi3\), \(\cos^{-1}\dfrac12=\dfrac\pi3\) and \(\tan^{-1}(-1)=-\dfrac\pi4\). So the value is \(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}-\dfrac\pi3-\dfrac\pi4=\) \(\dfrac{\pi}{12}\).

PP 9Moriah College, 2023, Trial Higher School Certificate Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q9(c)

\(\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\) is in the third quadrant with related angle \(\dfrac\pi3\), so \(\sin\dfrac{4\pi}{3}=-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\). Let \(\alpha=\cos^{-1}\Big(-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\Big)\); then \(\cos\alpha=-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\) with \(0\le\alpha\le\pi\), so \(\alpha\) is in the second quadrant with related angle \(\dfrac\pi6\). Hence \(\cos^{-1}\Big(\sin\dfrac{4\pi}{3}\Big)=\dfrac{5\pi}{6}\).

PP 10Northern Beaches Secondary College Manly Selective Campus, 2020, Year 12 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(d)

Adding the equations, \(4\sin^{-1}x=2\pi\), so \(\sin^{-1}x=\dfrac\pi2\), which is in the range \(\Big[-\dfrac\pi2,\dfrac\pi2\Big]\), and \(x=\sin\dfrac\pi2=1\).

Subtracting, \(6\cos^{-1}y=\pi\), so \(\cos^{-1}y=\dfrac\pi6\), which is in \([0,\pi]\), and \(y=\cos\dfrac\pi6=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\). So \(x=1,\ y=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\).

PP 11Sydney Grammar School, 2020, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q14(a)(i)

Let \(y=\sec x\), \(0<x<\dfrac\pi2\). The inverse is \(x=\sec y\), where \(0<y<\dfrac\pi2\). Then \(\cos y=\dfrac1x\), and because \(0<y<\dfrac\pi2\) lies inside the range \(0\le y\le\pi\) of \(\cos^{-1}\), \(y=\cos^{-1}\Big(\dfrac1x\Big)\). So \(f^{-1}(x)=\cos^{-1}\Big(\dfrac1x\Big)\).

On \(0<x<\dfrac\pi2\), \(\cos x\) takes every value in \(0<\cos x<1\), so \(\sec x\) takes every value greater than 1. The domain of \(f^{-1}(x)\) is the range of \(f(x)\): \(x>1\).
