Lesson 17G

Products to sums

Adding and subtracting the compound-angle formulae turns a product such as \(2\sin7x\cos4x\) into a sum such as \(\sin11x+\sin3x\). This lesson uses the four products-to-sums formulae to rewrite expressions, to find exact values and to prove identities, and then reverses them to solve equations.
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:products to sums}}
Products to sums

This section develops a set of identities that convert the product of two sine or cosine functions to the sum of two sine or cosine functions. For example, we shall show that \(2\sin7x\cos4x=\sin3x+\sin11x\).

The product form on the left is important for purposes such as finding the zeroes of the function. The sum form on the right will be used in Year 12 for integration.

We begin with the four compound-angle formulae involving sine and cosine:

\[\begin{aligned}\sin(A+B)&=\sin A\cos B+\cos A\sin B&&(1A)\\\sin(A-B)&=\sin A\cos B-\cos A\sin B&&(1B)\\\cos(A+B)&=\cos A\cos B-\sin A\sin B&&(1C)\\\cos(A-B)&=\cos A\cos B+\sin A\sin B&&(1D)\end{aligned}\]

Adding and subtracting equations (1A) and (1B), then adding and subtracting equations (1C) and (1D), gives the four products-to-sums formulae:

14 Products to sums
\[\begin{aligned}2\sin A\cos B&=\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)\\2\cos A\sin B&=\sin(A+B)-\sin(A-B)\\2\cos A\cos B&=\cos(A+B)+\cos(A-B)\\-2\sin A\sin B&=\cos(A+B)-\cos(A-B)\end{aligned}\]
Worked example 15Keep for homework
a Convert \(\sin7x\cos4x\) to a sum of sine functions.
b Convert \(\sin7x\sin4x\) to a sum of cosine functions.
Solution

a Using the first identity above, with \(A=7x\) and \(B=4x\),

\[\begin{aligned}\sin7x\cos4x&=\tfrac12\big(\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)\big)\\&=\tfrac12\big(\sin11x+\sin3x\big).\end{aligned}\]

b Using the fourth identity above, with \(A=7x\) and \(B=4x\),

\[\begin{aligned}\sin7x\sin4x&=-\tfrac12\big(\cos(A+B)-\cos(A-B)\big)\\&=-\tfrac12\big(\cos11x-\cos3x\big).\end{aligned}\]
Steps the book skips
  • The products in Example 15 have no factor 2, so each identity in Box 14 is divided by 2 first.
  • When the smaller angle comes first, use \(\sin(-\theta)=-\sin\theta\) and \(\cos(-\theta)=\cos\theta\): for example \(\sin(2x-5x)=-\sin3x\).

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 3:sums to products (enrichment)}}
Sums to products

The previous formulae can be reversed to become formulae for sums to products by making a simple pair of substitutions. These formulae are used in Year 12 when finding the derivative of \(y=\sin x\), but they are outside the course, and have therefore been placed in the Enrichment section of Exercise 17G.

Steps the book skips
  • Box 14 can be read from right to left. To write \(\cos3x+\cos x\) as a product, find \(A\) and \(B\) with \(A+B=3x\) and \(A-B=x\), that is \(A=2x\) and \(B=x\).
  • A sum equal to zero becomes a product equal to zero, so each factor can be set equal to zero. Find every solution of each factor in the domain.
