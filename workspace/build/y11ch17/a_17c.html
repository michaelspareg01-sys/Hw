Past-Paper Homework - Worked Solutions
PP 1Blacktown Boys' High School, 2020, HSC Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q1

\(y=\tan^{-1}x\) has asymptotes \(y=\pm\dfrac\pi2\). Multiplying by 3 stretches the graph vertically by a factor of 3, so the asymptotes become \(y=\pm\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\). Answer D.

PP 2Sydney Boys High School, 2020, Year 12 Term 3 Trial HSC, Mathematics Extension 1, Q3

Domain: \(-1\le x-1\le1\), so \(0\le x\le2\). Range: \(0\le\cos^{-1}(x-1)\le\pi\), so \(0\le y\le2\pi\). Answer D.

PP 3Normanhurst Boys High School, 2022, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Q4

Domain: \(-1\le\dfrac x3\le1\), so \(-3\le x\le3\). Range: \(-\pi\le y\le\pi\), and the function is increasing through the origin (it is \(\sin^{-1}\) stretched by 3 horizontally and by 2 vertically). Only graph (A) has end points \((-3,-\pi)\) and \((3,\pi)\) and is increasing. Answer (A). (B) has the wrong domain and range, (C) is a \(\cos^{-1}\) shape, and (D) is decreasing.

PP 4Gosford High School, 2023, Trial HSC Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q7

\(\sin x\) takes every value from \(-1\) to \(1\), including the end points. \(\tan^{-1}\) is increasing, so \(\tan^{-1}(\sin x)\) takes every value from \(\tan^{-1}(-1)=-\dfrac\pi4\) to \(\tan^{-1}1=\dfrac\pi4\), end points included: the range is \(-\dfrac\pi4\le y\le\dfrac\pi4\). Answer B.

PP 5Hornsby Girls High School, 2023, Year 12 Higher School Certificate Trial Examination Term 3, Mathematics Extension 1, Q10

The graph is defined for all real \(x\), so it is not (B) or (C), whose \(x^{-1}\) is undefined at \(x=0\). The graph reaches \(y=\pi\) (at \(x=-\dfrac\pi2\)), but \(\sin^{-1}\) never exceeds \(\dfrac\pi2\), so it is not (A). For (D), \(\cos^{-1}(\sin0)=\dfrac\pi2\), \(\cos^{-1}\Big(\sin\dfrac\pi2\Big)=\cos^{-1}1=0\) and \(\cos^{-1}\Big(\sin\Big(-\dfrac\pi2\Big)\Big)=\cos^{-1}(-1)=\pi\), matching the graph. Answer (D).

PP 6The King's School, 2021, Year 12 Alternative Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(a)

\(A\) is the left end point of the graph, where the input of \(\cos^{-1}\) is \(-1\): \(2x-3=-1\), so \(x=1\), and \(y=2\cos^{-1}(-1)=2\pi\). Hence \(A=(1,2\pi)\).

PP 7Hornsby Girls High School, 2023, Year 12 Higher School Certificate Trial Examination Term 3, Mathematics Extension 1, Q14(a)

Domain: \(-1\le\dfrac x3\le1\), so \(-3\le x\le3\). Range: \(-\dfrac\pi2\le\sin^{-1}\dfrac x3\le\dfrac\pi2\), so \(-\pi+\dfrac\pi2\le f(x)\le\pi+\dfrac\pi2\), that is, \(-\dfrac\pi2\le y\le\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\).

PP 8North Sydney Girls High School, 2023, HSC Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(e)(i)

The graph has domain \(-3\le x\le3\), so \(-1\le bx\le1\) gives \(b=\dfrac13\). Its range is \(0\le y\le2\), and \(a\cos^{-1}bx\) has range \(0\le y\le a\pi\), so \(a\pi=2\) and \(a=\dfrac2\pi\). Hence \(f(x)=\dfrac2\pi\cos^{-1}\dfrac x3\). (Check: \(f(0)=\dfrac2\pi\times\dfrac\pi2=1\), as on the graph.)

PP 9Normanhurst Boys High School, 2021, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Q12(a)

(i) Domain: \(-1\le\dfrac x6\le1\), so \(-6\le x\le6\). Range: \(0\le\cos^{-1}\dfrac x6\le\pi\), so \(-\dfrac\pi2\le f(x)\le4\pi-\dfrac\pi2\), that is, \(-\dfrac\pi2\le y\le\dfrac{7\pi}{2}\).

(ii) The graph is \(y=\cos^{-1}x\) stretched horizontally by 6 and vertically by 4, then shifted down \(\dfrac\pi2\). It is decreasing, from \(\Big(-6,\dfrac{7\pi}{2}\Big)\) through \(\Big(0,\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\Big)\) to \(\Big(6,-\dfrac\pi2\Big)\), and is vertical at its end points.

PP 10Sydney Boys High School, 2020, Year 12 Term 3 Trial HSC, Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(c)

\(y=\arcsin(3x+1)\) has domain \(-1\le3x+1\le1\), that is, \(-\dfrac23\le x\le0\), and increases from \(\Big(-\dfrac23,-\dfrac\pi2\Big)\) to \(\Big(0,\dfrac\pi2\Big)\). \(y=\arccos x\) has domain \(-1\le x\le1\) and decreases from \((-1,\pi)\) to \((1,0)\), passing through \(\Big(0,\dfrac\pi2\Big)\).

On \(-\dfrac23\le x\le0\) one curve is increasing and the other decreasing, so they meet at most once, and they meet at \(\Big(0,\dfrac\pi2\Big)\), where \(\arcsin1=\arccos0=\dfrac\pi2\). The solution is \(x=0\).

The \(x\)-intercept is \(x=\dfrac14\) and the \(y\)-intercept is \(\sin^{-1}\Big(-\dfrac12\Big)=-\dfrac\pi6\). The curve increases from \(\Big(-\dfrac14,-\dfrac\pi2\Big)\) to \(\Big(\dfrac34,\dfrac\pi2\Big)\).
