Past-Paper Homework - Worked Solutions
PP 1Caringbah High School, 2021, Year 12 Mathematics Extension 1 HSC Course Assessment Task 4, Q4

Both angles are acute, so \(\cos\alpha=\dfrac{15}{17}\) (from the triangle 8, 15, 17) and \(\cos\beta=\dfrac35\) (from 3, 4, 5). Then \(\sin(\alpha+\beta)=\dfrac{8}{17}\times\dfrac35+\dfrac{15}{17}\times\dfrac45=\dfrac{24+60}{85}=\) \(\dfrac{84}{85}\). Answer (B).

PP 2Hornsby Girls High School, 2022, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Higher School Certificate Trial Examination Term 3, Q5

\(\sin(3x+x)-\sin(3x-x)\)
\(=(\sin3x\cos x+\cos3x\sin x)-(\sin3x\cos x-\cos3x\sin x)\)
\(=\) \(2\cos3x\sin x\). Answer (B).

PP 3Abbotsleigh, 2023, Higher School Certificate Assessment 4 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q2

\(\tan\Big(\theta+\dfrac\pi3\Big)=\dfrac{\tan\theta+\sqrt3}{1-\sqrt3\tan\theta}=\dfrac{\frac13+\sqrt3}{1-\frac{\sqrt3}{3}}\). Multiplying top and bottom by 3, this is \(\dfrac{1+3\sqrt3}{3-\sqrt3}\). Answer (C).

PP 4Girraween High School, 2021, Trial Higher School Certificate Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(b)

\(\text{LHS}=\dfrac{\cos2x}{\sin2x}+\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}=\dfrac{\cos2x\sin x+\cos x\sin2x}{\sin2x\sin x}=\dfrac{\sin(2x+x)}{\sin2x\sin x}\), using the expansion of \(\sin(A+B)\) with \(A=2x\), \(B=x\). This is \(\dfrac{\sin3x}{\sin2x\sin x}\) \(=\text{RHS}\).

PP 5North Sydney Boys High School, 2022, HSC Course Assessment Task 3-Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(d)

Let \(A=\tan^{-1}\dfrac43\) and \(B=\cos^{-1}\dfrac{5}{13}\). Both are acute, so from the triangles 3, 4, 5 and 5, 12, 13: \(\sin A=\dfrac45\), \(\cos A=\dfrac35\), \(\cos B=\dfrac{5}{13}\), \(\sin B=\dfrac{12}{13}\).

\(\cos(A-B)=\cos A\cos B+\sin A\sin B=\dfrac35\times\dfrac5{13}+\dfrac45\times\dfrac{12}{13}=\dfrac{15+48}{65}=\) \(\dfrac{63}{65}\).

PP 6North Sydney Boys High School, 2020, Year 12 Assessment Task 3, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(e)

Let \(A=\cos^{-1}\dfrac23\): \(A\) is acute, \(\cos A=\dfrac23\) and \(\sin A=\dfrac{\sqrt5}{3}\). Let \(B=\tan^{-1}\Big(-\dfrac34\Big)\): \(-\dfrac\pi2<B<0\), so \(B\) is in the fourth quadrant, \(\sin B=-\dfrac35\) and \(\cos B=\dfrac45\).

\(\sin(A+B)=\sin A\cos B+\cos A\sin B=\dfrac{\sqrt5}{3}\times\dfrac45+\dfrac23\times\Big(-\dfrac35\Big)=\) \(\dfrac{4\sqrt5-6}{15}\).

PP 7Sydney Boys High School, 2020, Year 12 Term 3 Trial HSC, Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(b)

Since \(\cos45^\circ=\sin45^\circ=\dfrac1{\sqrt2}\), \(\dfrac{\sin x+\cos x}{\sqrt2}=\sin x\cos45^\circ+\cos x\sin45^\circ=\sin(x+45^\circ)\). So \(\sin(x+45^\circ)=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\), where \(45^\circ\le x+45^\circ\le405^\circ\).

Then \(x+45^\circ=60^\circ\) or \(120^\circ\) (the next one, \(420^\circ\), is too large), so \(x=15^\circ\) or \(75^\circ\).

PP 8Abbotsleigh, 2024, Higher School Certificate Assessment 4 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(a)

(i) \(\tan4\theta-\tan\theta=\dfrac{\sin4\theta}{\cos4\theta}-\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\dfrac{\sin4\theta\cos\theta-\cos4\theta\sin\theta}{\cos4\theta\cos\theta}\)
\(=\dfrac{\sin(4\theta-\theta)}{\cos4\theta\cos\theta}=\dfrac{\sin3\theta}{\cos4\theta\cos\theta}\).

(ii) \(\tan4\theta=\tan\theta\) means \(\tan4\theta-\tan\theta=0\), so by (i) \(\sin3\theta=0\), with \(\cos4\theta\) and \(\cos\theta\) not zero. For \(0\le3\theta\le3\pi\), \(3\theta=0,\ \pi,\ 2\pi,\ 3\pi\), so \(\theta=0,\ \dfrac\pi3,\ \dfrac{2\pi}{3},\ \pi\). At each of these \(\cos\theta\ne0\) and \(\cos4\theta\ne0\), so all four are solutions.
