Past-Paper Homework - Worked Solutions
PP 1Normanhurst Boys High School, 2023, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Q2

Let \(y=e^{x+2}\). The inverse relation is \(x=e^{y+2}\). Taking logarithms, \(y+2=\log_ex\), so \(f^{-1}(x)=-2+\log_ex\). Answer (A). Option (B) has the wrong sign on the 2, and options (C) and (D) are exponential functions, which are not inverses of an exponential function.

PP 2Abbotsleigh, 2024, Higher School Certificate Assessment 4 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q5

The domain of \(f^{-1}(x)\) is the range of \(f(x)\). Write \(f(x)=\dfrac{4x}{x-2}=\dfrac{4(x-2)+8}{x-2}=4+\dfrac{8}{x-2}\). The fraction \(\dfrac{8}{x-2}\) takes every real value except 0, so the range of \(f(x)\) is all real \(y\) except \(y=4\). (This hyperbola is one-to-one, so the inverse is a function.) The domain of \(f^{-1}(x)\) is \(x\in(-\infty,4)\cup(4,\infty)\). Answer C.

PP 3Knox Grammar School, 2022, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q3

\(f'(x)=3x^2+1>0\), so \(f(x)\) is increasing. For an increasing function the graphs of \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\) can meet only on \(y=x\): if \(f(a)=b\) and \(f(b)=a\) with \(a<b\), then \(b=f(a)<f(b)=a\), which is impossible. So solve \(f(x)=x\): \(x^3+x+8=x\), \(x^3=-8\), \(x=-2\). The point is \((-2,-2)\). Answer B.

PP 4Normanhurst Boys High School, 2020, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Q11(a)

If \(f^{-1}(5)=a\), then \(f(a)=5\). So \(\sqrt{2a+1}=5\), \(2a+1=25\) and \(a=12\). Hence \(f^{-1}(5)=12\).

PP 5The King's School, 2021, Year 12 Alternative Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(b)

For \(x\ge0\), \(f(0)=1\) and \(f(x)\) decreases towards 0 as \(x\to\infty\), without reaching 0. So \(f(x)\) has domain \(x\ge0\) and range \(0<y\le1\). The domain and range are exchanged for the inverse:

domain of \(f^{-1}(x)\): \(0<x\le1\), range of \(f^{-1}(x)\): \(y\ge0\).

PP 6Killara High School, 2021, Year 12 Mathematics Extension 1 Trial Examination, Q12(e)

\(y=2x^3+1\) is a cubic through \((0,1)\) and \((-1,-1)\), with \(x\)-intercept \(x=-\sqrt[3]{\tfrac12}\doteq-0.79\). It is increasing, so its inverse is a function: \(y=\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{x-1}{2}}\).

Reflect in \(y=x\): the inverse passes through \((1,0)\) (its \(x\)-intercept), \((-1,-1)\) (on \(y=x\)) and has \(y\)-intercept \(-\sqrt[3]{\tfrac12}\doteq-0.79\). It has a vertical tangent at \((1,0)\), the reflection of the horizontal tangent of the cubic at \((0,1)\).

PP 7Hornsby Girls High School, 2020, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Higher School Certificate Trial Examination Term 3, Q11(a)

(i) \(f(x)=x^2-4x+6=(x-2)^2+2\) is a parabola with vertex \((2,2)\). Every value \(y>2\) is taken twice, for example \(f(1)=f(3)=3\), so a horizontal line crosses the graph twice and the inverse is not a function. The domain must be restricted to make \(f(x)\) one-to-one.

(ii) With \(x\le2\): \(f(x)=(x-2)^2+2\). The inverse is \(x=(y-2)^2+2\), where \(y\le2\). Then \((y-2)^2=x-2\), so \(y-2=\pm\sqrt{x-2}\), and \(y\le2\) takes the negative root: \(f^{-1}(x)=2-\sqrt{x-2}\).

(iii) The restricted \(f(x)\) has domain \(x\le2\) and range \(y\ge2\). These are exchanged: domain \(x\ge2\), range \(y\le2\).

(iv) \(y=f(x)\) is only defined for \(x\le2\) and \(y=f^{-1}(x)\) only for \(x\ge2\), so the curves can meet only at \(x=2\). There \(f(2)=2\) and \(f^{-1}(2)=2-0=2\). So \(P=(2,2)\), which lies on \(y=x\). (Solving \(f(x)=x\) gives \(x^2-5x+6=0\), so \(x=2\) or \(3\); only \(x=2\) satisfies \(x\le2\).)
