Inverse functions, exact values and the trigonometric graphs

What this chapter builds on: inverse relations and inverse functions (Sections 5F and 5G), the exact values of the trigonometric functions at the special angles (6A, 6E and 11G), and the graphs of \(y=\sin x\), \(y=\cos x\) and \(y=\tan x\). Lesson 17A extends the work on inverse functions, and Lesson 17B uses all three ideas to define \(\sin^{-1}x\), \(\cos^{-1}x\) and \(\tan^{-1}x\).

Inverse relations (summary of 5F, Boxes 20 to 22)
  • The graph of the inverse relation is obtained by reflecting the original graph in the diagonal line \(y=x\).
  • The domain of the inverse is the range of the relation. The range of the inverse is the domain of the relation.
  • To find the equations and conditions of the inverse relation, write \(x\) for \(y\) and \(y\) for \(x\) every time each variable occurs. This process can be applied to any relation whose equations and conditions are known, whether or not it is a function.
Inverse functions (summary of 5G, Box 24)
  • If a function \(f(x)\) is one-to-one, that is, its inverse relation is also a function, then the inverse function is written as \(f^{-1}(x)\).
  • Each composite of the function and its inverse sends every number for which it is defined back to itself: \[\begin{aligned}f^{-1}\big(f(x)\big)&=x,\ \text{for all }x\text{ in the domain of }f(x)\\f\big(f^{-1}(x)\big)&=x,\ \text{for all }x\text{ in the domain of }f^{-1}(x).\end{aligned}\]
  • To find \(f^{-1}(x)\) from \(f(x)\): convert to \(y=\dots\) notation to generate the inverse relation; write \(y\) for \(x\) and \(x\) for \(y\) in all the equations and conditions; solve for \(y\), and convert back to the notation \(f^{-1}(x)=\dots\)
  • Be careful: \(f^{-1}(x)\) is the inverse function, while \(\big(f(x)\big)^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{f(x)}\) is the reciprocal.

Any angle (summary of 6E, Boxes 14 to 16)
  • ASTC (‘All Stations To Central’): in the first quadrant all six functions are positive, in the second only sine (and cosecant), in the third only tangent (and cotangent), and in the fourth only cosine (and secant).
  • The trigonometric functions of any angle \(\theta\) are the same as the trigonometric functions of its related acute angle, apart from a possible change of sign.
  • Draw a quadrant diagram. Place the ray in the correct quadrant, then use the ASTC rule to work out the sign of the answer. Find the related acute angle, then work out the value of the trigonometric function at this related angle. For example, \(\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{6}=-\cos\dfrac\pi6=-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\).
