Lesson 17F

The t-formulae

The t-formulae write \(\sin\theta\), \(\cos\theta\) and \(\tan\theta\) in terms of the single function \(t=\tan\tfrac12\theta\). This lesson uses them to rewrite expressions, to prove identities, and to solve equations such as \(\cos x-\sin x=1\).
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:the t-formulae}}
The t-formulae

The t-formulae express \(\sin\theta\), \(\cos\theta\) and \(\tan\theta\) as algebraic functions of the single trigonometric function \(\tan\tfrac12\theta\). In the proliferation of trigonometric identities, this can often provide a systematic approach that does not rely on seeing some clever trick.

13 The t-formulae

Let \(t=\tan\tfrac12\theta\). Then

\(\sin\theta=\dfrac{2t}{1+t^2}\qquad\cos\theta=\dfrac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}\qquad\tan\theta=\dfrac{2t}{1-t^2}\)

Proof

Let \(t=\tan\tfrac12\theta\). We seek to express \(\sin\theta\), \(\cos\theta\) and \(\tan\theta\) in terms of \(t\).

First, \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{2\tan\frac12\theta}{1-\tan^2\frac12\theta}=\dfrac{2t}{1-t^2}\), by the double-angle formula.

Secondly, \(\cos\theta=\cos^2\tfrac12\theta-\sin^2\tfrac12\theta\), by the double-angle formula,

\[\begin{aligned}&=\frac{\cos^2\frac12\theta-\sin^2\frac12\theta}{\cos^2\frac12\theta+\sin^2\frac12\theta},\ \text{ by the Pythagorean identity,}\\&=\frac{1-\tan^2\frac12\theta}{1+\tan^2\frac12\theta},\ \text{ dividing through by }\cos^2\tfrac12\theta,\\&=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}.\end{aligned}\]

Thirdly, \(\sin\theta=2\sin\tfrac12\theta\cos\tfrac12\theta\), by the double-angle formula,

\[\begin{aligned}&=\frac{2\sin\frac12\theta\cos\frac12\theta}{\cos^2\frac12\theta+\sin^2\frac12\theta},\ \text{ by the Pythagorean identity,}\\&=\frac{2\tan\frac12\theta}{1+\tan^2\frac12\theta},\ \text{ dividing through by }\cos^2\tfrac12\theta,\\&=\frac{2t}{1+t^2}.\end{aligned}\]

The right-angled triangle below demonstrates the relationship amongst the three formulae when \(\theta\) is acute. The three sides are related by Pythagoras’ theorem, and the algebra rests on the quadratic identity in \(t^2\): \((1-t^2)^2+(2t)^2=(1+t^2)^2\). This diagram may help you to memorise the t-formulae.

Worked example 14Keep for homework
Use the t-formulae to prove:
a \(\dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{1+\cos\theta}\)
b \(\sec2x+\tan2x=\tan\Big(x+\dfrac\pi4\Big)\)
Solution

a Let \(t=\tan\tfrac12x\).

\[\begin{aligned}\text{LHS}&=\Big(1-\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}\Big)\div\frac{2t}{1+t^2}\\&=\frac{1+t^2-1+t^2}{1+t^2}\times\frac{1+t^2}{2t}\\&=\frac{2t^2}{2t}\\&=t\end{aligned}\qquad\begin{aligned}\text{RHS}&=\frac{2t}{1+t^2}\times\Big(1+\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2}\Big)^{-1}\\&=\frac{2t}{1+t^2}\times\frac{1+t^2}{1+t^2+1-t^2}\\&=\frac{2t}{2}\\&=t\\&=\text{LHS}\end{aligned}\]

Notice that we have proven the further identity \(\dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{1+\cos\theta}=\tan\tfrac12\theta\).

b Let \(t=\tan x\).

\[\begin{aligned}\text{LHS}&=\frac{1+t^2}{1-t^2}+\frac{2t}{1-t^2}\\&=\frac{1+2t+t^2}{(1+t)(1-t)}\\&=\frac{(1+t)^2}{(1+t)(1-t)}\\&=\frac{1+t}{1-t}\end{aligned}\qquad\begin{aligned}\text{RHS}&=\frac{\tan x+1}{1-\tan x\times1}\\&=\frac{1+t}{1-t}\\&=\text{LHS}\end{aligned}\]
Steps the book skips
  • In part a, the book writes \(t=\tan\tfrac12x\) although the identity is in \(\theta\): read it as \(t=\tan\tfrac12\theta\).
  • In part b, the t-formulae are used with \(2x\) in place of \(\theta\), so \(t=\tan x\): \(\sec2x=\dfrac{1+t^2}{1-t^2}\) and \(\tan2x=\dfrac{2t}{1-t^2}\).
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 3:solving equations}}
Solving equations with the t-formulae

An equation in \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\) becomes an equation in \(t=\tan\tfrac12x\). Solve it for \(t\), then find \(\tfrac12x\) in the matching domain (for \(0\le x\le2\pi\), \(0\le\tfrac12x\le\pi\)).

Steps the book skips
  • \(t=\tan\tfrac12x\) is undefined when \(\tfrac12x=\tfrac\pi2\), that is, when \(x=\pi\). The equation in \(t\) cannot find \(x=\pi\), so substitute \(x=\pi\) into the original equation to test it.
