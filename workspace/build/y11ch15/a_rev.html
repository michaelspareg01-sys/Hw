1
a \(\dfrac{7!}{5!}=7\times6=42\)
b \({}^9C_3=\dfrac{9\times8\times7}{3\times2\times1}=84\)
c \({}^{12}C_{10}={}^{12}C_{2}=\dfrac{12\times11}{2\times1}=66\)
d \({}^{10}C_0={}^{10}C_{10}=1\) and \({}^{10}C_1={}^{10}C_9=10\), so the sum is \(1+10+10+1=22\).
2
a \({}^nC_1=\dfrac{n!}{1!\,(n-1)!}=n\) and \({}^nC_2=\dfrac{n!}{2!\,(n-2)!}=\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}\)
b \(\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}=45\) gives \(n^2-n-90=0\), so \((n-10)(n+9)=0\). Since \(n>0\), \(n=10\).
3
a \((2x^2)^3=2^3x^6=8x^6\)
b \(\dfrac{9}{x^2}\times x^3=9x\)
c \(\Big(-\dfrac2x\Big)^3=\dfrac{(-2)^3}{x^3}=-\dfrac{8}{x^3}\)
d \(8^{-2/3}=\dfrac{1}{8^{2/3}}=\dfrac{1}{(\sqrt[3]{8})^2}=\dfrac14\)
4
a \((2x-3)^2=4x^2-12x+9\)
b \((1+x)(1+2x+x^2)=1+2x+x^2+x+2x^2+x^3=1+3x+3x^2+x^3\)
c The \(x^2\) terms come from \(1\times x^2\) and \(2x\times(-x)\), so the coefficient is \(1-2=-1\).
5
a \(\dfrac{5!}{3!\,2!}=\dfrac{120}{12}=10\) arrangements
b \(10={}^5C_2\), the number of ways to choose which 2 of the 5 positions are the \(B\)s. Choosing the 3 positions for the \(A\)s gives \({}^5C_3\), which is also 10.
