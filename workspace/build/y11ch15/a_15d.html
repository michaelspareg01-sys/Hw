By the binomial theorem,

\[(1+x)^n=\dbinom n0+\dbinom n1x+\dbinom n2x^2+\cdots+\dbinom nnx^n.\]

Substitute \(x=1\). Because \(1+1=2\),

\[2^n=\dbinom n0+\dbinom n1+\dbinom n2+\cdots+\dbinom nn.\]

PP 3Killara High School, 2021, Year 12 Mathematics Extension 1 Trial Examination, Q13(e)

By the binomial theorem,

\[\begin{aligned}(1+x)^{2n}&={}^{2n}C_0+{}^{2n}C_1\,x+{}^{2n}C_2\,x^2+{}^{2n}C_3\,x^3+\cdots+{}^{2n}C_{2n}\,x^{2n},\\ (1-x)^{2n}&={}^{2n}C_0-{}^{2n}C_1\,x+{}^{2n}C_2\,x^2-{}^{2n}C_3\,x^3+\cdots+{}^{2n}C_{2n}\,x^{2n}.\end{aligned}\]

Adding, the odd powers of \(x\) cancel:

\[(1+x)^{2n}+(1-x)^{2n}=2\big({}^{2n}C_0+{}^{2n}C_2\,x^2+{}^{2n}C_4\,x^4+\cdots+{}^{2n}C_{2n}\,x^{2n}\big).\]

Now put \(n=50\) and \(x=1\). Because \({}^{100}C_0=1\),

\[2^{100}+0^{100}=2\big(1+{}^{100}C_2+{}^{100}C_4+\cdots+{}^{100}C_{100}\big),\]

so

\[1+{}^{100}C_2+{}^{100}C_4+\cdots+{}^{100}C_{100}=\dfrac{2^{100}}{2}=2^{99}.\]
PP 4Blacktown Boys’ High School, 2024, HSC Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(d)

The rows are reversible, and \(2026-1924=102\), so \({}^{2026}C_{1924}={}^{2026}C_{102}\). By the given identity with \(n=2025\) and \(r=101\),

\[{}^{2024}C_{100}+{}^{2024}C_{101}={}^{2025}C_{101}.\]

By the given identity again, with \(n=2026\) and \(r=102\),

\[{}^{2026}C_{102}={}^{2025}C_{101}+{}^{2025}C_{102}.\]

Hence

\[\begin{aligned}{}^{2026}C_{1924}-{}^{2024}C_{100}-{}^{2024}C_{101}&={}^{2026}C_{102}-{}^{2025}C_{101}\\&={}^{2025}C_{102}.\end{aligned}\]

So one possible set is \(p=2025\) and \(q=102\). (Another is \(p=2025\) and \(q=1923\).)

PP 6Kinross Wolaroi School, 2023, Year 12 Mathematics Extension 1 Trial HSC Examination, Q14(d)

Because \((1+x)^3(1+x)^6=(1+x)^9\), the coefficients of \(x^6\) on both sides are equal. On the right, the coefficient of \(x^6\) is \(\dbinom96\). On the left,

\[(1+x)^3(1+x)^6=\Big(\dbinom30+\dbinom31x+\dbinom32x^2+\dbinom33x^3\Big)\Big(\dbinom60+\dbinom61x+\cdots+\dbinom66x^6\Big).\]

A term in \(x^6\) comes from the term in \(x^k\) in the first bracket times the term in \(x^{6-k}\) in the second bracket, for \(k=0,1,2,3\). So the coefficient of \(x^6\) on the left is

\[\dbinom30\dbinom66+\dbinom31\dbinom65+\dbinom32\dbinom64+\dbinom33\dbinom63.\]

Equating the coefficients of \(x^6\) gives the result.

PP 7North Sydney Boys High School, 2023, Year 12, Assessment task 3, Term 2, Mathematics Extension 1, Q14(c)

By the binomial theorem, \(a_k={}^nC_k\) and \(b_k={}^{n+1}C_k\). Expand the right-hand side of \((1+x)^{n+1}=(1+x)^n(1+x)\):

\[\big(a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_nx^n\big)(1+x)=a_0+(a_0+a_1)x+(a_1+a_2)x^2+\cdots+a_nx^{n+1}.\]

Equating the coefficients of \(x^k\), \(a_{k-1}+a_k=b_k\) for \(k=1,2,\dots,n\). Also \(a_0=1\). So the expression is

\[\dfrac{b_1b_2\cdots b_n}{a_0a_1a_2\cdots a_n}=\dfrac{b_1}{a_1}\times\dfrac{b_2}{a_2}\times\cdots\times\dfrac{b_n}{a_n}.\]

By the formula for \({}^nC_r\), for \(k=1,2,\dots,n\),

\[\dfrac{b_k}{a_k}=\dfrac{(n+1)!}{k!\,(n+1-k)!}\times\dfrac{k!\,(n-k)!}{n!}=\dfrac{n+1}{n+1-k}.\]

So the expression is

\[\dfrac{n+1}{n}\times\dfrac{n+1}{n-1}\times\cdots\times\dfrac{n+1}{1}=\dfrac{(n+1)^n}{n!}.\]
