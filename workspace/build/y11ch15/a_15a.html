Past-Paper Homework - Worked Solutions
PP 1The King's School, 2021, Year 12 Alternative Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q2

Take out the factor \(-1\): \(2x-1=-(1-2x)\), and \((-1)^3=-1\), so \((2x-1)^3=-(1-2x)^3\). Row 3 of Pascal's triangle is 1, 3, 3, 1, so

\[(1-2x)^3=1+3(-2x)+3(-2x)^2+(-2x)^3=1-6x+12x^2-8x^3.\]

Hence \((2x-1)^3=8x^3-12x^2+6x-1\). Answer (B). Option (A) has the wrong sign on the \(x^2\) term, (C) has the wrong coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(x\), and (D) forgets that \((2x)^3=8x^3\).

PP 2Ascham School, 2020, Mathematics Extension 1 Trial HSC Examination, Q8

Take out the factor \(-4\): \(3x-4=-4\Big(1-\dfrac{3x}{4}\Big)\), so \((3x-4)^6=(-4)^6\Big(1-\dfrac{3x}{4}\Big)^6=4096\Big(1-\dfrac{3x}{4}\Big)^6\).

Row 6 is 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1. The expansion has 7 terms, so the fourth term is the middle term, whichever end you count from. It is

\[4096\times20\Big(-\dfrac{3x}{4}\Big)^3=4096\times20\times\Big(-\dfrac{27x^3}{64}\Big)=-34560x^3.\]

The coefficient of the fourth term is \(-34560\). Answer (B).

PP 3North Sydney Boys High School, 2020, Year 12 Assessment Task 3, Mathematics Extension 1, Q2

Take out the factor \(x\): \((a+x)^6=(x+a)^6=x^6\Big(1+\dfrac ax\Big)^6\). Row 6 is 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1. Since \(x^3=x^6\times x^{-3}\), we need the term of the bracket in \(x^{-3}\), which is \(20\Big(\dfrac ax\Big)^3=\dfrac{20a^3}{x^3}\). So the term in \(x^3\) is \(x^6\times\dfrac{20a^3}{x^3}=20a^3x^3\).

Then \(20a^3=1280\), so \(a^3=64\) and \(a=4\). Answer (B).

PP 4Girraween High School, 2021, Trial Higher School Certificate Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q2

Take out the factor \(2x\): \((2x+k)^6=(2x)^6\Big(1+\dfrac{k}{2x}\Big)^6=64x^6\Big(1+\dfrac{k}{2x}\Big)^6\). Row 6 is 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1.

Since \(x=x^6\times x^{-5}\), the term in \(x\) comes from \(6\Big(\dfrac{k}{2x}\Big)^5=\dfrac{6k^5}{32x^5}\), so it is \(64x^6\times\dfrac{6k^5}{32x^5}=12k^5x\).

Since \(x^2=x^6\times x^{-4}\), the term in \(x^2\) comes from \(15\Big(\dfrac{k}{2x}\Big)^4=\dfrac{15k^4}{16x^4}\), so it is \(64x^6\times\dfrac{15k^4}{16x^4}=60k^4x^2\).

Putting \(12k^5=60k^4\) gives \(12k^4(k-5)=0\), so \(k=0\) or \(k=5\). None of the options is 0, so \(k=5\). Answer (A).

PP 5Moriah College, 2023, Trial Higher School Certificate Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q9(d)

(i) Row 3 of Pascal's triangle is 1, 3, 3, 1, so \((1+x)^3=1+3x+3x^2+x^3\).

(ii) First \((2x-1)^2=4x^2-4x+1\), so

\[(2x-1)^2(1+x)^3=\big(4x^2-4x+1\big)\big(1+3x+3x^2+x^3\big).\]

The terms in \(x^4\) come from \(4x^2\times3x^2=12x^4\) and \((-4x)\times x^3=-4x^4\). The constant 1 cannot give \(x^4\), because \((1+x)^3\) stops at \(x^3\). So the coefficient of \(x^4\) is \(12-4=8\).

PP 6Sydney Grammar School, 2023, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(a)

Take out the factor \(x\): \(x-\dfrac2x=x\Big(1-\dfrac{2}{x^2}\Big)\), so \(\Big(x-\dfrac2x\Big)^4=x^4\Big(1-\dfrac{2}{x^2}\Big)^4\). Row 4 is 1, 4, 6, 4, 1, so

\[\Big(1-\dfrac{2}{x^2}\Big)^4=1+4\Big(-\dfrac{2}{x^2}\Big)+6\Big(-\dfrac{2}{x^2}\Big)^2+4\Big(-\dfrac{2}{x^2}\Big)^3+\Big(-\dfrac{2}{x^2}\Big)^4=1-\dfrac{8}{x^2}+\dfrac{24}{x^4}-\dfrac{32}{x^6}+\dfrac{16}{x^8}.\]

Multiplying by \(x^4\),

\[\Big(x-\dfrac2x\Big)^4=x^4-8x^2+24-\dfrac{32}{x^2}+\dfrac{16}{x^4}.\]
PP 7Girraween High School, 2023, Year 12 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q16(a)

Take out the factor \(x\): \(x+\dfrac2x=x\Big(1+\dfrac{2}{x^2}\Big)\), so \(\Big(x+\dfrac2x\Big)^6=x^6\Big(1+\dfrac{2}{x^2}\Big)^6\).

Row 6 is 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1. The constant term needs the term of the bracket in \(x^{-6}\). This is the fourth term, \(20\Big(\dfrac{2}{x^2}\Big)^3=\dfrac{160}{x^6}\). Multiplying by \(x^6\), the constant term is \(160\).

PP 8Abbotsleigh, 2021, Higher School Certificate Assessment Task 4, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(c)

Take out the factor \(3x^2\): \(3x^2-2a=3x^2\Big(1-\dfrac{2a}{3x^2}\Big)\), so

\[\big(3x^2-2a\big)^5=\big(3x^2\big)^5\Big(1-\dfrac{2a}{3x^2}\Big)^5=243x^{10}\Big(1-\dfrac{2a}{3x^2}\Big)^5.\]

Row 5 is 1, 5, 10, 10, 5, 1. Since \(x^6=x^{10}\times x^{-4}\), we need the term of the bracket in \(x^{-4}\). That is the third term, \(10\Big(-\dfrac{2a}{3x^2}\Big)^2=\dfrac{40a^2}{9x^4}\). So the term in \(x^6\) is

\[243x^{10}\times\dfrac{40a^2}{9x^4}=1080a^2x^6,\]

and the coefficient of \(x^6\) is \(1080a^2\).

PP 9Normanhurst Boys High School, 2022, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(b)

Take out the factor \(3x\): \(3x-\dfrac{1}{x^2}=3x\Big(1-\dfrac{1}{3x^3}\Big)\), so \(\Big(3x-\dfrac{1}{x^2}\Big)^6=729x^6\Big(1-\dfrac{1}{3x^3}\Big)^6\).

Row 6 is 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1. The constant term needs the term of the bracket in \(x^{-6}\). This is the third term, \(15\Big(-\dfrac{1}{3x^3}\Big)^2=\dfrac{15}{9x^6}\). Multiplying by \(729x^6\), the constant term is

\[729\times\dfrac{15}{9}=1215=\dbinom62 3^4.\]
