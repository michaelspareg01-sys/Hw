PP 1Sydney Grammar School, 2020, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q2

By the binomial theorem, the term in \(x^3\) has \((-3x)^3\), so

\[(2-3x)^8=\cdots+{}^8C_3\times2^5\times(-3x)^3+\cdots\]

Here \({}^8C_3=\dfrac{8\times7\times6}{3\times2\times1}=56\), so the term is

\[56\times32\times(-27x^3)=-48384x^3.\]

The coefficient is \(-48384\). Answer (D). Option (A) is only \({}^8C_3\), and (B) leaves out the factor \(2^5\).

PP 2Sydney Grammar School, 2022, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q7

Write \(\Big(4x^2-\dfrac1x\Big)^9=\big(4x^2-x^{-1}\big)^9\). The first term \(\big(4x^2\big)^9\) has \(x^{18}\). From one term to the next, one factor \(x^2\) is replaced by \(x^{-1},\) so the power of \(x\) goes down by 3. The power \(x^0\) is reached after 6 steps, so the constant term is

\[{}^9C_6\times\big(4x^2\big)^3\times\big(-x^{-1}\big)^6=84\times64x^6\times x^{-6}=5376.\]

Answer (D). Option (C) is only \({}^9C_6\), and (B) has the wrong sign: \(\big(-x^{-1}\big)^6\) is positive.

PP 3Abbotsleigh, 2023, Higher School Certificate Assessment 4 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(c)

By the binomial theorem, the term in \(x^6\) has \((2x)^6\) and \((-p)^3\):

\[(2x-p)^9=\cdots+{}^9C_3\times(2x)^6\times(-p)^3+\cdots\]

So the coefficient of \(x^6\) is \(84\times64\times(-p^3)=-5376p^3\). Then

\[-5376p^3=-672000,\quad\text{so}\quad p^3=125\quad\text{and}\quad p=5.\]
PP 4North Sydney Girls High School, 2023, HSC Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(b)

(i) By the binomial theorem,

\[(2+3x)^6=\cdots+{}^6C_k\times2^{6-k}\times(3x)^k+\cdots\]

so the coefficient of \(x^k\) is \({}^6C_k\times2^{6-k}\times3^k\).

(ii) In \((2+3x)^6\big(1-4x^2\big)\), the term in \(x^3\) comes from \(1\times(\text{term in }x^3)\) and \(-4x^2\times(\text{term in }x)\). By part (i), the coefficients of \(x^3\) and \(x\) in \((2+3x)^6\) are

\[{}^6C_3\times2^3\times3^3=4320\quad\text{and}\quad{}^6C_1\times2^5\times3=576.\]

So the coefficient of \(x^3\) is

\[4320-4\times576=2016.\]

PP 7Hornsby Girls High School, 2023, Year 12 Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(d)

By the binomial theorem, the coefficients of \(x^6\) and \(x^8\) in \((1+2x)^n\) are \({}^nC_6\times2^6\) and \({}^nC_8\times2^8\). So

\[\dfrac{{}^nC_6\times2^6}{{}^nC_8\times2^8}=\dfrac73.\]

Now

\[\dfrac{{}^nC_6}{{}^nC_8}=\dfrac{n!}{6!\,(n-6)!}\times\dfrac{8!\,(n-8)!}{n!}=\dfrac{8\times7}{(n-6)(n-7)},\]

so

\[\begin{aligned}\dfrac{56}{4(n-6)(n-7)}&=\dfrac73\\ (n-6)(n-7)&=6\\n^2-13n+36&=0\\ (n-4)(n-9)&=0\end{aligned}\]

There is a term in \(x^8\), so \(n\ge8\). Hence \(n=9\).

PP 8Normanhurst Boys High School, 2021, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(d)

Write \(\Big(px^3-\dfrac{q}{x^2}\Big)^9=\big(px^3-qx^{-2}\big)^9\). The first term is \(\big(px^3\big)^9=p^9x^{27}\), so the coefficient of \(x^{27}\) is \(p^9\). From one term to the next, one factor \(x^3\) is replaced by \(x^{-2},\) so the power of \(x\) goes down by 5: \(x^{27},\ x^{22},\ x^{17},\ \dots\) The term in \(x^{17}\) is reached after 2 steps:

\[{}^9C_2\times\big(px^3\big)^7\times\big(-qx^{-2}\big)^2=36p^7q^2x^{17}.\]

The coefficients are equal, so \(p^9=36p^7q^2\). Since \(p\ne0\), \(p^2=36q^2\). Since \(p\) and \(q\) are positive, \(p=6q\), that is, \(p-6q=0\).
