Counting and index laws

A short review of what this chapter combines: factorials and \({}^nC_r\) from Chapter 14, the index laws including negative powers, and expanding brackets. Lesson 15C shows that the numbers in a binomial expansion are the \({}^nC_r\) you already know.

Factorials and \({}^nC_r\) (Chapter 14, 14A and 14E)
  • \(n!=n(n-1)(n-2)\times\cdots\times2\times1\), with \(0!=1\) and \(n!=n\times(n-1)!\).
  • The symbol \({}^nC_r\), or \(\dbinom nr\), is spoken “\(n\) choose \(r\)”. It is the number of \(r\)-member subsets of an \(n\)-member set, that is, the number of unordered selections of \(r\) objects from \(n\) objects.
\[{}^nC_r=\dbinom nr=\dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}=\dfrac{n(n-1)\times\cdots\times(n-r+1)}{1\times2\times\cdots\times r}\qquad(r=0,1,\dots,n)\]
  • For example, \({}^8C_5=\dfrac{8!}{3!\,5!}=\dfrac{8\times7\times6}{3\times2\times1}=56\).
  • \({}^nC_r={}^nC_{n-r}\): choosing the \(r\) objects to take is the same as choosing the \(n-r\) to leave. So \({}^nC_0={}^nC_n=1\) and \({}^nC_1={}^nC_{n-1}=n\).
  • The total number of subsets is \(2^n\), so \({}^nC_0+{}^nC_1+\cdots+{}^nC_n=2^n\). Also \({}^nC_r\) counts words: the number of \(n\)-letter words with \(r\) letters alike of one kind and \(n-r\) alike of another is \(\dfrac{n!}{r!\,(n-r)!}={}^nC_r\). For example \(xxyy\) can be arranged in \(\dfrac{4!}{2!\,2!}=6\) ways.
Index laws (Advanced, Year 11), for \(a,b\ne0\)
\[a^m\times a^n=a^{m+n}\qquad a^m\div a^n=a^{m-n}\qquad (a^m)^n=a^{mn}\qquad (ab)^n=a^nb^n\qquad \Big(\dfrac ab\Big)^n=\dfrac{a^n}{b^n}\] \[a^0=1\qquad a^{-n}=\dfrac1{a^n}\qquad a^{1/n}=\sqrt[n]{a}\qquad a^{m/n}=\big(\sqrt[n]{a}\big)^m\]
  • A power applies to the number as well as to the letter: \((2x)^3=8x^3\), not \(2x^3\).
  • Take care with signs: \((-x)^n=x^n\) when \(n\) is even and \((-x)^n=-x^n\) when \(n\) is odd.
  • Write a power of \(x\) in the denominator as a negative power: \(\dfrac3{x^2}=3x^{-2}\), and \(\Big(\dfrac2x\Big)^3=\dfrac8{x^3}=8x^{-3}\).
Expanding brackets (Year 10 and Advanced)
  • A binomial is an expression with two terms, such as \(1-x\) or \(3x^4-\tfrac12x^2\).
  • \((a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2\), \((a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2\) and \((a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2\).
  • To expand a product, multiply every term of one bracket by every term of the other, then collect like terms. A higher power is built one bracket at a time: \((1+x)^3=(1+x)(1+2x+x^2)=1+3x+3x^2+x^3\).
  • The coefficient of a term is the number multiplying it, so the coefficient of \(x^2\) in \(5-3x+7x^2\) is \(7\). The constant term (term independent of \(x\)) has no \(x\) in it.
