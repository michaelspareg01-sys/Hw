Past-Paper Homework - Worked Solutions
PP 1Sydney Grammar School, 2021, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q2

Row 6 of Pascal's triangle is 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1. In \((2x+5)^6\) the powers of \(2x\) go down from 6 while the powers of 5 go up from 0. The term in \(x^2\) contains \((2x)^2\), so it is the fifth term:

\[15\times(2x)^2\times5^4=15\times4x^2\times625=37500x^2.\]

The coefficient is \(37500\). Answer (C). Option (A) is only the number from the triangle, and (D) leaves out the 15.

PP 2Hurlstone Agricultural High School, 2021, Higher School Certificate Assessment Task 4, Mathematics Extension 1, Q2

Row 6 is 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1, so

\[\big(x-\sqrt2\big)^6=x^6+6x^5\big(-\sqrt2\big)+15x^4\big(-\sqrt2\big)^2+\cdots\]

The term in \(x^4\) is \(15x^4\times2=30x^4\), because \(\big(-\sqrt2\big)^2=2\) is positive. The coefficient is \(30\). Answer (B). Option (A) has the wrong sign.

PP 3Hurlstone Agricultural High School, 2023, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q9

Write \(\Big(2x^2+\dfrac1x\Big)^5=\big(2x^2+x^{-1}\big)^5\). Row 5 is 1, 5, 10, 10, 5, 1. The first term \(\big(2x^2\big)^5\) has \(x^{10}\). From one term to the next, one factor \(x^2\) is replaced by \(x^{-1}\), so the power of \(x\) goes down by 3: \(x^{10},\ x^7,\ x^4,\ x,\ \dots\) The term in \(x\) is the fourth term:

\[10\times\big(2x^2\big)^2\times\big(x^{-1}\big)^3=10\times4x^4\times x^{-3}=40x.\]

The coefficient is \(40\). Answer (B).

PP 4Sydney Girls High School, 2023, Trial Higher School Certificate Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(c)

Write \(\Big(x-\dfrac{2}{x^2}\Big)^6=\big(x-2x^{-2}\big)^6\). Row 6 is 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1, so

\[\big(x-2x^{-2}\big)^6=x^6+6x^5\big(-2x^{-2}\big)+15x^4\big(-2x^{-2}\big)^2+20x^3\big(-2x^{-2}\big)^3+\cdots\]

The powers of \(x\) are \(x^6,\ x^3,\ x^0,\ \dots\), going down by 3. So the constant term is the third term:

\[15\times x^4\times4x^{-4}=60.\]
PP 5Knox Grammar School, 2023, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(a)

Write \(\Big(x+\dfrac2x\Big)^{10}=\big(x+2x^{-1}\big)^{10}\). Row 10 is 1, 10, 45, 120, 210, 252, 210, 120, 45, 10, 1. The powers of \(x\) are \(x^{10},\ x^8,\ x^6,\ x^4,\ \dots\), going down by 2, so the term in \(x^4\) is the fourth term:

\[120\times x^7\times\big(2x^{-1}\big)^3=120\times x^7\times8x^{-3}=960x^4.\]

The coefficient of \(x^4\) is \(960\).

PP 6Ascham School, 2023, Year 12 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(b)

Row 5 is 1, 5, 10, 10, 5, 1. Expand only as far as the term in \(x^2\):

\[\begin{aligned}(3-2x)^5&=3^5+5\times3^4\times(-2x)+10\times3^3\times(-2x)^2+\cdots\\&=243-810x+1080x^2-\cdots\end{aligned}\]

In \(\big(2+x^2\big)(3-2x)^5\), the terms in \(x^2\) are \(2\times1080x^2\) and \(x^2\times243\). So the coefficient of \(x^2\) is

\[2\times1080+243=2403.\]
PP 7Pymble Ladies' College, 2023, Mathematics Extension 1 HSC Trial Examination Term 3, Q13(a)

Write \(\Big(2x+\dfrac{5}{x^2}\Big)^6=\big(2x+5x^{-2}\big)^6\). Row 6 is 1, 6, 15, 20, 15, 6, 1, so

\[\begin{aligned}\big(2x+5x^{-2}\big)^6&=(2x)^6+6(2x)^5\big(5x^{-2}\big)+15(2x)^4\big(5x^{-2}\big)^2+\cdots\\&=64x^6+960x^3+6000+\cdots\end{aligned}\]

The later terms have negative powers of \(x\). The constant 2 needs the constant term 6000, and \(-\dfrac{3}{x^3}=-3x^{-3}\) needs the term \(960x^3\). So the term independent of \(x\) is

\[2\times6000-3x^{-3}\times960x^3=12000-2880=9120.\]
PP 8St Aloysius' College, 2024, Trial Higher School Certificate Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q14(c)

Row 12 is 1, 12, 66, 220, 495, 792, 924, 792, 495, 220, 66, 12, 1. In \((a-bx)^{12}\) the powers of \(a\) go down from 12 while the powers of \(-bx\) go up from 0. So the terms in \(x^7\) and \(x^8\) are the eighth and ninth terms:

\[792a^5(-bx)^7=-792a^5b^7x^7\quad\text{and}\quad495a^4(-bx)^8=495a^4b^8x^8.\]

In \((1+x)(a-bx)^{12}\), the term in \(x^8\) comes from \(1\times495a^4b^8x^8\) and \(x\times\big(-792a^5b^7x^7\big)\). So the coefficient of \(x^8\) is

\[495a^4b^8-792a^5b^7=99a^4b^7(5b-8a).\]

This is zero and \(a,b\ne0\), so \(8a=5b\). Hence \(\dfrac ab=\dfrac58\).

PP 9James Ruse Agricultural High School, 2023, Year 12 Trial HSC Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q15(b)

Write \(\Big(\dfrac{x^3}{2}+\dfrac ax\Big)^8=\Big(\dfrac{x^3}{2}+ax^{-1}\Big)^8\). Row 8 is 1, 8, 28, 56, 70, 56, 28, 8, 1. The first term \(\Big(\dfrac{x^3}{2}\Big)^8\) has \(x^{24}\). From one term to the next, one factor \(x^3\) is replaced by \(x^{-1}\), so the power of \(x\) goes down by 4. The power \(x^0\) is reached after 6 steps, in the seventh term:

\[28\times\Big(\dfrac{x^3}{2}\Big)^2\times\big(ax^{-1}\big)^6=28\times\dfrac{x^6}{4}\times a^6x^{-6}=7a^6.\]

Then \(7a^6=5103\), so \(a^6=729=3^6\), and \(a=3\) or \(a=-3\).
