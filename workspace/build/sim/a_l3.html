Tutor demo. The pair of equations is the answer; the solution is shown so that you can check your pair.
a Let \(x\) be the larger number and \(y\) the smaller number. \(x+y=50\) and \(x-y=14\).
Adding: \(2x=64\), so \(x=32\) and \(y=50-32=18\). Check: \(32+18=50\) and \(32-18=14\). The numbers are 32 and 18.
b Let \(m\) be the number of muffins sold and \(s\) the number of scones sold. \(m+s=20\) and \(4m+3s=71\).
From the first, \(s=20-m\); then \(4m+3(20-m)=71\), so \(m+60=71\) and \(m=11\); \(s=20-11=9\). Check: \(11+9=20\) and \(4(11)+3(9)=44+27=71\). It sold 11 muffins and 9 scones.
c Let \(a\) be Ali’s age now and \(b\) be Ben’s age now, in years. \(a=b+4\) and \(a-5=2(b-5)\).
Substituting: \(b+4-5=2b-10\), so \(b-1=2b-10\) and \(b=9\); \(a=9+4=13\). Check: \(13=9+4\) and \(13-5=8=2(9-5)\). Ali is 13 and Ben is 9.

Check in (1): \(22+9=31\). Check in (2): \(22+4=26=2(9+4)\).

PP 7Sydney Grammar School, 2025, Annual Examination, Form III Mathematics, Q7(c)

\(x\) km/h is the current speed and \(t\) hours the current journey time, as given. The distance is \(xt\) km for every journey. Twenty-four minutes is \(\tfrac{24}{60}=0.4\) hours, so \((x+1)(t-0.4)=xt\) and \((x+6)(t-2)=xt\).

Expanding: \(xt-0.4x+t-0.4=xt\) and \(xt-2x+6t-12=xt\). The \(xt\) terms cancel, leaving the pair \(t-0.4x=0.4\) (1) and \(6t-2x=12\) (2).

Taking \(6\times(1)\): \(6t-2.4x=2.4\). Subtracting from (2): \(0.4x=9.6\), so \(x=24\). In (1): \(t=0.4+0.4(24)=10\). So \(x=24\) and \(t=10\).
Check in (1): \(10-0.4(24)=10-9.6=0.4\). Check in (2): \(6(10)-2(24)=60-48=12\).

Distance \(=24\times10=240\) km. Also \(25\times9.6=240\) and \(30\times8=240\). The train line from Bathurst to Sydney is 240 km long.
