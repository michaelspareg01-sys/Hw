Lesson 3

Problem solving

Many problems describe two unknown quantities and give two separate facts about them. Each fact becomes an equation, so the problem becomes a pair of simultaneous equations, solved by substitution or elimination. This lesson covers the whole process: defining the pronumerals, forming the two equations, solving them, and answering the question in words. The steps come from Year 8 section 2H Applications; the problems from Year 11 Advanced Exercise 1G, the worksheets and school examinations. The chapter review is at the end.
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:defining the pronumerals and forming two equations}}
Applications (Year 8, 2H)

Although knowing how to solve equations is useful, it is important to be able to recognise when real-world situations can be thought of as equations. This is the case whenever it is known that two values are equal.

In this case, an equation can be constructed and solved. It is important to translate this solution into a meaningful answer within the real-world context.

Key ideas (Year 8, 2H)
  • An equation can be used to describe any situation in which two values are equal.
  • To solve a problem follow these steps.
1 Define pronumerals to stand for unknown numbers.
1 Let \(x=\) Jerome’s age.
\(\therefore x+4=\) his sister’s age
2 Write an equation to describe the problem.
2 \(x+x+4=32\)
3 Solve the equation by inspection or systematically.
3 \(\begin{array}{rcll}2x+4&=&32&\\2x&=&28&\quad(-4)\\x&=&14&\quad(\div 2)\end{array}\)
4 Make sure you answer the original question, including the correct units (e.g. dollars, years, cm).
4 Jerome is 14 years old and his sister is 18.
5 Check that your answer is reasonable.
Two unknowns, two equations

In this lesson each problem has two unknown quantities. Give each one its own pronumeral, and say exactly what it stands for, with its units. Each separate fact in the problem gives one equation, so a problem with two unknowns needs two facts. The two equations are then solved by the methods of Lessons 1 and 2.

Worked exampleYear 8 Example 14 (2H) Solving problems with two related unknowns: keep for homework

Jane and Luke have a combined age of 60. Given that Jane is twice as old as Luke, find the ages of Luke and Jane.

Solution
Explanation
Let \(l=\) Luke’s age.
Define a pronumeral for the unknown value. Once Luke’s age is found, we can double it to find Jane’s age.
\(l+2l=60\)
Write an equation to describe the situation. Note that Jane’s age is \(2l\) because she is twice as old as Luke.
\(\begin{array}{rcll}3l&=&60&\\l&=&20&\quad(\div 3)\end{array}\)
Solve the equation by first combining like terms.
Luke is 20 years old and Jane is 40 years old.
Answer the original question.
Steps the book skips: Example 14 with two pronumerals

The same problem can be written as a pair of simultaneous equations.

Let \(l\) be Luke’s age in years and \(j\) be Jane’s age in years.
\(l+j=60\) (1) and \(j=2l\) (2)
Substituting (2) into (1): \(l+2l=60\), so \(3l=60\) and \(l=20\). Then \(j=2(20)=40\). So \(l=20\) and \(j=40\).
Check in (1): \(20+40=60\). Check in (2): \(40=2(20)\).

Luke is 20 years old and Jane is 40 years old. This agrees with the book.

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 2:solving and answering in words}}
Answering the question

Steps 3 to 5 of the Key ideas still apply with two equations: solve the pair, then write a sentence that answers the question that was asked, with its units. Check the answer against the words of the problem, not only against your equations: an error in forming the equations is not found by substituting into them.
