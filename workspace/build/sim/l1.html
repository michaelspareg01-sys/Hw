Lesson 1

The graphical method and substitution

Two straight lines that cross meet at exactly one point. The coordinates of that point make both equations true, so they are the solution of the pair of equations. This lesson finds that point in two ways: by drawing both lines and reading off where they meet, and by algebra, using the substitution method. The teaching follows Year 11 Advanced sections 3C Review of linear graphs and 1G Solving simultaneous equations, with the Key ideas of Year 8 section 7G.
{{sec:teal:key:Concept 1:a solution of a pair of equations}}
Key ideas (Year 8, 7G)
  • A point \((x,y)\) is a solution to the equation for a line if its coordinates make the equation true.
    • An equation is true when LHS \(=\) RHS after the coordinates are substituted.
    • Every point on a straight line is a solution to the equation for that line.
    • Every point that is not on a straight line is not a solution to the equation for that line.
  • The point of intersection of two straight lines is the only solution that satisfies both equations.
    • The point of intersection is the shared point where two straight lines cross each other.
    • This is the only point with coordinates that makes both equations true.
      For example, \((1,3)\) is the only point that makes both \(y=6-3x\) and \(y=2x+1\) true.
      Substituting \((1,3)\):
\[\begin{aligned}y&=6-3x\\3&=6-3\times1\\3&=3\ \text{(True)}\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}y&=2x+1\\3&=2\times1+1\\3&=3\ \text{(True)}\end{aligned}\]
{{svg:l1_7g}}
Simultaneous equations

Two equations that must both be true at the same time are called simultaneous equations. To solve them means to find the values of \(x\) and \(y\) that make both equations true. When the two equations are straight lines that cross, those values are the coordinates of the point of intersection. A point that lies on only one of the lines is not a solution of the pair.

Sketching linear functions (Year 11 Advanced, 3C)

When all three terms of the equation are non-zero, the easiest way to sketch a linear function is to find the two intercepts with the axes.

Sketching a linear function whose equation has three non-zero terms

  • Find the \(x\)-intercept by putting \(y=0\).
  • Find the \(y\)-intercept by putting \(x=0\).
Reading the graph

Draw both lines on the same number plane, using the same scale on both axes, and mark the point where they cross. A graph drawn by hand gives the coordinates of this point only as accurately as the drawing, so the book calls the answer an estimate. Substituting the coordinates into both equations shows whether the estimate is exact.

Solving simultaneous equations (Year 11 Advanced, 1G)

There are two algebraic approaches to solving simultaneous equations: substitution and elimination. They can be applied to both linear and non-linear simultaneous equations.

Solution by substitution

This method can be applied whenever one of the equations can be solved for one of the variables.

Solving simultaneous equations by substitution

  • Solve one of the equations for one of the variables.
  • Then substitute it into the other equation.
Setting out

Number the equations (1) and (2), as the book does, and say which equation is substituted into which. Substituting gives one equation with one unknown, which is solved as in the revision pages. Finish by checking the answer in both of the original equations.

Worked exampleYear 11 Advanced Example 21 (1G): keep for homework

Solve each pair of simultaneous equations by substitution.

a\(\begin{aligned}3x-2y&=29\quad(1)\\4x+y&=24\quad(2)\end{aligned}\)

Solution
a Solving (2) for \(y\),\(y=24-4x.\)(2A)  Substituting (2A) into (1),\(3x-2(24-4x)=29\) \(x=7.\)  Substituting \(x=7\) into (1),\(21-2y=29\) \(y=-4.\)

 Hence \(x=7\) and \(y=-4\). (This should be checked in the original equations.)

Steps the book skips: Example 21
Expanding the brackets, \(3x-2(24-4x)=29\) becomes \(3x-48+8x=29\), so \(11x-48=29\), \(11x=77\) and \(x=7\). Then \(21-2y=29\) gives \(-2y=8\), so \(y=-4\).
Check in (1): \(3(7)-2(-4)=21+8=29\). Check in (2): \(4(7)+(-4)=28-4=24\). Both equations are true.

Part b of the book’s example is a pair with \(x^2\) in it, which is not linear, so it is left out of this pack.

Solve the simultaneous equations in Example 8, and check that the solution agrees with the estimate from the graph.

Solution
The equations are\(x+2y-6=0\)(1) \(x-y-3=0\)(2) Subtracting (2) from (1),\(3y-3=0\) \(y=1\) Substituting into (1),\(x+2-6=0\) \(x=4\)Check this by substitution into (2).

Steps the book skips: Using simultaneous equations

Subtracting one equation from the other is the elimination method of Lesson 2. The substitution method of this lesson gives the same point.

Equation (2) solved for \(x\) is \(x=y+3\). Substituting into (1), \((y+3)+2y-6=0\), so \(3y-3=0\) and \(y=1\). Then \(x=1+3=4\).
Check in (2): \(4-1-3=0\). Check in (1): \(4+2(1)-6=0\). The lines meet at \((4,1)\).
