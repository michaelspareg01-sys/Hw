Past-Paper Homework - Worked Solutions
PP 1The King’s School, 2021, Year 12 Alternative Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q8

The marbles of one colour look alike, but they are still 14 different marbles, so every unordered selection of 3 of them is equally likely. There are \({}^{14}\mathrm{C}_{3}=364\) such selections.

For exactly two blue marbles, choose 2 of the 5 blue marbles in \({}^{5}\mathrm{C}_{2}=10\) ways, and the third marble from the \(6+3=9\) marbles that are not blue in \({}^{9}\mathrm{C}_{1}=9\) ways. Hence

\[P(\text{exactly two blue})=\dfrac{{}^{5}\mathrm{C}_{2}\times{}^{9}\mathrm{C}_{1}}{{}^{14}\mathrm{C}_{3}}=\dfrac{90}{364}=\dfrac{45}{182}.\]

Answer (B). Option (A) forgets to choose the third marble, which must not be blue.

PP 2Killara High School, 2023, Year 12 Examination, Mathematics Extension 1 Trial, Q6

The draw is an unordered selection of 6 of the 30 numbers, so there are \({}^{30}\mathrm{C}_{6}=593\,775\) equally likely draws.

A draw matches exactly \(k\) of the player’s numbers when it contains \(k\) of the player’s 6 numbers and \(6-k\) of the other 24 numbers. ‘At least 4’ means 4, 5 or 6 matches, so

\[\begin{aligned}\text{number of favourable draws}&={}^{6}\mathrm{C}_{4}\times{}^{24}\mathrm{C}_{2}+{}^{6}\mathrm{C}_{5}\times{}^{24}\mathrm{C}_{1}+{}^{6}\mathrm{C}_{6}\times{}^{24}\mathrm{C}_{0}\\ &=15\times276+6\times24+1\\ &=4285.\end{aligned}\] \[\text{Hence}\qquad P(\text{at least 4 matches})=\dfrac{4285}{593\,775}\approx0.0072.\]

Answer B. To three decimal places the probability is 0.007.

PP 3Normanhurst Boys High School, 2020, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Q11(f)

A team is an unordered selection. The sample space is the set of all teams of 13 chosen from the 25 players, so it has \({}^{25}\mathrm{C}_{13}=5\,200\,300\) members. A team of only female players is a choice of 13 of the 20 female players, in \({}^{20}\mathrm{C}_{13}=77\,520\) ways. Hence

\[P(\text{only female players})=\dfrac{{}^{20}\mathrm{C}_{13}}{{}^{25}\mathrm{C}_{13}}=\dfrac{77\,520}{5\,200\,300}=\dfrac{12}{805}.\]
PP 4Sydney Technical High School, 2021, Extension 1 Mathematics, Trial HSC, Q11(c)

The two cards form an unordered hand, so the sample space has \({}^{52}\mathrm{C}_{2}=1326\) members. ‘At least 1 heart’ is a warning, so consider the complement, ‘no heart’. Then both cards come from the 39 cards that are not hearts, in \({}^{39}\mathrm{C}_{2}=741\) ways. Hence

\[P(\text{at least 1 heart})=1-\dfrac{{}^{39}\mathrm{C}_{2}}{{}^{52}\mathrm{C}_{2}}=1-\dfrac{741}{1326}=\dfrac{15}{34}.\]

Check with ordered selections: \(1-\dfrac{39\times38}{52\times51}=\dfrac{15}{34}\).

PP 5Hornsby Girls High School, 2022, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Higher School Certificate Trial Examination Term 3, Q12(c)

There are \(10+8=18\) students, so there are \({}^{18}\mathrm{C}_{5}=8568\) possible committees of 5. Choose the 3 girls in \({}^{10}\mathrm{C}_{3}=120\) ways and, separately, the 2 boys in \({}^{8}\mathrm{C}_{2}=28\) ways. Hence

\[P(\text{3 girls and 2 boys})=\dfrac{{}^{10}\mathrm{C}_{3}\times{}^{8}\mathrm{C}_{2}}{{}^{18}\mathrm{C}_{5}}=\dfrac{120\times28}{8568}=\dfrac{20}{51}.\]
PP 6Normanhurst Boys High School, 2022, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Q11(c)(i)(ii)

(i) A committee is an unordered selection of 7 of the 20 prefects:

\[\text{number of committees}={}^{20}\mathrm{C}_{7}=77\,520\]

(ii) Jun is on the committee and Alex is not, so the other 6 members are chosen from the 18 prefects other than Jun and Alex, in \({}^{18}\mathrm{C}_{6}=18\,564\) ways. Hence

\[P(\text{Jun but not Alex})=\dfrac{{}^{18}\mathrm{C}_{6}}{{}^{20}\mathrm{C}_{7}}=\dfrac{18\,564}{77\,520}=\dfrac{91}{380}.\]
PP 7Girraween High School, 2023, Year 12 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(c)(i)(ii)

The division is fixed once the 20 students in class A are chosen, because class B is the other 20. So there are \({}^{40}\mathrm{C}_{20}\) equally likely divisions.

(i) If all four friends are in class A, the other 16 students in A are chosen from the other 36 students, in \({}^{36}\mathrm{C}_{16}\) ways. If all four are in class B, class A is 20 of the other 36 students, in \({}^{36}\mathrm{C}_{20}={}^{36}\mathrm{C}_{16}\) ways. Since

\[\dfrac{{}^{36}\mathrm{C}_{16}}{{}^{40}\mathrm{C}_{20}}=\dfrac{36!}{16!\times20!}\times\dfrac{20!\times20!}{40!}=\dfrac{20\times19\times18\times17}{40\times39\times38\times37}=\dfrac{51}{962},\] \[P(\text{all four in the same class})=\dfrac{2\times{}^{36}\mathrm{C}_{16}}{{}^{40}\mathrm{C}_{20}}=\dfrac{51}{481}\approx0.106.\]

(ii) For exactly three friends in class A, choose the three in \({}^{4}\mathrm{C}_{3}=4\) ways. The fourth friend is in class B, so the other 17 students in A are chosen from the other 36 students, in \({}^{36}\mathrm{C}_{17}\) ways. Exactly three in class B is counted in the same way. Since

\[\dfrac{{}^{36}\mathrm{C}_{17}}{{}^{40}\mathrm{C}_{20}}=\dfrac{36!}{17!\times19!}\times\dfrac{20!\times20!}{40!}=\dfrac{20\times19\times18\times20}{40\times39\times38\times37}=\dfrac{30}{481},\] \[P(\text{exactly three in the same class})=\dfrac{2\times{}^{4}\mathrm{C}_{3}\times{}^{36}\mathrm{C}_{17}}{{}^{40}\mathrm{C}_{20}}=\dfrac{240}{481}\approx0.499.\]
