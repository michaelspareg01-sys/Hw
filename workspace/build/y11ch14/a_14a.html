PP 1Cherrybrook Technology High School, 2020, Year 12 AP4, Mathematics Extension 1, Q1

Fill the four places of the PIN one at a time. The first digit can be any of the 10 digits. No digit can be repeated, so there are 9 choices left for the second digit, 8 for the third and 7 for the fourth.

{{svg:pin4}} \[\text{number of PINs}=10\times9\times8\times7=\dfrac{10!}{6!}=5040.\]

Answer (D). Option (C), \(10\,000=10^4\), would allow repeated digits. Option (B), 210, is \(5040\div4!\): it counts the sets of four digits and ignores their order, but 1234 and 4321 are different PINs.

PP 3Normanhurst Boys High School, 2022, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Q11(a)(i)

Unrolling, \((n+2)!=(n+2)(n+1)!\), so \(\dfrac{1}{(n+1)!}=\dfrac{n+2}{(n+2)!}\). Hence

\[\begin{aligned}\text{LHS}&=\dfrac{n+2}{(n+2)!}-\dfrac{n+1}{(n+2)!}\\ &=\dfrac{(n+2)-(n+1)}{(n+2)!}\\ &=\dfrac{1}{(n+2)!}=\text{RHS}.\end{aligned}\]
PP 4Normanhurst Boys High School, 2021, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Q11(e)(i)

Unrolling, \((n+3)!=(n+3)(n+2)!\), so \(\dfrac{1}{(n+2)!}=\dfrac{n+3}{(n+3)!}\). Hence

\[\begin{aligned}\text{LHS}&=\dfrac{n+3}{(n+3)!}-\dfrac{n+1}{(n+3)!}\\ &=\dfrac{(n+3)-(n+1)}{(n+3)!}\\ &=\dfrac{2}{(n+3)!}=\text{RHS}.\end{aligned}\]
PP 5Sydney Boys High School, 2020, Year 12 Term 3 Trial HSC, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(c)

Unrolling, \((n+2)!=(n+2)(n+1)\,n!\), so the equation is \((n+2)(n+1)\,n!=72\,n!\). Since \(n!\ne0\), divide both sides by \(n!\):

\[\begin{aligned}(n+2)(n+1)&=72\\ n^2+3n+2&=72\\ n^2+3n-70&=0\\ (n-7)(n+10)&=0,\end{aligned}\]

so \(n=7\) or \(n=-10\). But \(n!\) is defined only for whole numbers \(n\ge0\), so \(n=-10\) is impossible. Hence \(n=7\).

Check: \(9!=362\,880\) and \(72\times7!=72\times5040=362\,880\).
