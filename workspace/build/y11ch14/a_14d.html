PP 1Knox Grammar School, 2022, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q7

EXCELLENT has 9 letters: three Es, two Ls, and X, C, N, T once each. By Box 10,

\[\text{number of arrangements}=\dfrac{9!}{3!\times2!}=30\,240.\]

Answer B. Option D, \(362\,880=9!\), treats all nine letters as different. Option C, \(60\,480=\dfrac{9!}{3!}\), allows for the repeated Es but not for the repeated Ls.

PP 2Sydney Boys High School, 2020, Year 12 Term 3 Trial HSC, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(a)

Only the colours matter, so the row of chairs is a six-letter word using the colours black, black, blue, green, yellow and grey. The two black chairs cannot be told apart, so by Box 10

\[\text{number of arrangements}=\dfrac{6!}{2!}=360.\]

Note that the two black chairs are not glued together: other chairs may stand between them.

PP 3Sydney Technical High School, 2023, Extension 1 Mathematics, Trial HSC, Q1

SPUDDLE has 7 letters, with two Ds. Deal with the restriction first: the P goes in the first place in 1 way. The remaining six letters S, U, D, D, L, E fill the other six places, with two Ds alike, so

\[\text{number of arrangements}=1\times\dfrac{6!}{2!}=360.\]

Answer B. Option A, \(\dfrac{7!}{2!}\), is the number of arrangements with no restriction.

PP 4Sydney Grammar School, 2022, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(a)

TABLECLOTH has 10 letters, with two Ts and two Ls, and the other six letters A, B, E, C, O, H once each. By Box 10,

\[\text{number of arrangements}=\dfrac{10!}{2!\times2!}=907\,200.\]
PP 5Sydney Grammar School, 2023, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(c)

The four letters of MILO are all different, so MILO has \(4!=24\) arrangements.

SAVVAS has 6 letters: two Ss, two As and two Vs. By Box 10 it has

\[\dfrac{6!}{2!\times2!\times2!}=90\text{ arrangements.}\]

Since \(90>24\), SAVVAS has more arrangements.

PP 6Baulkham Hills High School, 2020, Year 12 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q8

EXTENSION has 9 letters: two Es, two Ns, and X, T, S, I, O once each. Glue the two Es together as one group EE. Because the two Es are identical, the group can be ordered in only 1 way. There are now 8 items to arrange: EE, X, T, N, S, I, O, N, with two Ns alike. By Box 10,

\[\text{number of arrangements}=\dfrac{8!}{2!}=20\,160.\]

Answer D. Option C, \(\dfrac{8!}{2!\,2!}\), wrongly divides by \(2!\) for the Es as well. Once the Es are glued together they form a single item, so there is nothing left to overcount.

PP 7St George Girls High School, 2020, Trial HSC Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(c)(i)(ii)

STATISTICS has 10 letters: three Ss, three Ts, two Is, and A and C once each.

(i) By Box 10,

\[\text{number of arrangements}=\dfrac{10!}{3!\times3!\times2!}=50\,400.\]

(ii) Glue the A and C together as one group. The group can be ordered in \(2!\) ways (AC or CA). There are now 9 items: the group, three Ss, three Ts and two Is. Hence

\[\begin{aligned}\text{number of arrangements}&=2!\times\dfrac{9!}{3!\times3!\times2!}\\ &=10\,080.\end{aligned}\]
PP 8Normanhurst Boys High School, 2021, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Q3

CALCULUS has 8 letters: two Cs, two Ls, two Us, and A and S once each. The number of different arrangements is

\[|S|=\dfrac{8!}{2!\times2!\times2!}=5040.\]

These arrangements are equally likely: if the 8 letters are shuffled at random, each different arrangement comes from exactly \(2!\times2!\times2!=8\) of the \(8!\) orders of the letters.

For a C at the start and at the end, both Cs are used up. The six letters A, L, U, L, U, S fill the middle six places, with two Ls and two Us alike:

\[|E|=\dfrac{6!}{2!\times2!}=180.\] \[P(\text{C at the start and the end})=\dfrac{180}{5040}=\dfrac{1}{28}.\]

Answer (C).

PP 9Ascham School, 2020, Trial HSC Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q2

The six cards A, A, C, H, M, S include two As. The number of different words the cards can form is

\[\dfrac{6!}{2!}=360.\]

These words are equally likely, since each one comes from exactly \(2!=2\) of the \(6!\) orders of the cards (the two A cards can be swapped). Only one of the words is ASCHAM, so

\[P(\text{ASCHAM})=\dfrac{1}{360}.\]

Answer B. Option C, \(\dfrac{1}{720}\), forgets that the two A cards can be swapped.

PP 10Normanhurst Boys High School, 2022, Mathematics Extension 1, Year 12 Course Assessment Task 4 (Trial Examination), Q11(e)

The grid is 5 unit squares wide and 3 unit squares high. Every path from \(A\) to \(B\) is made of 5 steps right (R) and 3 steps down (D), in some order. So each path is an eight-letter word with five Rs and three Ds, and each such word gives exactly one path. By Box 11,

\[\text{number of paths}=\dfrac{8!}{5!\times3!}=56.\]
PP 11St Aloysius’ College, 2024, Trial Higher School Certificate Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q8

The two grids meet only at the single point \(C\), so every path from \(A\) to \(B\) passes through \(C\).

{{svg:stalo24c}}

From \(A\) to \(C\) a path has 5 steps right and 3 steps up: \(\dfrac{8!}{5!\times3!}=56\) paths (Box 11). From \(C\) to \(B\) a path has 4 steps right and 2 steps up: \(\dfrac{6!}{4!\times2!}=15\) paths. Any first part can be followed by any second part, so

\[\text{number of paths}=56\times15=840.\]

Answer (B).

PP 12Hurlstone Agricultural High School, 2023, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q10

Line up the 10 players in a fixed order, and give each player a label for what happens to them: 1st, 2nd, 3rd or 4th; F for a finalist who is not placed; or O for a player who is not selected. Each way of carrying out the process gives exactly one list of labels, and each list using the labels

\[1\text{st},\ 2\text{nd},\ 3\text{rd},\ 4\text{th},\ \text{F},\ \text{F},\ \text{O},\ \text{O},\ \text{O},\ \text{O}\]

gives exactly one way. This is a ten-letter word with two Fs alike and four Os alike, so by Box 10

\[\text{number of ways}=\dfrac{10!}{4!\times2!}=75\,600.\]

Answer (C). Option (D), \(\dfrac{10!}{6!}\), only places 4 of the 10 players and forgets to choose the other 2 finalists.
