Lesson Examples
C1
Tutor demo.

a The graph of \(y=\dfrac2x\) has asymptotes \(x=0\) and \(y=0\). Move it down \(1\) unit.
The asymptotes are \(x=0\) and \(y=-1\).
When \(y=0\), \(\ \dfrac2x=1\), so \(x=2\).

{{svg:a6j_c1d}}

b The region crosses the \(x\)-axis at \(x=2\), so split the integral there. Here \(x\gt0\), so \(|x|=x\).

\[\begin{aligned}\int_1^2\Big(\dfrac2x-1\Big)\,dx&=\Big[2\log_ex-x\Big]_1^2\\&=\big(2\log_e2-2\big)-\big(2\log_e1-1\big)\\&=\big(2\log_e2-2\big)-\big(0-1\big)\\&=2\log_e2-1.\end{aligned}\]

This is positive (about \(0.386\)), because this part is above the \(x\)-axis.

\[\begin{aligned}\int_2^4\Big(\dfrac2x-1\Big)\,dx&=\Big[2\log_ex-x\Big]_2^4\\&=\big(2\log_e4-4\big)-\big(2\log_e2-2\big)\\&=2\log_e4-2\log_e2-2\\&=2\log_e2-2.\end{aligned}\]

This is negative (about \(-0.614\)), because this part is below the \(x\)-axis. In the last line, \(2\log_e4-2\log_e2=2\log_e\dfrac42=2\log_e2\).

\[\begin{aligned}\text{Hence area}&=\big(2\log_e2-1\big)+\big(2-2\log_e2\big)\\&=1\text{ square unit.}\end{aligned}\]
Your turn.

a The graph of \(y=\dfrac4x\) has asymptotes \(x=0\) and \(y=0\). Move it down \(1\) unit.
The asymptotes are \(x=0\) and \(y=-1\).
When \(y=0\), \(\ \dfrac4x=1\), so \(x=4\).

{{svg:a6j_c1y}}

b The region crosses the \(x\)-axis at \(x=4\), so split the integral there. Here \(x\gt0\), so \(|x|=x\).

\[\begin{aligned}\int_2^4\Big(\dfrac4x-1\Big)\,dx&=\Big[4\log_ex-x\Big]_2^4\\&=\big(4\log_e4-4\big)-\big(4\log_e2-2\big)\\&=4\log_e4-4\log_e2-2\\&=4\log_e2-2.\end{aligned}\]

This is positive (about \(0.773\)), because this part is above the \(x\)-axis.

\[\begin{aligned}\int_4^8\Big(\dfrac4x-1\Big)\,dx&=\Big[4\log_ex-x\Big]_4^8\\&=\big(4\log_e8-8\big)-\big(4\log_e4-4\big)\\&=4\log_e8-4\log_e4-4\\&=4\log_e2-4.\end{aligned}\]

This is negative (about \(-1.227\)), because this part is below the \(x\)-axis. Here \(4\log_e4-4\log_e2=4\log_e\dfrac42=4\log_e2\) and \(4\log_e8-4\log_e4=4\log_e\dfrac84=4\log_e2\).

\[\begin{aligned}\text{Hence area}&=\big(4\log_e2-2\big)+\big(4-4\log_e2\big)\\&=2\text{ square units.}\end{aligned}\]
C2
Tutor demo.

a Substituting the line \(y=4-x\) into the hyperbola,
\(x(4-x)=3\),  so \(4x-x^2=3\),
\(x^2-4x+3=0\)
\((x-1)(x-3)=0\)
\(x=1\) or \(3\),
so the curves meet at \((1,3)\) and \((3,1)\).

b The hyperbola \(xy=3\) has both axes as asymptotes. The line \(x+y=4\) has \(x\)-intercept \((4,0)\) and \(y\)-intercept \((0,4)\).

{{svg:a6j_c2d}}

c The line is on top: at \(x=2\), the line gives \(2\) and the hyperbola gives \(1\tfrac12\).

\[\begin{aligned}\text{Area}&=\int_1^3\Big((4-x)-\dfrac3x\Big)\,dx\\&=\Big[4x-\tfrac12x^2-3\log_e|x|\Big]_1^3\\&=\big(12-4\tfrac12-3\log_e3\big)-\big(4-\tfrac12-3\log_e1\big)\\&=\big(7\tfrac12-3\log_e3\big)-\big(3\tfrac12-0\big)\\&=\big(4-3\log_e3\big)\text{ square units}\\&\doteq0.704\text{ square units.}\end{aligned}\]
