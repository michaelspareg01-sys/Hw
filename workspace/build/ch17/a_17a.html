Tutor demo. In one hour the machines make 100 bolts, 60 by A and 40 by B. For a bolt chosen at random,

A Bernoulli random variable comes from a Bernoulli trial, which has just two outcomes, ‘success’ and ‘failure’. The race times in A can be any positive number, the card values in B have 13 possibilities, and the number of emails in D can be 0, 1, 2, 3, and so on. In C the result is either ‘red’ or ‘not red’, which is two outcomes. Answer C.

PP 2Blacktown Boys' High School, 2024, HSC Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q6

Let \(X\) be the number of hits in 10 shots, with \(p=\tfrac23\) and \(q=\tfrac13\). Then

\[P(X=9)={}^{10}C_9\big(\tfrac23\big)^9\big(\tfrac13\big)^1=\frac{10\times2^9}{3^{10}}=\frac{5\times2^{10}}{3^{10}}=5\big(\tfrac23\big)^{10}.\]

Answer D. Option A has \(p\) and \(q\) the wrong way round, B leaves out \({}^{10}C_9=10\), and C leaves out the factor \(\tfrac13\) for the one miss.

PP 4Baulkham Hills High School, 2023, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(c)(i)

\[P(X=3)={}^5C_3\big(\tfrac14\big)^3\big(\tfrac34\big)^2=10\times\frac{1}{64}\times\frac{9}{16}=\frac{45}{512}.\]

PP 7Blacktown Boys' High School, 2020, HSC Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(a)

Let \(X\) be the number of the 15 bags that do not contain 50 lollipops. Each bag fails with probability \(p=1-0.92=0.08\), so \(q=0.92\) and \(n=15\).

(i) \(P(X=2)={}^{15}C_2\times0.08^2\times0.92^{13}=0.227\,30\ldots\doteq0.2273\)

\[\begin{aligned}P(X>2)&=1-\big(P(X=0)+P(X=1)+P(X=2)\big)\\&=1-\big(0.92^{15}+{}^{15}C_1\times0.08\times0.92^{14}+{}^{15}C_2\times0.08^2\times0.92^{13}\big)\\&=0.112\,96\ldots\doteq0.1130\end{aligned}\]
PP 8James Ruse Agricultural High School, 2020, Year 12 Trial HSC Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q11(b)

Two dice give 36 equally likely outcomes, and four of them total 9: \((3,6)\), \((4,5)\), \((5,4)\), \((6,3)\). So each round is a success with probability \(p=\tfrac{4}{36}=\tfrac19\), and \(q=\tfrac89\).

Let \(X\) be the number of successes in six rounds. Then

\[\begin{aligned}P(X\ge5)&=P(X=5)+P(X=6)\\&={}^6C_5\big(\tfrac19\big)^5\big(\tfrac89\big)^1+{}^6C_6\big(\tfrac19\big)^6\big(\tfrac89\big)^0\\&=\frac{48}{9^6}+\frac{1}{9^6}=\frac{49}{531\,441}\doteq0.000\,092\,2.\end{aligned}\]
PP 9Knox Grammar School, 2022, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(c)(i)(ii)

(i) A tomato is twice as likely to come from Farm A as from Farm B, so \(P(\text{A})=\tfrac23\) and \(P(\text{B})=\tfrac13\). By the product rule and the addition rule,

\[\begin{aligned}P(\text{determinate})&=P(\text{A and determinate})+P(\text{B and determinate})\\&=\tfrac23\times0.6+\tfrac13\times0.7=\tfrac{12}{30}+\tfrac{7}{30}=\tfrac{19}{30}.\end{aligned}\]

(ii) Let \(X\) be the number of determinate tomatoes among the ten, so \(p=\tfrac{19}{30}\) and \(q=\tfrac{11}{30}\). The complement of ‘no more than eight’ is ‘nine or ten’, so

\[\begin{aligned}P(X\le8)&=1-P(X=9)-P(X=10)\\&=1-{}^{10}C_9\big(\tfrac{19}{30}\big)^9\big(\tfrac{11}{30}\big)^1-{}^{10}C_{10}\big(\tfrac{19}{30}\big)^{10}\big(\tfrac{11}{30}\big)^0\\&=0.929\,50\ldots\doteq0.93\end{aligned}\]
PP 10Killara High School, 2024, Year 12 Examination, Mathematics Extension 1 HSC Trial Examination, Q9

Let \(p\) be the probability that a day is sunny. Three consecutive sunny days have probability \(p^3=0.512\), so \(p=\sqrt[3]{0.512}=0.8\) and \(q=0.2\). Then

\[P(\text{exactly two sunny days in }n\text{ days})={}^nC_2\times0.8^2\times0.2^{n-2}.\]

Test the options: \(n=4\) gives \(6\times0.64\times0.04=0.1536\); \(n=5\) gives \(10\times0.64\times0.008=0.0512\); \(n=6\) gives \(15\times0.64\times0.0016=0.015\,36\); \(n=7\) gives \(21\times0.64\times0.000\,32=0.004\,300\,8\). Only \(n=5\) works. Answer B.
