(i) The mean is \(\mu_{\hat p}=p=0.7\) (not 0.67, which is the sample result). The standard deviation is

\[\sigma_{\hat p}=\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}n}=\sqrt{\frac{0.7\times0.3}{1012}}\doteq0.0144052.\]

(ii) Using the normal approximation to \(\hat p\) without a continuity correction, as in the school solution.

The sample proportion is \(\hat p=0.67\). Here \(np=1012\times0.7=708.4\) and \(n(1-p)=1012\times0.3=303.6\), both greater than 5 (indeed greater than 10), so the normal approximation can be applied.

\[\begin{aligned}P(\hat p\le0.67)&\doteq P\Big(Z\le\frac{0.67-0.7}{0.0144052}\Big)\\&\doteq P(Z\le-2.08)\\&=1-0.9812\\&=0.0188\doteq1.9\%.\end{aligned}\]

This probability is less than 0.05. If 70% of adults really drink the cereal milk, a poll result of 67% or less would be very unlikely, so there is doubt about the result of this poll.

PP 6Abbotsleigh, 2024, Higher School Certificate Assessment 4 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q14(c)(i)

By the empirical rule, 99.7% certainty means within 3 standard deviations either side of the mean \(p\). So the maximum error of 0.001 must be at least 3 standard deviations:

\[3\sigma\le0.001,\qquad\text{so}\qquad\sigma\le\frac{0.001}3=\frac1{3000}.\]

Using the estimate \(p=0.81\),

\[\sqrt{\frac{0.81\times0.19}n}\le\frac1{3000},\qquad\text{so}\qquad\frac{0.1539}n\le\frac1{9\,000\,000},\qquad\text{so}\qquad n\ge0.1539\times9\,000\,000=1\,385\,100.\]

The mathematician needs at least 1 385 100 trials.
