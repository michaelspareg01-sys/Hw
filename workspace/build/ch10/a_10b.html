PP 1Ascham School, 2020, Mathematics Extension 1 Trial HSC Examination, Q7

When \(x=0\), \(y=4\), so the original height is 4. Put \(y=4\): \(-3x^2+2x=0\), that is, \(x(2-3x)=0\). So \(x=0\) (the start) or \(x=\dfrac23\). It travels \(\dfrac23\) unit horizontally before it is back at height 4. Answer (B).

PP 2North Sydney Girls High School, 2020, HSC Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(d)(iii)

(iii) As in the given equation, write \(\alpha\) for \(\alpha^\circ\). From \(\tv{r}\), \(x=30t\cos\alpha\) and \(y=30t\sin\alpha-5t^2+20\). So \(t=\dfrac{x}{30\cos\alpha}\), and

\[y=30\sin\alpha\times\dfrac{x}{30\cos\alpha}-5\times\dfrac{x^2}{900\cos^2\alpha}+20=x\tan\alpha-\dfrac{x^2}{180}\sec^2\alpha+20.\]

The target is at \(x=90\), \(y=1\). Using \(\sec^2\alpha=1+\tan^2\alpha\),

\[1=90\tan\alpha-45\left(1+\tan^2\alpha\right)+20,\quad\text{so}\quad45\tan^2\alpha-90\tan\alpha+26=0.\]

By the quadratic formula,

\[\tan\alpha=\dfrac{90\pm\sqrt{90^2-4\times45\times26}}{90}=\dfrac{90\pm\sqrt{3420}}{90}=1\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{95}}{15}.\]

So \(\tan\alpha\approx0.3502\) or \(\tan\alpha\approx1.6498\), giving \(\alpha\approx19^\circ18'\) or \(\alpha\approx58^\circ47'\).

PP 3Sydney Grammar School, 2022, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(d)

(i) The projectile lands when \(y=0\): \(t\left(V\sin\alpha-5t\right)=0\). Since \(t\neq0\), the time of flight is \(t=\dfrac{V\sin\alpha}{5}\). Substituting into \(x\),

\[R=V\cos\alpha\times\dfrac{V\sin\alpha}{5}=\dfrac{V^2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{5}=\dfrac{V^2\sin2\alpha}{10}.\]

(ii) For a given \(V\), \(R\) is largest when \(\sin2\alpha\) is largest. Since \(0^\circ<\alpha<90^\circ\), we have \(0^\circ<2\alpha<180^\circ\), and \(\sin2\alpha\le1\) with equality only when \(2\alpha=90^\circ\). So \(R_{\max}\) occurs when \(\alpha=45^\circ\), and

\[R_{\max}=\dfrac{V^2\times1}{10}=\dfrac{V^2}{10}.\]

(iii) The target is 12 km \(=12\,000\) m away, so it must be within the maximum range: \(\dfrac{V^2}{10}\ge12\,000\). Then \(V^2\ge120\,000\), so

\[V\ge\sqrt{120\,000}=200\sqrt3\approx346.4>343.\]

So the launch speed must be faster than the speed of sound, 343 m/s.

PP 4Baulkham Hills High School, 2023, Higher School Certificate Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q14(c)

(i) Since \(\theta\neq90^\circ\), \(\cos\theta\neq0\). From \(\tv{r}(t)\), \(x=Vt\cos\theta\), so \(t=\dfrac{x}{V\cos\theta}\). Substituting into \(y=Vt\sin\theta-\tfrac12gt^2\),

\[y=x\tan\theta-\dfrac{gx^2}{2V^2\cos^2\theta}=x\tan\theta-\dfrac{x^2}{4h}\left(1+\tan^2\theta\right),\]

because \(h=\dfrac{V^2}{2g}\) gives \(\dfrac{g}{2V^2}=\dfrac{1}{4h}\), and \(\dfrac{1}{\cos^2\theta}=\sec^2\theta=1+\tan^2\theta\).

(ii) A ball at angle \(\theta\) passes through \((a,b)\) when \(b=a\tan\theta-\dfrac{a^2}{4h}\left(1+\tan^2\theta\right)\). Multiplying by \(4h\) and rearranging,

\[a^2\tan^2\theta-4ha\tan\theta+\left(a^2+4hb\right)=0.\]

This is a quadratic in \(\tan\theta\). The two balls have different angles of elevation, so \(\tan\theta_1\) and \(\tan\theta_2\) must be two different real roots. Hence the discriminant is positive:

\[16h^2a^2-4a^2\left(a^2+4hb\right)>0,\quad\text{that is,}\quad4a^2\left(4h^2-4hb-a^2\right)>0.\]

Since \(a^2>0\), this gives \(a^2<4h^2-4hb=4h(h-b)\).

(iii) By the product of the roots of the quadratic in (ii),

\[\tan\theta_1\tan\theta_2=\dfrac{a^2+4hb}{a^2}=1+\dfrac{4hb}{a^2}\ge1,\]

since \(h>0\) and \((a,b)\) is not below the ground, so \(b\ge0\). But if \(0^\circ<\theta_1<45^\circ\) and \(0^\circ<\theta_2<45^\circ\), then \(0<\tan\theta_1<1\) and \(0<\tan\theta_2<1\), so \(\tan\theta_1\tan\theta_2<1\). This is a contradiction. So two balls with \(\theta_1<45^\circ\) and \(\theta_2<45^\circ\) cannot both pass through \((a,b)\).

PP 5Killara High School, 2024, Year 12 Examination, Mathematics Extension 1 HSC Trial Examination, Q14(d)

For \(P\), the vertical component of \(\tv{r}\) is zero when \(t\left(u\sin\theta-\dfrac{gt}{2}\right)=0\). So \(P\) lands at \(t_P=\dfrac{2u\sin\theta}{g}\), and its range is \(u\cos\theta\,t_P=\dfrac{2u^2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{g}=\dfrac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}\).

For \(Q\), replace \(u\) by \(2u\) and \(\theta\) by \(2\theta\). It lands at \(t_Q=\dfrac{2(2u)\sin2\theta}{g}=\dfrac{4u\sin2\theta}{g}\), and its range is \(\dfrac{(2u)^2\sin4\theta}{g}=\dfrac{4u^2\sin4\theta}{g}\).

Both land at \(B\), so the ranges are equal:

\[\dfrac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}=\dfrac{4u^2\sin4\theta}{g}=\dfrac{8u^2\sin2\theta\cos2\theta}{g}.\]

Since \(\theta\) and \(2\theta\) are acute, \(\sin2\theta\neq0\), so \(\cos2\theta=\dfrac18\). Then \(2\cos^2\theta-1=\dfrac18\), so \(\cos^2\theta=\dfrac{9}{16}\) and, as \(\theta\) is acute, \(\cos\theta=\dfrac34\).

Now compare the times:

\[t_Q=\dfrac{4u\times2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{g}=4\cos\theta\times\dfrac{2u\sin\theta}{g}=4\cos\theta\times t_P=4\times\dfrac34\times0.6=1.8.\]

\(Q\) takes 1.8 seconds to travel from \(A\) to \(B\).
