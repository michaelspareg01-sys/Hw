Chapter revision

Motion in one dimension

A short review of Advanced Sections 9A–9C: displacement, velocity and acceleration as derivatives, and integrating with respect to time. These ideas are used in both directions, horizontal and vertical, throughout projectile motion.
{{sec:violet:loop:Summary:keep this page open while you work}}
Displacement, velocity and acceleration (9A, 9B)
  • Motion in one dimension is described by the displacement \(x\) on a number line as a function of the time \(t\) after time zero. Negative values of time are excluded unless stated.
  • Average velocity \(=\dfrac{\text{change in displacement}}{\text{change in time}}=\dfrac{x_2-x_1}{t_2-t_1}\), the gradient of the chord on the \(x\)–\(t\) graph. Average speed \(=\dfrac{\text{distance travelled}}{\text{time taken}}\).
  • Distance travelled is not the same as displacement: split the journey at every time the particle changes direction.
\[v=\dfrac{dx}{dt}=\dot x\qquad\qquad a=\dfrac{dv}{dt}=\dfrac{d^2x}{dt^2}=\ddot x\qquad\qquad \text{speed}=|v|\]

A particle is stationary when \(v=0\). Displacement and velocity are vector quantities (they have a sign); distance and speed are scalars and are never negative.

Integrating with respect to time (9C)
  • Given the acceleration, integrate to find the velocity. Given the velocity, integrate to find the displacement.
  • Never omit the constants of integration. Use an initial (boundary) condition to evaluate each one.
  • A body falling freely accelerates downwards at \(g\doteq9.8\ \text{m/s}^2\) (or \(10\ \text{m/s}^2\) in round figures), provided air resistance is ignored. If upwards is positive, start with \(\ddot x=-g\) and integrate.
The chain rule and resolving (Year 11)

\(\dfrac{dy}{dt}=\dfrac{dy}{dx}\times\dfrac{dx}{dt}\) and \(\dfrac{dx}{dy}=1\Big/\dfrac{dy}{dx}\). In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse \(v\) and angle \(\theta\), the adjacent side is \(v\cos\theta\) and the opposite side is \(v\sin\theta\).
