Past-Paper Homework - Worked Solutions
PP 1Kambala, 2020, Yearly Examination September 2020, Preliminary Mathematics Advanced, Q11(a)(b)

(a) This is SAS, so use the cosine rule with the included angle \(B=60^\circ\):

\[AC^2=5^2+8^2-2\times5\times8\times\cos60^\circ=25+64-80\times\tfrac12=49.\]

So \(AC=\) 7 cm.

(b) Area \(=\tfrac12\times5\times8\times\sin60^\circ=20\times\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=\) \(10\sqrt3\) cm².

PP 2North Sydney Girls High School, 2020, Preliminary Mathematics Advanced, Q13(b)

The angle \(\theta\) is included between the sides 10 m and 18 m, so by the area formula

\[\tfrac12\times10\times18\times\sin\theta=86,\qquad \sin\theta=\frac{86}{90}\doteqdot0.9556.\]

The related angle is about \(72^\circ50'\), and \(\theta\) may be acute or obtuse, so \(\theta\doteqdot73^\circ\) or \(107^\circ\). (Both give a triangle, because the third side is found from the other two sides and \(\theta\).)

PP 3Loreto Kirribilli, 2020, Year 11 Assessment Task 3, Mathematics Advanced, Q31

The side 9.8 cm is opposite \(\theta\) and the side 6.2 cm is opposite \(24^\circ\). By the sine rule,

\[\frac{\sin\theta}{9.8}=\frac{\sin24^\circ}{6.2},\qquad \sin\theta=\frac{9.8\sin24^\circ}{6.2}\doteqdot0.6429.\]

The related angle is about \(40^\circ\). The question says \(\theta\) is obtuse, so \(\theta\doteqdot180^\circ-40^\circ=\) \(140^\circ\). (Check: \(140^\circ+24^\circ<180^\circ\).)

PP 4Cheltenham Girls High School, 2020, Year 11 Mathematics Advanced Preliminary Final Examination, Q24(a)(b)

(a) At \(C\), the bearing of \(A\) is \(140^\circ+180^\circ=320^\circ\), so \(CA\) makes an angle of \(360^\circ-320^\circ=40^\circ\) with north, on the west side. \(CB\) makes \(38^\circ\) with north on the east side. So \(\angle ACB=40^\circ+38^\circ=78^\circ\).

(b) SAS, so the cosine rule:

\[AB^2=35^2+42^2-2\times35\times42\times\cos78^\circ\doteqdot2377.7,\qquad AB\doteqdot48.8,\]

so the distance is about 49 km.

PP 5Killara High School, 2022, Year 11 Preliminary Examination, Mathematics Advanced, Q20(a)(b)

(a) Both bearings are measured clockwise from north at \(A\), so \(\angle BAT=95^\circ-19^\circ=76^\circ\).

(b) \(AT=10.8\) km. In \(\triangle ABT\), use the cosine rule for \(BT\):

\[BT^2=6^2+10.8^2-2\times6\times10.8\times\cos76^\circ\doteqdot121.29.\]

So \(BT\doteqdot\) 11.01 km.

Since \(10.8<11.01\), Town \(A\) is closer to point \(T\).
