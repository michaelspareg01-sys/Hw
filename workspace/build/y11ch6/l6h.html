Lesson 6H

Trigonometric equations

This piece of work is absolutely vital, because so many problems in later work end up with a trigonometric equation that has to be solved. There are many small details and qualifications in the methods, and the subject needs a great deal of careful study. The lesson covers the domain, boundary angles, the quadrants-and-related-angle method, the reciprocal functions, compound angles, and equations with more than one trigonometric function.

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 2:the standard method: quadrants and the related acute angle}}
The standard method

Most trigonometric equations eventually come down to one or more equations such as

\[\sin x=-\tfrac12,\quad\text{where }-180^\circ\le x\le180^\circ.\]

Provided that the angle is not a boundary angle, the method is:

28 The quadrants-and-related-angle method
  1. Draw a quadrant diagram, then draw a ray in each quadrant that the angle could be in.
  2. Find the related acute angle (only work with positive numbers here):
    a using special angles, or
    b using the calculator to find an approximation.
    Never enter a negative number into the calculator at this point.
  3. Mark the angles on the ends of the rays, taking account of any restrictions on \(x\), and write a conclusion.
Note

When using the calculator, never enter a negative number and take an inverse trigonometric function of it. In Example 17 below, the calculator was used to find the related acute angle whose tan was 3, which is \(71^\circ34'\), correct to the nearest minute. The positive number 3 was entered, not \(-3\).

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 4:equations with compound angles}}
Equations with compound angles

In some equations, the angle is a function of \(x\) rather than simply \(x\) itself. For example,

\[\begin{aligned}&\tan2x=\sqrt3,\ \text{where }0^\circ\le x\le360^\circ,\qquad\text{or}\\ &\sin(x-250^\circ)=\frac{\sqrt3}{2},\ \text{where }0^\circ\le x\le360^\circ.\end{aligned}\]

These equations are really trigonometric equations in the compound angles \(2x\) and \((x-250^\circ)\) respectively. The secret lies in solving for the compound angle, and in first calculating the domain for that compound angle.

30 Equations with compound angles
  1. Let \(u\) be the compound angle.
  2. Find the restrictions on \(u\) from the given restrictions on \(x\).
  3. Solve the trigonometric equation for \(u\).
  4. Hence solve for \(x\).

Worked example 19From the text: keep for homework
Solve \(\tan2x=\sqrt3\), where \(0^\circ\le x\le360^\circ\).
Solution
\[\begin{aligned}&\text{Let} & u&=2x.\\ &\text{Then} & \tan u&=\sqrt3.\\ &\text{The restriction on }x\text{ is} & 0^\circ&\le x\le360^\circ\\ &\boxed{\times2} & 0^\circ&\le 2x\le720^\circ\\ &\text{and replacing }2x\text{ by }u, & 0^\circ&\le u\le720^\circ.\end{aligned}\]

(The restriction on \(u\) is the key step here.)

\[\begin{aligned}&\text{Hence from the diagram,} & u&=60^\circ, 240^\circ, 420^\circ\text{ or }600^\circ.\\ &\text{Because }x=\tfrac12u, & x&=30^\circ, 120^\circ, 210^\circ\text{ or }300^\circ.\end{aligned}\]
{{svg:h_ex19}}
Worked example 20From the text: keep for homework
Solve \(\sin(x-250^\circ)=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\), where \(0^\circ\le x\le360^\circ\).
Solution
\[\begin{aligned}&\text{Let} & u&=x-250^\circ.\\ &\text{Then} & \sin u&=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}.\\ &\text{The restriction on }x\text{ is} & 0^\circ&\le x\le360^\circ\\ &\boxed{-250^\circ} & -250^\circ&\le x-250^\circ\le110^\circ\\ &\text{and replacing }x-250^\circ\text{ by }u, & -250^\circ&\le u\le110^\circ.\end{aligned}\]

(Again, the restriction on \(u\) is the key step here.)

\[\begin{aligned}&\text{Hence from the diagram,} & u&=-240^\circ\text{ or }60^\circ.\\ &\text{Because }x=u+250^\circ, & x&=10^\circ\text{ or }310^\circ.\end{aligned}\]

{{sec:teal:key:Concept 5:equations with more than one trigonometric function}}
Equations with more than one trigonometric function

Some trigonometric equations involve more than one trigonometric function. For example,

\[\sin x+\sqrt3\cos x=0.\]

The general approach is to use trigonometric identities to produce an equation in only one trigonometric function.

Tutor demoOne function only
Solve \(2\sin x+3\cos x=0\), where \(0^\circ\le x\le360^\circ\). Give the solutions correct to the nearest minute. {{work:38}}
Your turnSame method
Solve \(\sin x-2\cos x=0\), where \(0^\circ\le x\le360^\circ\). Give the solutions correct to the nearest minute. {{work:38}}
Worked example 21From the text: keep for homework
Solve \(\sin x+\sqrt3\cos x=0\), where \(0^\circ\le x\le360^\circ\).
Solution
\[\begin{aligned}&& \sin x+\sqrt3\cos x&=0\\ &\boxed{\div\cos x} & \tan x+\sqrt3&=0\\ && \tan x&=-\sqrt3,\quad\text{where }0^\circ\le x\le360^\circ.\end{aligned}\]

Because \(\tan x\) is negative, \(x\) is in quadrants 2 or 4.
The tan of the related acute angle is \(\sqrt3\), so the related angle is \(60^\circ\).
Hence \(x=120^\circ\) or \(300^\circ\).

Steps the book skips: Example 21

Dividing by \(\cos x\) is allowed here because \(\cos x\ne0\) for every solution: if \(\cos x=0\), the equation would give \(\sin x=0\) as well, and \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\) are never zero together.
