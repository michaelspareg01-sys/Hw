20 The reciprocal identities

For any angle \(\theta\):

\[\begin{aligned}\operatorname{cosec}\theta&=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\quad(\text{provided that }\sin\theta\ne0)\\ \sec\theta&=\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\quad(\text{provided that }\cos\theta\ne0)\\ \cot\theta&=\frac{1}{\tan\theta}\quad(\text{provided that }\tan\theta\ne0\text{ and }\cot\theta\ne0)\end{aligned}\]
21 The ratio identities

For any angle \(\theta\):

\[\begin{aligned}\tan\theta&=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\quad(\text{provided that }\cos\theta\ne0)\\ \cot\theta&=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}\quad(\text{provided that }\sin\theta\ne0)\end{aligned}\]

Note: We cannot use a calculator to find \(\cot90^\circ\) or \(\cot270^\circ\) by first finding \(\tan90^\circ\) or \(\tan270^\circ\), because both are undefined. We already know, however, that \(\cot90^\circ=\cot270^\circ=0\).

22 The Pythagorean identities

For any angle \(\theta\):

\[\begin{aligned}\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta&=1\\ \tan^2\theta+1&=\sec^2\theta\quad(\text{provided that }\cos\theta\ne0)\\ \cot^2\theta+1&=\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta\quad(\text{provided that }\sin\theta\ne0)\end{aligned}\]
23 The complementary angle identities

For any angle \(\theta\):

\[\begin{aligned}\cos(90^\circ-\theta)&=\sin\theta\\ \cot(90^\circ-\theta)&=\tan\theta\quad(\text{provided that }\tan\theta\text{ is defined})\\ \operatorname{cosec}(90^\circ-\theta)&=\sec\theta\quad(\text{provided that }\sec\theta\text{ is defined})\end{aligned}\]

For example, \(\cos20^\circ=\sin70^\circ\), \(\operatorname{cosec}20^\circ=\sec70^\circ\), \(\cot20^\circ=\tan70^\circ\).

24 Cosine, cosecant and cotangent

cosine \(\theta\) = sine (complement of \(\theta\))
cotangent \(\theta\) = tangent (complement of \(\theta\))
cosecant \(\theta\) = secant (complement of \(\theta\))

25 Proving trigonometric identities
  • Work separately on the LHS and the RHS until they are the same.
  • Use the four sets of identities in Boxes 20–23 above.

Mostly it is only necessary to work on one of the two sides. The important thing is never to treat it as an equation, moving terms from one side to the other.

Elimination

If \(x\) and \(y\) are given as functions of \(\theta\), then using the techniques of simultaneous equations, the variable \(\theta\) can often be eliminated to give a relation (rarely a function) between \(x\) and \(y\).
