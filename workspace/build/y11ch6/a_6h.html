Lesson Examples
C1
Tutor demo. Sketch \(y=\cos x\) for \(-360^\circ\le x\le360^\circ\).
a The curve touches \(y=-1\) at \(x=-180^\circ\) or \(180^\circ\).
b The curve crosses the \(x\)-axis at \(x=-270^\circ, -90^\circ, 90^\circ\) or \(270^\circ\).
Your turn. a From the graph of \(y=\sin x\): \(x=-270^\circ\) or \(90^\circ\).
b From the graph of \(y=\tan x\), the zeroes are at multiples of \(180^\circ\): \(x=-180^\circ, 0^\circ, 180^\circ\) or \(360^\circ\).
C2
Tutor demo. a \(\tan x\) is negative, so \(x\) is in quadrant 2 or 4. The related angle has \(\tan=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\), so it is \(30^\circ\). With \(-180^\circ\le x\le180^\circ\), \(x=-30^\circ\) or \(150^\circ\).
b \(\tan x\) is positive, so \(x\) is in quadrant 1 or 3. The related angle is \(\tan^{-1}2.5\doteqdot68^\circ\). So \(x\doteqdot68^\circ\) or \(248^\circ\).
Your turn. a \(\cos x\) is negative, so \(x\) is in quadrant 2 or 3; the related angle is \(60^\circ\). With \(-180^\circ\le x\le180^\circ\), \(x=-120^\circ\) or \(120^\circ\).
b \(\cos x\) is negative, so \(x\) is in quadrant 2 or 3. The related angle is \(\cos^{-1}0.4\doteqdot66^\circ25'\) (enter \(+0.4\)). So \(x\doteqdot180^\circ-66^\circ25'\) or \(180^\circ+66^\circ25'\), that is, \(x\doteqdot114^\circ\) or \(246^\circ\).
C3
Tutor demo. a Taking reciprocals, \(\tan x=-1\). Quadrant 2 or 4, related angle \(45^\circ\), so \(x=-45^\circ\) or \(135^\circ\).
b Taking reciprocals, \(\sin x=2\), which has no solutions, because \(\sin x\) can never be greater than 1.
Your turn. a \(\sin x=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\): quadrant 3 or 4, related angle \(45^\circ\), so \(x=225^\circ\) or \(315^\circ\).
b \(\cos x=-\tfrac{1}{0.8}=-1.25\), which has no solutions, because \(\cos x\) can never be less than \(-1\).
C4
Tutor demo. a Let \(u=2x\), so \(0^\circ\le u\le720^\circ\) and \(\cos u=\tfrac12\). Quadrant 1 or 4, related angle \(60^\circ\): \(u=60^\circ, 300^\circ, 420^\circ, 660^\circ\). Halving, \(x=30^\circ, 150^\circ, 210^\circ\) or \(330^\circ\).
b Let \(u=x+20^\circ\), so \(20^\circ\le u\le380^\circ\) and \(\tan u=1\): \(u=45^\circ\) or \(225^\circ\) (\(405^\circ\) is too big). Subtracting \(20^\circ\), \(x=25^\circ\) or \(205^\circ\).
Your turn. a Let \(u=2x\), \(0^\circ\le u\le720^\circ\), \(\sin u=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\): \(u=60^\circ, 120^\circ, 420^\circ, 480^\circ\), so \(x=30^\circ, 60^\circ, 210^\circ\) or \(240^\circ\).
b Let \(u=x-40^\circ\), \(-40^\circ\le u\le320^\circ\), \(\cos u=-\tfrac12\): quadrant 2 or 3, related angle \(60^\circ\), \(u=120^\circ\) or \(240^\circ\). Adding \(40^\circ\), \(x=160^\circ\) or \(280^\circ\).
C5
Tutor demo. Divide by \(\cos x\): \(2\tan x+3=0\), so \(\tan x=-1.5\). Quadrant 2 or 4; the related angle is \(\tan^{-1}1.5\doteqdot56^\circ19'\). So \(x\doteqdot123^\circ41'\) or \(303^\circ41'\).
Your turn. Divide by \(\cos x\): \(\tan x=2\). Quadrant 1 or 3; the related angle is \(\tan^{-1}2\doteqdot63^\circ26'\). So \(x\doteqdot63^\circ26'\) or \(243^\circ26'\).
