Past-Paper Homework - Worked Solutions
PP 1Sydney Grammar School, 2021, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(d)(i)

Work on the right-hand side and take out the common factor \(\sin x\):

\[\begin{aligned}\text{RHS}&=\sin x-\cos^2x\sin x\\&=\sin x\,(1-\cos^2x)\\&=\sin x\times\sin^2x\qquad(\text{because }\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1)\\&=\sin^3x\\&=\text{LHS}.\end{aligned}\]
PP 2Fort St High School, 2021, Year 11 Assessment Task 3 - Online examination, Mathematics Advanced, Q3(b)

Expand as a difference of squares, then use \(\cot^2x+1=\operatorname{cosec}^2x\):

\[\begin{aligned}\text{LHS}&=\operatorname{cosec}^2x-\cot^2x\\&=(\cot^2x+1)-\cot^2x\\&=1=\text{RHS}.\end{aligned}\]
PP 3Baulkham Hills High School, 2024, Year 11 Yearly Examination, Mathematics Advanced, Q27

Write the bracket in sine and cosine and take out \(\sin^2x\):

\[\begin{aligned}\tan^2x-\sin^2x&=\frac{\sin^2x}{\cos^2x}-\sin^2x=\frac{\sin^2x\,(1-\cos^2x)}{\cos^2x}\\&=\frac{\sin^2x\sin^2x}{\cos^2x}=\sin^2x\tan^2x.\end{aligned}\]

So \(\text{LHS}=\operatorname{cosec}^2x\times\sin^2x\tan^2x=\dfrac{1}{\sin^2x}\times\sin^2x\tan^2x=\tan^2x=\text{RHS}.\)

PP 4Killara High School, 2020, Year 11 Mathematics Advanced Task 3, Q26

Divide each term of the numerator by \(\cos^2\theta\):

\[\begin{aligned}\text{LHS}&=\frac{1}{\cos^2\theta}-\sin^2\theta=\sec^2\theta-\sin^2\theta\\&=(1+\tan^2\theta)-\sin^2\theta\\&=\tan^2\theta+(1-\sin^2\theta)=\tan^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=\text{RHS}.\end{aligned}\]
PP 5Blacktown Boys’ High School, 2022, Year 11 Mathematics Advanced AT3, Q25(a)

Use the common denominator \((\sec\theta-1)(\sec\theta+1)=\sec^2\theta-1=\tan^2\theta\):

\[\begin{aligned}\text{LHS}&=\frac{\tan\theta\,(\sec\theta+1)-\tan\theta\,(\sec\theta-1)}{\sec^2\theta-1}\\&=\frac{2\tan\theta}{\tan^2\theta}=\frac{2}{\tan\theta}=2\cot\theta=\text{RHS}.\end{aligned}\]
PP 6North Sydney Boys High School, 2022, HSC Course Assessment Task 3-Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(c)

The Pythagorean identity \(\cot^2\theta+1=\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta\) gives \(\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta-\cot^2\theta=1\). Factor it as a difference of squares:

\[(\operatorname{cosec}\theta-\cot\theta)(\operatorname{cosec}\theta+\cot\theta)=1.\]

The first bracket is \(\tfrac45\), so \(\tfrac45(\operatorname{cosec}\theta+\cot\theta)=1\), that is,

\[\operatorname{cosec}\theta+\cot\theta=\tfrac54.\qquad(2)\]

Together with \(\operatorname{cosec}\theta-\cot\theta=\tfrac45\) (1), subtract (1) from (2):

\[2\cot\theta=\tfrac54-\tfrac45=\tfrac{25-16}{20}=\tfrac{9}{20},\qquad \cot\theta=\tfrac{9}{40}.\]

So \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{40}{9}\).

Check: adding (1) and (2) gives \(\operatorname{cosec}\theta=\tfrac{41}{40}\), and \(\tfrac{41}{40}-\tfrac{9}{40}=\tfrac{32}{40}=\tfrac45\). Also \(\left(\tfrac{41}{40}\right)^2-\left(\tfrac{9}{40}\right)^2=\tfrac{1681-81}{1600}=1\), as it must be.

PP 7Cherrybrook Technology High School, 2022, Year 11 AP2 Mathematics Advanced, Q20

Use the common denominator \(\sin x\,(1+\cos x)\):

\[\begin{aligned}\text{LHS}&=\frac{\sin^2x+(1+\cos x)^2}{\sin x\,(1+\cos x)}=\frac{\sin^2x+1+2\cos x+\cos^2x}{\sin x\,(1+\cos x)}\\&=\frac{2+2\cos x}{\sin x\,(1+\cos x)}\qquad(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1)\\&=\frac{2(1+\cos x)}{\sin x\,(1+\cos x)}=\frac{2}{\sin x}=2\operatorname{cosec}x=\text{RHS}.\end{aligned}\]
