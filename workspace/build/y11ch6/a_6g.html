PP 1Sydney Grammar School, 2021, Trial Examination, Form VI Mathematics Extension 1, Q13(d)(i)

Work on the right-hand side and take out the common factor \(\sin x\):

\[\begin{aligned}\text{RHS}&=\sin x-\cos^2x\sin x\\&=\sin x\,(1-\cos^2x)\\&=\sin x\times\sin^2x\qquad(\text{because }\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1)\\&=\sin^3x\\&=\text{LHS}.\end{aligned}\]
PP 2North Sydney Boys High School, 2022, HSC Course Assessment Task 3-Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(c)

The Pythagorean identity \(\cot^2\theta+1=\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta\) gives \(\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta-\cot^2\theta=1\). Factor it as a difference of squares:

\[(\operatorname{cosec}\theta-\cot\theta)(\operatorname{cosec}\theta+\cot\theta)=1.\]

The first bracket is \(\tfrac45\), so \(\tfrac45(\operatorname{cosec}\theta+\cot\theta)=1\), that is,

\[\operatorname{cosec}\theta+\cot\theta=\tfrac54.\qquad(2)\]

Together with \(\operatorname{cosec}\theta-\cot\theta=\tfrac45\) (1), subtract (1) from (2):

\[2\cot\theta=\tfrac54-\tfrac45=\tfrac{25-16}{20}=\tfrac{9}{20},\qquad \cot\theta=\tfrac{9}{40}.\]

So \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{40}{9}\).

Check: adding (1) and (2) gives \(\operatorname{cosec}\theta=\tfrac{41}{40}\), and \(\tfrac{41}{40}-\tfrac{9}{40}=\tfrac{32}{40}=\tfrac45\). Also \(\left(\tfrac{41}{40}\right)^2-\left(\tfrac{9}{40}\right)^2=\tfrac{1681-81}{1600}=1\), as it must be.
