The trigonometric functions for acute angles (Year 11 Advanced, 5A)

Let \(\theta\) be any acute angle, \(0^\circ\lt\theta\lt90^\circ\). Construct a right-angled triangle with an acute angle \(\theta\), and label the sides:

  • hyp — the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle,
  • opp — the side opposite the angle \(\theta\),
  • adj — the third side, adjacent to \(\theta\) but is not the hypotenuse.
{{svg:rev_sides}}

1 The trigonometric functions for an acute angle \(\theta\)

\[\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}\qquad\quad\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}\qquad\quad\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}\]

4 To find an unknown side of a right-angled triangle

  1. Start by writing \(\dfrac{\text{unknown side}}{\text{known side}}=\ldots\) (place the unknown at top left).
  2. Complete the RHS with sin, cos or tan, or the reciprocal of one of these.

5 Finding an unknown angle, given two sides of a right-angled triangle

Work out from the known sides which one of \(\cos\theta\), \(\sin\theta\) or \(\tan\theta\) to use.

Left out of this summaryBox 1 in the book also defines cosec, sec and cot, the reciprocals of sin, cos and tan. They are not part of the Year 10 course, so this pack does not use them.

Angles of elevation and depression, and bearings (Year 11 Advanced, 5B)

When the figure has two or more triangles, always name the triangle that you are working in.

Angles of elevation and depression. Angles of elevation and depression are always measured from the horizontal, and are always acute angles.

Compass bearings and true bearings. Compass bearings are based on north, south, east and west. Any other direction is specified by the deviation from north or south towards the east or west. The sine rule and the area formula (Year 11 Advanced, 5I)

The usual statement of all three rules uses the convention shown in the diagram to the right. The vertices are named with upper-case letters, then each side takes its name from the lower-case letter of the opposite vertex.

{{svg:rev_abc}}

30 The sine rule. In any triangle \(ABC\),

\[\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}.\]

‘The ratio of each side to the sine of the opposite angle is constant.’

31 Using the sine rule to find a side. In the AAS congruence situation:

\[\frac{\text{unknown side}}{\text{sine of its opposite angle}}=\frac{\text{known side}}{\text{sine of its opposite angle}}.\]

Always place the unknown side at the top left of the equation.

If two angles of a triangle are known, so is the third, because the angles add to 180°.

32 The area formula. In any triangle \(ABC\),

\[\text{area}\ \Delta ABC=\tfrac12bc\sin A.\]

‘The area of a triangle is half the product of any two sides times the sine of the included angle’.

The cosine rule (Year 11 Advanced, 5J)

35 The cosine rule. In any triangle \(ABC\),

\[a^2=b^2+c^2-2bc\cos A.\]

‘The square of any side of a triangle equals: the sum of the squares of the other two sides, minus twice the product of those sides and the cosine of their included angle.’

37 Using the cosine rule to find a side. In the SAS congruence situation: (square of any side) = (sum of squares of other two sides) − (twice the product of those sides) × (cosine of their included angle).

38 Using the cosine rule to find an angle. In the SSS congruence situation: Substitute into the cosine rule and solve for \(\cos\theta\).

39 The cosine rule with \(\cos A\) as subject. In any triangle \(ABC\),

\[\cos A=\frac{b^2+c^2-a^2}{2bc}.\]

Notice that \(\cos\theta\) is positive when \(\theta\) is acute, and is negative when \(\theta\) is obtuse. Hence, there is only ever one solution for the unknown angle, unlike the situation for the sine rule, in which there are often two possible angles.

Worked exampleYear 11 Advanced Example 29 (5J)
Find \(\theta\) in the triangle drawn to the right.
{{svg:rev_ex29}}
Solution

Substituting into the cosine rule,

\[\begin{aligned}6^2&=3^2+4^2-2\times3\times4\times\cos\theta\\ 24\cos\theta&=-11\\ \cos\theta&=-\frac{11}{24}\\ \theta&\doteqdot117^\circ17'.\end{aligned}\]
OR

Using the box 39 formula,

\[\begin{aligned}\cos\theta&=\frac{3^2+4^2-6^2}{2\times3\times4}\\ \cos\theta&=\frac{-11}{24}\\ \theta&\doteqdot117^\circ17'.\end{aligned}\]

Steps the book skips: Example 29On the left, \(36=9+16-24\cos\theta=25-24\cos\theta\), so \(24\cos\theta=25-36=-11\). On the right, \(9+16-36=-11\). The cosine is negative, so \(\theta\) is obtuse: \(\cos^{-1}\big(-\tfrac{11}{24}\big)=117.28\ldots^\circ\doteqdot117^\circ17'\).

Which rule to use (Year 11 Advanced, 5K)

40 The sine, cosine and area rules and the standard congruence tests. In a right-angled triangle, use simple trigonometry and Pythagoras. Otherwise:

  • AAS: Use the sine rule to find each of the other two sides.
  • ASS: [The ambiguous situation] Use the sine rule to find the unknown angle opposite a known side. There may or may not be two possible solutions.
  • SAS: Use the cosine rule to find the third side. Use the area formula to find the area.
  • SSS: Use the cosine rule to find any angle.
