Lesson Examples
C1
Tutor demo.
a Meeting point: solve \(x^2=(x-4)^2\).
\[\begin{aligned}x^2&=x^2-8x+16\\8x&=16\\x&=2,\end{aligned}\]
and \(y=2^2=4\), so the curves meet at \((2,4)\). The sketch is on the right.
b The region is under \(y=x^2\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=2\), and under \(y=(x-4)^2\) from \(x=2\) to \(x=4\).
{{svg:a5g_c1d}}
c The region is above the \(x\)-axis. Cut it at \(x=2\).
\[\begin{aligned}\int_0^2 x^2\,dx&=\Big[\frac{x^3}{3}\Big]_0^2\\&=\tfrac83-0\\&=\tfrac83\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\int_2^4(x-4)^2\,dx&=\Big[\frac{(x-4)^3}{3}\Big]_2^4\\&=\frac{0^3}{3}-\frac{(-2)^3}{3}\\&=0-\big(-\tfrac83\big)\\&=\tfrac83\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\text{area}&=\tfrac83+\tfrac83\\&=\tfrac{16}{3}\\&=5\tfrac13\text{ square units.}\end{aligned}\]
Your turn.
a Meeting point: solve \(x^2=(x-6)^2\).
\[\begin{aligned}x^2&=x^2-12x+36\\12x&=36\\x&=3,\end{aligned}\]
and \(y=3^2=9\), so the curves meet at \((3,9)\). The sketch is on the right.
b The region is under \(y=x^2\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=3\), and under \(y=(x-6)^2\) from \(x=3\) to \(x=6\).
{{svg:a5g_c1y}}
c Cut the region at \(x=3\).
\[\begin{aligned}\int_0^3 x^2\,dx&=\Big[\frac{x^3}{3}\Big]_0^3\\&=\tfrac{27}{3}-0\\&=9\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\int_3^6(x-6)^2\,dx&=\Big[\frac{(x-6)^3}{3}\Big]_3^6\\&=\frac{0^3}{3}-\frac{(-3)^3}{3}\\&=0-(-9)\\&=9\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\text{area}&=9+9\\&=18\text{ square units.}\end{aligned}\]
C2
Tutor demo.
a Substituting \(y=x+3\) into \(y=(x-3)^2\) gives
\[\begin{aligned}(x-3)^2&=x+3\\x^2-6x+9&=x+3\\x^2-7x+6&=0\\{}(x-1)(x-6)&=0\\x&=1\text{ or }6.\end{aligned}\]
When \(x=1\), \(y=1+3=4\). When \(x=6\), \(y=6+3=9\). So the graphs meet at \((1,4)\) and \((6,9)\).
b The sketch is on the right.
{{svg:a5g_c2d}}
c At \(x=2\), the line gives \(y=5\) and the parabola gives \(y=1\), so the line is above the parabola.
\[\begin{aligned}\text{area}&=\int_1^6\big((x+3)-(x-3)^2\big)\,dx\\&=\int_1^6\big(x+3-(x^2-6x+9)\big)\,dx\\&=\int_1^6(-x^2+7x-6)\,dx\\&=\Big[-\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{7x^2}{2}-6x\Big]_1^6\\&=\big(-72+126-36\big)-\big(-\tfrac13+3\tfrac12-6\big)\\&=18-\big(-2\tfrac56\big)\\&=20\tfrac56\text{ square units.}\end{aligned}\]
(At \(x=6\): \(-\tfrac{216}{3}=-72\) and \(\tfrac{7\times36}{2}=126\). At \(x=1\): \(-\tfrac26+\tfrac{21}{6}-\tfrac{36}{6}=-\tfrac{17}{6}=-2\tfrac56\).)
Your turn.
a Substituting \(y=x+1\) into \(y=(x-1)^2\) gives
\[\begin{aligned}(x-1)^2&=x+1\\x^2-2x+1&=x+1\\x^2-3x&=0\\x(x-3)&=0\\x&=0\text{ or }3.\end{aligned}\]
When \(x=0\), \(y=1\). When \(x=3\), \(y=4\). So the graphs meet at \((0,1)\) and \((3,4)\).
b The sketch is on the right.
{{svg:a5g_c2y}}
c At \(x=1\), the line gives \(y=2\) and the parabola gives \(y=0\), so the line is above the parabola.
\[\begin{aligned}\text{area}&=\int_0^3\big((x+1)-(x-1)^2\big)\,dx\\&=\int_0^3\big(x+1-(x^2-2x+1)\big)\,dx\\&=\int_0^3(-x^2+3x)\,dx\\&=\Big[-\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{3x^2}{2}\Big]_0^3\\&=\big(-9+13\tfrac12\big)-(0+0)\\&=4\tfrac12\text{ square units.}\end{aligned}\]
C3
Tutor demo.
a Substituting \(y=x-3\) into \(y=x^2-6x+3\) gives
\[\begin{aligned}x^2-6x+3&=x-3\\x^2-7x+6&=0\\{}(x-1)(x-6)&=0\\x&=1\text{ or }6.\end{aligned}\]
When \(x=1\), \(y=-2\). When \(x=6\), \(y=3\). So the curves meet at \((1,-2)\) and \((6,3)\).
b The sketch is on the right. The line is above the parabola: at \(x=2\), the line gives \(y=-1\) and the parabola gives \(y=-5\). The region crosses the \(x\)-axis, but Box 17 still applies.
{{svg:a5g_c3d}}
\[\begin{aligned}\text{area}&=\int_1^6\big((x-3)-(x^2-6x+3)\big)\,dx\\&=\int_1^6(x-3-x^2+6x-3)\,dx\\&=\int_1^6(-x^2+7x-6)\,dx\\&=\Big[-\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{7x^2}{2}-6x\Big]_1^6\\&=\big(-72+126-36\big)-\big(-\tfrac13+3\tfrac12-6\big)\\&=18+2\tfrac56\\&=20\tfrac56\text{ square units,}\end{aligned}\]
the same as in C2, because both curves are those of C2 shifted down 6.
Your turn.
a Substituting \(y=x-2\) into \(y=x^2-2x-2\) gives
\[\begin{aligned}x^2-2x-2&=x-2\\x^2-3x&=0\\x(x-3)&=0\\x&=0\text{ or }3.\end{aligned}\]
When \(x=0\), \(y=-2\). When \(x=3\), \(y=1\). So the curves meet at \((0,-2)\) and \((3,1)\).
b The sketch is on the right. The line is above the parabola: at \(x=1\), the line gives \(y=-1\) and the parabola gives \(y=-3\).
{{svg:a5g_c3y}}
\[\begin{aligned}\text{area}&=\int_0^3\big((x-2)-(x^2-2x-2)\big)\,dx\\&=\int_0^3(x-2-x^2+2x+2)\,dx\\&=\int_0^3(-x^2+3x)\,dx\\&=\Big[-\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{3x^2}{2}\Big]_0^3\\&=\big(-9+13\tfrac12\big)-(0+0)\\&=4\tfrac12\text{ square units.}\end{aligned}\]
C4
Tutor demo. In the left-hand region \(0\le x\le1\), the second curve is above the first: at \(x=0\), the second curve gives \(y=7\) and the first gives \(y=-1\).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{area}&=\int_0^1\big((x^2-8x+7)-(-x^2+2x-1)\big)\,dx\\&=\int_0^1(x^2-8x+7+x^2-2x+1)\,dx\\&=\int_0^1(2x^2-10x+8)\,dx\\&=\Big[\frac{2x^3}{3}-5x^2+8x\Big]_0^1\\&=\big(\tfrac23-5+8\big)-(0-0+0)\\&=3\tfrac23\text{ u}^2\end{aligned}\]
In the right-hand region \(1\le x\le4\), the first curve is above the second: at \(x=2\), the first curve gives \(y=-1\) and the second gives \(y=-5\).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{area}&=\int_1^4\big((-x^2+2x-1)-(x^2-8x+7)\big)\,dx\\&=\int_1^4(-x^2+2x-1-x^2+8x-7)\,dx\\&=\int_1^4(-2x^2+10x-8)\,dx\\&=\Big[-\frac{2x^3}{3}+5x^2-8x\Big]_1^4\\&=\big(-42\tfrac23+80-32\big)-\big(-\tfrac23+5-8\big)\\&=5\tfrac13-\big(-3\tfrac23\big)\\&=9\text{ u}^2\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\text{total area}&=3\tfrac23+9\\&=12\tfrac23\text{ u}^2.\end{aligned}\]
Your turn. In the left-hand region \(0\le x\le1\), the second curve is above the first: at \(x=0\), the second curve gives \(y=5\) and the first gives \(y=-1\).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{area}&=\int_0^1\big((x^2-6x+5)-(-x^2+2x-1)\big)\,dx\\&=\int_0^1(2x^2-8x+6)\,dx\\&=\Big[\frac{2x^3}{3}-4x^2+6x\Big]_0^1\\&=\big(\tfrac23-4+6\big)-(0-0+0)\\&=2\tfrac23\text{ u}^2\end{aligned}\]
In the right-hand region \(1\le x\le3\), the first curve is above the second: at \(x=2\), the first curve gives \(y=-1\) and the second gives \(y=-3\).
\[\begin{aligned}\text{area}&=\int_1^3\big((-x^2+2x-1)-(x^2-6x+5)\big)\,dx\\&=\int_1^3(-2x^2+8x-6)\,dx\\&=\Big[-\frac{2x^3}{3}+4x^2-6x\Big]_1^3\\&=\big(-18+36-18\big)-\big(-\tfrac23+4-6\big)\\&=0-\big(-2\tfrac23\big)\\&=2\tfrac23\text{ u}^2\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}\text{total area}&=2\tfrac23+2\tfrac23\\&=5\tfrac13\text{ u}^2.\end{aligned}\]
