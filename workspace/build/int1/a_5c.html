PP 1Sydney Technical High School, 2021, Extension 1 Mathematics, Trial HSC, Q6

The graph is above the \(x\)-axis for \(0\le x<2\), below it for \(2<x<6\), and above it again for \(6<x<8\). Each integral is a sum of signed areas.

  • \(\displaystyle\int_0^2f(x)\,dx=\int_0^1f(x)\,dx+\int_1^2f(x)\,dx\) (dissection at \(x=1\)), and \(\displaystyle\int_1^2f(x)\,dx>0\) because the curve is above the axis there. So (B) is greater than (A).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_0^8f(x)\,dx=\int_0^2f(x)\,dx-(\text{area from 2 to 6})+(\text{area from 6 to 8})\). The region below the axis from 2 to 6 is wider (4 units) and deeper (about twice the height) than the small hump from 6 to 8, so the area from 2 to 6 is greater, and (D) is less than (B).
  • \(\displaystyle\int_0^7f(x)\,dx=\int_0^8f(x)\,dx-\int_7^8f(x)\,dx\), and \(\displaystyle\int_7^8f(x)\,dx>0\), so (C) is less than (D), and so less than (B).

Adding any area below the axis makes the integral smaller, so the greatest value is reached at \(x=2\), where the curve crosses from above to below the axis. Answer (B).

PP 2Abbotsleigh, 2023, Higher School Certificate Assessment 4 Trial Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q10

The graph of \(y=\cos^{-1}x\) falls from \((-1,\pi)\) through \(\big(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big)\) to \((1,0)\), and \(\cos^{-1}(-x)=\pi-\cos^{-1}x\). Let \(g(x)=\cos^{-1}x-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\). Then

{{svg:p5c_abs}}
\[\begin{aligned}g(-x)&=\cos^{-1}(-x)-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\\&=\pi-\cos^{-1}x-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\\&=\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\cos^{-1}x\\&=-g(x),\end{aligned}\]

so \(g(x)\) is odd, and \(\cos^{-1}x=\tfrac{\pi}{2}+g(x)\).

\[\begin{aligned}\int_{-1}^{1}\cos^{-1}x\,dx&=\int_{-1}^{1}\tfrac{\pi}{2}\,dx+\int_{-1}^{1}g(x)\,dx\qquad\text{(integral of a sum)}\\&=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\times2+0\qquad\text{(a rectangle, and }g\text{ is odd)}\\&=\pi.\end{aligned}\]

Geometrically: the curve cuts the rectangle \(-1\le x\le1\), \(0\le y\le\pi\) (area \(2\times\pi=2\pi\)) into the region \(S\) under the curve and the region \(T\) above it. A rotation of \(180^\circ\) about \(\big(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big)\) maps \(S\) onto \(T\), so each has area \(\tfrac12\times2\pi=\pi\). Answer (C). Option (D) is the area of the whole rectangle.

PP 3Epping Boys High School, 2024, Trial Higher School Certificate Examination, Mathematics Extension 1, Q12(c)(ii)

Let \(g(x)=2\cos^{-1}(x-1)-\pi\). Its graph is the graph of \(y=f(x)\) in part (i) moved down by \(\pi\): it runs from \((0,\pi)\) through \((1,0)\) to \((2,-\pi)\).

The graph of \(y=g(x)\) has point symmetry about \((1,0)\), because for \(0\le t\le1\), using \(\cos^{-1}(-t)=\pi-\cos^{-1}t\),

{{svg:p5c_eps}}
\[\begin{aligned}g(1-t)&=2\cos^{-1}(-t)-\pi\\&=2\big(\pi-\cos^{-1}t\big)-\pi\\&=\pi-2\cos^{-1}t\\&=-\big(2\cos^{-1}t-\pi\big)\\&=-g(1+t).\end{aligned}\]

(Equivalently, the graph of \(y=f(x)\) has point symmetry about \((1,\pi)\).) The region \(P\) above the \(x\)-axis from \(x=0\) to \(x=1\) and the region \(Q\) below the axis from \(x=1\) to \(x=2\) are congruent (a rotation of \(180^\circ\) about \((1,0)\) maps one onto the other), so they have equal areas. Hence, dissecting the interval at \(x=1\),

\[\int_0^2\big[2\cos^{-1}(x-1)-\pi\big]\,dx=\int_0^1g(x)\,dx+\int_1^2g(x)\,dx=\text{area }P-\text{area }Q=0.\]
