C3
Tutor demo. \(y=x^3\) is increasing for \(x\ge0\), so lower rectangles use the left ends and upper rectangles use the right ends.
a For \(0\le x\le1\), \(0\le x^3\le1\), so the region lies inside the unit square, which has area 1. Hence \(0<A<1\).
b Two strips of width \(\tfrac12\). Lower: \(\tfrac12\times0^3+\tfrac12\times\big(\tfrac12\big)^3=0+\tfrac12\times\tfrac18=\tfrac{1}{16}\). Upper: \(\tfrac12\times\big(\tfrac12\big)^3+\tfrac12\times1^3=\tfrac{1}{16}+\tfrac{8}{16}=\tfrac{9}{16}\). So \(\tfrac{1}{16}<A<\tfrac{9}{16}\).
c Four strips of width \(\tfrac14\), with heights \(\big(\tfrac k4\big)^3=\tfrac{k^3}{64}\). Lower: \(\tfrac14\times\tfrac{0+1+8+27}{64}=\tfrac{36}{256}=\tfrac{9}{64}\). Upper: \(\tfrac14\times\tfrac{1+8+27+64}{64}=\tfrac{100}{256}=\tfrac{25}{64}\). So \(\tfrac{9}{64}<A<\tfrac{25}{64}\). (Exercise 5A Question 15 shows that \(A=\tfrac14=\tfrac{16}{64}\).)
Your turn.
a For \(0\le x\le2\), \(0\le x^2\le4\), so the region lies inside the rectangle with width 2 and height 4, which has area 8. Hence \(0<A<8\).
b Two strips of width 1. Lower: \(1\times0^2+1\times1^2=1\). Upper: \(1\times1^2+1\times2^2=1+4=5\). So \(1<A<5\).
c Four strips of width \(\tfrac12\), with heights \(0,\ \tfrac14,\ 1,\ \tfrac94,\ 4\) at \(x=0,\ \tfrac12,\ 1,\ \tfrac32,\ 2\). Lower: \(\tfrac12\big(0+\tfrac14+1+\tfrac94\big)=\tfrac12\times\tfrac72=\tfrac74\). Upper: \(\tfrac12\big(\tfrac14+1+\tfrac94+4\big)=\tfrac12\times\tfrac{15}{2}=\tfrac{15}{4}\). So \(\tfrac74<A<\tfrac{15}{4}\).
C4
Tutor demo. A The \(n\) subintervals have width \(\tfrac2n\) and end at \(x=\tfrac2n,\ \tfrac4n,\ \dots,\ \tfrac{2n}{n}=2\). As in the text, (sum of lower rectangles) \(\le A\le\) (sum of upper rectangles).
B The upper heights are \(\big(\tfrac{2}{n}\big)^2=\tfrac{4\times1^2}{n^2},\ \big(\tfrac{4}{n}\big)^2=\tfrac{4\times2^2}{n^2},\ \dots,\ \big(\tfrac{2n}{n}\big)^2=\tfrac{4\times n^2}{n^2}\), so \[\begin{aligned}\text{sum of upper rectangles}&=\frac2n\Big(\frac{4\times1^2}{n^2}+\frac{4\times2^2}{n^2}+\cdots+\frac{4\times n^2}{n^2}\Big)\\&=\frac2n\times\frac{4}{n^2}\times(1^2+2^2+\cdots+n^2)\\&=\frac{8}{n^3}\times\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}\\&=\frac{8\,n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6\times n\times n\times n}\\&=\frac43\times\frac nn\times\frac{n+1}{n}\times\frac{2n+1}{n}\\&=\frac43\Big(1+\frac1n\Big)\Big(2+\frac1n\Big),\end{aligned}\] which has limit \(\tfrac43\times1\times2=\tfrac83\) as \(n\to\infty\).
C The lower heights are \(0,\ \tfrac{4\times1^2}{n^2},\ \dots,\ \tfrac{4(n-1)^2}{n^2}\), so, with \(n-1\) in place of \(n\) in the formula, \[\begin{aligned}\text{sum of lower rectangles}&=\frac2n\Big(0+\frac{4\times1^2}{n^2}+\cdots+\frac{4(n-1)^2}{n^2}\Big)\\&=\frac{8}{n^3}\big(1^2+2^2+\cdots+(n-1)^2\big)\\&=\frac{8}{n^3}\times\frac{(n-1)n(2n-1)}{6}\\&=\frac43\times\frac nn\times\frac{n-1}{n}\times\frac{2n-1}{n}\\&=\frac43\Big(1-\frac1n\Big)\Big(2-\frac1n\Big),\end{aligned}\] which also has limit \(\tfrac43\times1\times2=\tfrac83\).
D \(A\) is trapped between two sums with the same limit \(\tfrac83\), so \(A=\tfrac83\).
Your turn. A The \(n\) subintervals have width \(\tfrac1n\), and (sum of lower rectangles) \(\le A\le\) (sum of upper rectangles).
B The upper heights are \(\tfrac1n,\ \tfrac2n,\ \dots,\ \tfrac nn\), so \[\begin{aligned}\text{sum of upper rectangles}&=\frac1n\Big(\frac1n+\frac2n+\cdots+\frac nn\Big)\\&=\frac{1}{n^2}(1+2+\cdots+n)\\&=\frac{1}{n^2}\times\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\\&=\frac12\times\frac nn\times\frac{n+1}{n}\\&=\frac12\Big(1+\frac1n\Big),\end{aligned}\] which has limit \(\tfrac12\) as \(n\to\infty\).
C The lower heights are \(0,\ \tfrac1n,\ \dots,\ \tfrac{n-1}{n}\), so \[\begin{aligned}\text{sum of lower rectangles}&=\frac1n\Big(0+\frac1n+\cdots+\frac{n-1}{n}\Big)\\&=\frac{1}{n^2}\big(1+2+\cdots+(n-1)\big)\\&=\frac{1}{n^2}\times\frac{(n-1)n}{2}\\&=\frac12\times\frac nn\times\frac{n-1}{n}\\&=\frac12\Big(1-\frac1n\Big),\end{aligned}\] which also has limit \(\tfrac12\).
D Hence \(A=\tfrac12\). Check: the region is the triangle with base 1 and height 1, with area \(\tfrac12\times1\times1=\tfrac12\).

From \(x=1\) to \(x=3\), the graph is the horizontal line \(y=2\). From \(x=3\) to \(x=5\), it is the line \(y=x-1\), which rises from \(3-1=2\) to \(5-1=4\). The two pieces meet at \((3,2)\). So the region under the graph from \(x=1\) to \(x=5\) is a rectangle followed by a trapezium.

\[\begin{aligned}\int_1^5f(x)\,dx&=\text{rectangle}+\text{trapezium}\\&=(3-1)\times2+\frac{2+4}{2}\times(5-3)\\&=4+6\\&=10.\end{aligned}\]
