a \(x^2\) is a primitive of \(2x\). \[\begin{aligned}\int_0^3 2x\,dx&=\Big[x^2\Big]_0^3\\&=3^2-0^2\\&=9.\end{aligned}\] Areas: a triangle with base \(3-0=3\) and height \(2\times3=6\), so its area is \(\tfrac12\times3\times6=9\).
b \[\begin{aligned}\int_2^5(2x-3)\,dx&=\Big[x^2-3x\Big]_2^5\\&=(25-15)-(4-6)\\&=10-(-2)\\&=12.\end{aligned}\] Areas: a trapezium with width \(5-2=3\) and parallel sides \(2\times2-3=1\) and \(2\times5-3=7\), so its area is \(\dfrac{1+7}{2}\times3=4\times3=12\).

Tutor demo.

Read \(f\) from the graph. From \(x=-1\) to \(x=0\), the graph is the straight line through \((-1,0)\) and \((0,1)\), which has gradient 1 and \(y\)-intercept 1, so \(f(x)=x+1\) there.

Now look at the input \(x^2-1\). For \(-1\le x\le1\), \(0\le x^2\le1\), so \(-1\le x^2-1\le0\). So the input always lies on that left-hand line, and

\[f(x^2-1)=(x^2-1)+1=x^2\quad\text{for }-1\le x\le1.\]

Hence, by the fundamental theorem,

\[\begin{aligned}\int_{-1}^{1}f(x^2-1)\,dx&=\int_{-1}^{1}x^2\,dx\\&=\Big[\frac{x^3}{3}\Big]_{-1}^{1}\\&=\frac13-\Big(-\frac13\Big)\\&=\frac23.\end{aligned}\]

Answer D. Option B is \(\displaystyle\int_0^1x^2\,dx\), only half of the interval.
