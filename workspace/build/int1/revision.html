Indices, derivatives, sigma notation and areas

A short review of the Year 11 work on indices (7A–7B), differentiating powers of \(x\) (8C, 8E, 8F), the chain rule (8G) and semicircles (3G), and of sigma notation (Year 12, 1E) and the area formulae. Every lesson in this pack uses the power rule and the index laws.

Index laws, zero and negative indices (Year 11, 7A)

In the power \(a^n\), the number \(a\) is the base and \(n\) is the index (or exponent). For positive integers \(n\), \(a^n=a\times a\times\cdots\times a\) with \(n\) factors.

Combining powers with the same base

  • Multiply: add the indices, \(a^m\times a^n=a^{m+n}\).
  • Divide: subtract the indices, \(a^m\div a^n=a^{m-n}\).
  • Power of a power: multiply, \((a^m)^n=a^{mn}\).

Powers of products and quotients

  • \((ab)^n=a^n\times b^n\)
  • \(\Big(\dfrac ab\Big)^n=\dfrac{a^n}{b^n}\)

Zero and negative indices (\(a\ne0\)) are defined so that the index laws still work. We know \(a^3\div a^3=1\), and the laws give \(a^{3-3}=a^0\), so we define \(a^0=1\). We know \(a^2\div a^3=\frac1a\), and the laws give \(a^{2-3}=a^{-1}\), so we define \(a^{-1}=\frac1a\). In general,

\[a^0=1\qquad a^{-1}=\dfrac1a\qquad a^{-2}=\dfrac{1}{a^2}\qquad a^{-n}=\dfrac{1}{a^n}.\]
  • The negative sign in the index says, ‘Take the reciprocal’. Always do this first. The reciprocal of \(\tfrac23\) is \(\tfrac32\), so \(\Big(\dfrac23\Big)^{-3}=\Big(\dfrac32\Big)^3=\dfrac{27}{8}\).
  • A constant factor stays where it is: \(\frac{5}{x^2}=5\times\frac{1}{x^2}=5x^{-2}\) and \(\frac{1}{3x^5}=\frac13\times\frac{1}{x^5}=\tfrac13x^{-5}\).
Worked exampleYear 11 Example 5 (7A)

a\(x^4\times x^{-10}\)b\(12a^2b^4\div6a^3b^4\)c\((2^n)^{-3}\)
Solution
a \(\begin{aligned}[t]x^4\times x^{-10}&=x^{4-10}\\&=x^{-6}\\&=\dfrac{1}{x^6}\end{aligned}\) b \(\begin{aligned}[t]12a^2b^4\div6a^3b^4&=\tfrac{12}{6}a^{2-3}b^{4-4}\\&=2a^{-1}b^0\\&=2a^{-1}\\&=\dfrac2a\end{aligned}\) c \(\begin{aligned}[t](2^n)^{-3}&=2^{n\times(-3)}\\&=2^{-3n}\\&=\dfrac{1}{2^{3n}}\end{aligned}\)

Fractional indices (Year 11, 7B)

Let \(a\ge0\). Because \(\big(\sqrt a\big)^2=a\) and the index laws give \(\big(a^{\frac12}\big)^2=a^1=a\), we define \(a^{\frac12}=\sqrt a\). In the same way \(a^{\frac13}=\sqrt[3]a\), and in general

\[a^{\frac1n}=\sqrt[n]a\qquad a^{\frac mn}=\big(\sqrt[n]a\big)^m=\sqrt[n]{a^m}\qquad a^{-\frac mn}=\dfrac{1}{a^{\frac mn}},\]

where the non-negative root is always taken (for \(a^{\frac mn}\) and \(a^{-\frac mn}\), take \(a\gt0\)).

Deal with a complicated index in this order: (1) if the index is negative, take the reciprocal; (2) if the index has a denominator, take that root; (3) finally, take the power given by the numerator. For example, \(\Big(\dfrac{8}{27}\Big)^{-\frac43}=\Big(\dfrac{27}{8}\Big)^{\frac43}=\Big(\dfrac32\Big)^4=\dfrac{81}{16}\).

Conversions used all the time in calculus (here \(x\gt0\)):

\[\sqrt x=x^{\frac12}\qquad\dfrac{1}{\sqrt x}=x^{-\frac12}\qquad\sqrt[3]{x^2}=x^{\frac23}\] \[x\sqrt x=x^1\times x^{\frac12}=x^{\frac32}\qquad\dfrac{1}{x\sqrt x}=x^{-\frac32}\]
  • The textbook often writes these indices as mixed numbers: \(x^{1\frac12}=x^{\frac32}\) and \(x^{-1\frac12}=x^{-\frac32}\).
  • \(\sqrt9=9^{\frac12}=3\) (not \(-3\)), and \(\sqrt{-9}\) and \((-9)^{\frac12}\) are undefined. So \(\sqrt x\) and \(x^{\frac12}\) are defined only for \(x\ge0\).

a \(\sqrt{x^3}=x^{\frac32}\)b \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^3}}=x^{-\frac32}\)c \(\sqrt[3]{x^2}=x^{\frac23}\)d \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{x^2}}=x^{-\frac23}\)

Area formulae

All area formulae are based on two principles: the area of a rectangle is length \(\times\) breadth, and when a region is dissected (cut up and rearranged), its area is unchanged.

{{svg:shapes}}
  • Rectangle: \(A=lb\)
  • Triangle: \(A=\tfrac12bh=\tfrac12\times\text{base}\times\text{height}\)
  • Trapezium: \(A=\tfrac12(a+b)h\), the average of the parallel sides \(\times\) the width
  • Circle: \(A=\pi r^2\)
  • Semicircle: \(A=\tfrac12\pi r^2\)
  • Quadrant (quarter circle): \(A=\tfrac14\pi r^2\)

The height \(h\) of a triangle and the width \(h\) of a trapezium are perpendicular distances. For a trapezium, \(h\) is the distance between the parallel sides.

Our own example: using the area formulae

Question. Find the area of:

aa trapezium with parallel sides 3 cm and 7 cm, at a perpendicular distance of 4 cm apart,ba quadrant of radius 6 cm,ca triangle with base 5 cm and perpendicular height 9 cm.

Solution.

a \(\begin{aligned}[t]A&=\tfrac12(a+b)h\\&=\tfrac12\times(3+7)\times4\\&=20\text{ cm}^2\end{aligned}\) b \(\begin{aligned}[t]A&=\tfrac14\pi r^2\\&=\tfrac14\times\pi\times6^2\\&=9\pi\text{ cm}^2\end{aligned}\) c \(\begin{aligned}[t]A&=\tfrac12bh\\&=\tfrac12\times5\times9\\&=22\tfrac12\text{ cm}^2\end{aligned}\)

Leave \(\pi\) in an exact answer, as in part b, unless the question asks for a decimal.

Circles and semicircles (Year 11, 3G), and the graph of \(y=|x|\) (4D)

By Pythagoras’ theorem, the circle with centre the origin and radius \(a\) is \(x^2+y^2=a^2\). It is not a function. Solving for \(y\) gives two functions:

  • \(y=\sqrt{a^2-x^2}\) is the upper semicircle,
  • \(y=-\sqrt{a^2-x^2}\) is the lower semicircle.

The graph of \(y=|x|\) is two half-lines meeting at the origin, \(y=x\) for \(x\ge0\) and \(y=-x\) for \(x\lt0\).

Sums of terms and sigma notation (Year 12, 1E)

For a sequence \(T_1,\ T_2,\ T_3,\ \dots\), the sum of the first \(n\) terms (the \(n\)th partial sum) is \(S_n=T_1+T_2+T_3+\cdots+T_n\). The terms can be recovered from the partial sums: \(T_1=S_1\) and \(T_n=S_n-S_{n-1}\) for \(n\ge2\).

Sigma notation is a concise notation for these sums. The symbol \(\Sigma\) is the Greek capital letter sigma and stands for ‘sum’:

\[\sum_{n=5}^{20}T_n=T_5+T_6+T_7+T_8+\cdots+T_{20},\]

and any two integers can be used in place of 5 and 20. For example,

\[\sum_{n=2}^{5}n^2=2^2+3^2+4^2+5^2=4+9+16+25=54.\]
  • This says ‘evaluate the function \(n^2\) for all the integers from \(n=2\) to \(n=5\), then add up the resulting values’. There are \(5-2+1=4\) terms.
  • The letter under the sigma is only a counter. Other letters such as \(k\), \(r\) and \(\ell\) are also used.
  • A series is the sequence of partial sums: \(S_1=T_1\), \(S_2=T_1+T_2\), \(S_3=T_1+T_2+T_3\), and so on.
  • In Lesson 5A, the area under a curve is approximated by adding up the areas \(f(x)\,\delta x\) of thin strips, written as \(\displaystyle\sum_{x=a}^{b}f(x)\,\delta x\).
Worked exampleYear 12 Example 21 (1E)
Evaluate these sums.
a\(\displaystyle\sum_{n=4}^{7}(5n+1)\)b\(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{5}(-2)^n\)
Solution
a \(\begin{aligned}[t]\sum_{n=4}^{7}(5n+1)&=21+26+31+36\\&=114\end{aligned}\) b \(\begin{aligned}[t]\sum_{n=1}^{5}(-2)^n&=-2+4-8+16-32\\&=-22\end{aligned}\)

Steps the book skips: Year 12 Example 21The terms in a are \(5\times4+1=21\), \(5\times5+1=26\), \(5\times6+1=31\) and \(5\times7+1=36\). In b, odd powers of \(-2\) are negative and even powers are positive.

Differentiating powers of \(x\) (Year 11, 8C, 8E, 8F)

The derivative of any power of \(x\). Let \(f(x)=x^n\), where \(n\) is any real number. Then \(f'(x)=nx^{n-1}\). This rule is usually memorised as: ‘Take the index as a factor, and reduce the index by 1.’

  • It applies unchanged when \(n\) is negative or a fraction. For example, if \(y=x^{-4}\), then \(\frac{dy}{dx}=-4x^{-5}\), and if \(y=x^{\frac52}\), then \(\frac{dy}{dx}=\tfrac52x^{\frac32}\).
  • The derivative of a constant is zero.

Linear combinations. Sums and multiples are differentiated one term at a time. If \(f(x)=g(x)+k(x)\), then \(f'(x)=g'(x)+k'(x)\). If \(f(x)=ag(x)\), then \(f'(x)=ag'(x)\).

Get the function into powers of \(x\) first.

  • Expand a product of brackets first: \(x^3(x-10)=x^4-10x^3\).
  • Convert reciprocals of powers to negative indices, and surds to fractional indices.
  • If the denominator of a fraction is a single term, divide it into each term of the numerator.
  • Then apply the rule. Convert the answer back to fraction or surd form when the question asks.
Worked exampleYear 11 Example 17 (8E)
Differentiate each function, giving your answer without negative indices.
a\(f(x)=\dfrac1x\)b\(f(x)=\dfrac{5}{x^2}\)c\(f(x)=\dfrac{1}{3x^5}\)
Solution

In each example, first convert the expression to negative indices, then apply the rule, then convert back to fraction notation.

a \(\begin{aligned}[t]f(x)&=\dfrac1x\\&=x^{-1}\\f'(x)&=-x^{-2}\\&=-\dfrac{1}{x^2}\end{aligned}\) b \(\begin{aligned}[t]f(x)&=\dfrac{5}{x^2}\\&=5x^{-2}\\f'(x)&=-10x^{-3}\\&=-\dfrac{10}{x^3}\end{aligned}\) c \(\begin{aligned}[t]f(x)&=\dfrac{1}{3x^5}\\&=\tfrac13x^{-5}\\f'(x)&=-\tfrac53x^{-6}\\&=-\dfrac{5}{3x^6}\end{aligned}\)

Steps the book skips: Year 11 Example 17The missing middle steps: in b, \(5\times(-2)x^{-2-1}=-10x^{-3}\); in c, \(\tfrac13\times(-5)x^{-5-1}=-\tfrac53x^{-6}\).

Worked exampleYear 11 Example 21 (8F)
Write each function using a fractional index, then differentiate it, then convert the answer back to surd form.
a\(y=12\sqrt x\)b\(y=\sqrt{16x}\)c\(y=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt x}\)
Solution
a \(\begin{aligned}[t]y&=12\sqrt x\\&=12x^{\frac12}\\\dfrac{dy}{dx}&=12\times\tfrac12\times x^{-\frac12}\\&=\dfrac{6}{\sqrt x}\end{aligned}\) b \(\begin{aligned}[t]y&=\sqrt{16x}\\&=4x^{\frac12}\\\dfrac{dy}{dx}&=4\times\tfrac12\times x^{-\frac12}\\&=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt x}\end{aligned}\) c \(\begin{aligned}[t]y&=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt x}\\&=3x^{-\frac12}\\\dfrac{dy}{dx}&=-\tfrac12\times3\times x^{-1\frac12}\\&=-\dfrac{3}{2x\sqrt x}\end{aligned}\)

Steps the book skips: Year 11 Example 21In b, \(\sqrt{16x}=\sqrt{16}\times\sqrt x=4x^{\frac12}\). In c, \(x^{-1\frac12}=x^{-\frac32}=\dfrac{1}{x^{\frac32}}=\dfrac{1}{x\sqrt x}\), so \(-\tfrac32x^{-1\frac12}=-\dfrac{3}{2x\sqrt x}\).

Worked exampleYear 11 Example 23 (8F)
Divide through by the denominator, then differentiate.
a\(y=\dfrac{10x-6}{\sqrt x}\)b\(y=\dfrac{3x+4\sqrt x}{x}\)
Solution
a \(\begin{aligned}[t]y&=\dfrac{10x^1}{x^{\frac12}}-\dfrac{6}{x^{\frac12}}\\&=10x^{\frac12}-6x^{-\frac12}\\\dfrac{dy}{dx}&=5x^{-\frac12}+3x^{-1\frac12}\end{aligned}\) b \(\begin{aligned}[t]y&=\dfrac{3x}{x}+\dfrac{4x^{\frac12}}{x^1}\\&=3+4x^{-\frac12}\\\dfrac{dy}{dx}&=-2x^{-1\frac12}\end{aligned}\)

Steps the book skips: Year 11 Example 23The index steps: in a, \(x^1\div x^{\frac12}=x^{1-\frac12}=x^{\frac12}\), then \(10\times\tfrac12=5\) and \(-6\times(-\tfrac12)=3\). In b, \(x^{\frac12}\div x^1=x^{-\frac12}\), the constant 3 has derivative 0, and \(4\times(-\tfrac12)=-2\).

The chain rule (Year 11, 8G)

A composite function is a chain of two functions. For example, \(y=\sqrt{25-x^2}\) is ‘square and subtract from 25’, followed by ‘take the positive square root’: let \(u=25-x^2\), then \(y=\sqrt u\).

The chain rule. Suppose that \(y\) is a function of \(u\), where \(u\) is a function of \(x\). Then

\[\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{dy}{du}\times\dfrac{du}{dx}.\]
  • Recommended setting-out: work on the right first. Write \(u\) as a function of \(x\) and \(y\) as a function of \(u\), find \(\dfrac{du}{dx}\) and \(\dfrac{dy}{du}\), then multiply them on the left.
  • Powers of a linear function. With \(u=ax+b\) and \(y=u^n\), \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=a\) and \(\dfrac{dy}{du}=nu^{n-1}\), so \[\dfrac{d}{dx}(ax+b)^n=an(ax+b)^{n-1}.\]
  • A shorter setting-out uses the standard form \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\big(f(x)\big)^n=n\big(f(x)\big)^{n-1}f'(x)\). It is safer to write \(f(x)\) or \(u\) on the right and keep at least one middle step.
  • Lesson 5I reverses this process, so the full setting-out must be clearly understood.
Worked exampleYear 11 Example 24 (8G)
Use the chain rule to differentiate each function.
a\((x^2+1)^6\)b\(7(3x+4)^5\)
Solution
a \(\begin{aligned}[t]\text{Let }y&=(x^2+1)^6.\\\text{Then }\dfrac{dy}{dx}&=\dfrac{dy}{du}\times\dfrac{du}{dx}\\&=6(x^2+1)^5\times2x\\&=12x(x^2+1)^5.\end{aligned}\)
\(\begin{aligned}[t]\text{Let }u&=x^2+1.\\\text{Then }y&=u^6.\\\text{Hence }\dfrac{du}{dx}&=2x\\\text{and }\dfrac{dy}{du}&=6u^5.\end{aligned}\)
b \(\begin{aligned}[t]\text{Let }y&=7(3x+4)^5.\\\text{Then }\dfrac{dy}{dx}&=\dfrac{dy}{du}\times\dfrac{du}{dx}\\&=35(3x+4)^4\times3\\&=105(3x+4)^4.\end{aligned}\)
\(\begin{aligned}[t]\text{Let }u&=3x+4.\\\text{Then }y&=7u^5.\\\text{Hence }\dfrac{du}{dx}&=3\\\text{and }\dfrac{dy}{du}&=35u^4.\end{aligned}\)

Steps the book skips: Year 11 Example 24In each line \(\dfrac{dy}{du}\) is written in terms of \(x\) by putting \(u\) back: in a, \(6u^5=6(x^2+1)^5\); in b, \(35u^4=35(3x+4)^4\).

Worked exampleYear 11 Example 25 (8G)
Use the formula \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(ax+b)^n=an(ax+b)^{n-1}\) to differentiate each function. Convert the answers to parts b and c back to fraction or surd form.
a\(y=(4x-1)^7\)b\(y=\dfrac{1}{3x-8}\)c\(y=\sqrt{5x+3}\)
Solution

a \(\begin{aligned}[t]y&=(4x-1)^7\\\dfrac{dy}{dx}&=4\times7\times(4x-1)^6\qquad\text{(here }a=4,\ b=-1\text{ and }n=7)\\&=28(4x-1)^6\end{aligned}\)

b \(\begin{aligned}[t]y&=(3x-8)^{-1}\qquad\text{(convert to index form)}\\\dfrac{dy}{dx}&=3\times(-1)\times(3x-8)^{-2}\qquad\text{(here }a=3,\ b=-8\text{ and }n=-1)\\&=-\dfrac{3}{(3x-8)^2}\end{aligned}\)

c \(\begin{aligned}[t]y&=(5x+3)^{\frac12}\qquad\text{(convert to index form)}\\\dfrac{dy}{dx}&=5\times\tfrac12\times(5x+3)^{-\frac12}\qquad\text{(here }a=5,\ b=3\text{ and }n=\tfrac12)\\&=\dfrac{5}{2\sqrt{5x+3}}\end{aligned}\)
