Write \(p^{(k)}(x)\) for the \(k\)th derivative of \(p(x)\).
a Let \(p(x)=a_nx^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_1x+a_0\), where \(a_n\ne0\). Differentiating lowers the index of each power of \(x\) by 1, and the derivative of a constant is zero. So after \(k\) derivatives, a term \(a_jx^j\) with \(j\ge k\) becomes \(j(j-1)\cdots(j-k+1)\,a_jx^{j-k}\), and a term with \(j\lt k\) has become zero.
After \(n\) derivatives every term with \(j\lt n\) has vanished, and \(a_nx^n\) has become the constant \(n(n-1)\cdots2\times1\times a_n=n!\,a_n\). So \(p^{(n)}(x)=n!\,a_n\ne0\), because \(a_n\ne0\): the \(n\)th derivative does not vanish.
The \((n+1)\)th derivative is the derivative of this constant, which is zero, and every higher derivative is the derivative of zero, which is zero. So the \((n+1)\)th and higher derivatives vanish.
b The \((n+1)\)th derivative is zero, so by Box 14A the \(n\)th derivative is a constant: \(p^{(n)}(x)=c\), and \(c\ne0\) because the \(n\)th derivative does not vanish.
Now take the primitive \(n\) times. Each primitive raises every index by 1 and adds a new constant term:
 \(p^{(n-1)}(x)=cx+C_1\),  \(p^{(n-2)}(x)=\dfrac{c}{2}x^2+C_1x+C_2\),  \(p^{(n-3)}(x)=\dfrac{c}{2\times3}x^3+\dfrac{C_1}{2}x^2+C_2x+C_3\),  and so on, until
 \(p(x)=\dfrac{c}{n!}x^n+(\text{terms in lower powers of }x)\).
The coefficient \(\dfrac{c}{n!}\) of \(x^n\) is not zero, so \(p(x)\) has degree \(n\).
